## Death and Continuity

*Sleep, anaesthesia and death read without an invariant bearer, through Dōgen's firewood and ash and Tsongkhapa's mere I.*

Firewood becomes ash, and cannot return to being firewood; yet, Dōgen says, we should not regard the ash as after and the firewood as before. Each abides fully in its own position. Life does not become death, as winter does not become spring.

Under general anaesthesia three hours pass on the clock and none in experience. No river of time flowed by itself, carrying an unconscious passenger. On waking, the familiar self is put together again: fully functional, and nowhere to be found as a standing spectator.

Continuity works like a candle through the night. The flame at midnight is neither the flame at dusk nor an unrelated flame, and nothing travels between them. The person who falls asleep and wakes is the mere I, designated on changing body and mind, which keeps its promises and can still change.

## What the Fear Is Tracking

The dread of death, in its most common form, is not only the dread of pain or of leaving people behind, though those are real. It is the dread that a particular experience, this one, the taste of this afternoon and the light in this room, will be lost, and lost to someone who has been holding it. The fear presupposes two things: that experience is a possession, and that a possessor stands behind it who can be deprived. Remove the possessor and the second half of the fear has no subject. Remove the possession and the first half has no object.

## Why Experience Needs No Carrier

The logic of dependency does not simply deny the possessor. It shows that experience does not need one. An appearance is designated on its conditions: a body, a sense field, a memory, an attention, a world. It is valid because it works, and it is vivid because it is clear in itself. That clarity is what the Dzogchen tradition points to with the word luminosity. It is not a substance behind the display. It is the knowing clarity of the display, the way a sound is heard as sound and a cold is felt as cold.

This is why luminosity is sufficient. Every candidate for a carrier, whether a soul, a stream of consciousness, a neural core, a store of potentialities or a conserved energy, is itself an object of seeing. It appears, it is named, and it is picked out by criteria, so it is designated like everything else and would need a carrier of its own. Luminosity is not such an object. It is the clarity by which an appearance is known as it appears. Dependent designation accounts for the reliability of an appearance and its correction by conditions. Luminosity accounts for its vividness. Its passing is accounted for by the conditions changing. Nothing else is needed. A reader who tries to name what luminosity requires beyond these will find that each attempt turns the clarity into an object, and each object is again a designated appearance.

Put together, this removes the fear's logical basis. Experience can be vivid and complete without being owned, and it can end without being taken from anyone. Death is not the loss of a stored experience by a stored self. It is the ending of a set of conditions for a particular vividness.

## Seeing It Again and Again

The conviction is not reached once and kept. It is reached by looking, repeatedly, at how appearances form. Take sound. A bell is struck, and there is a ringing. The ringing arises from a striking, a bell, an ear, an attention, a memory of bells, a sense of where the sound is coming from. None of these is the sound, and the sound is not hidden in any of them. Yet the ringing is vivid, clear and complete while it lasts. Keep looking, and the vividness is seen to be the ringing's own, not borrowed from a stored essence of sound waiting in the bell.

Do the same with colour, taste, smell, a sensation of warmth in the chest, a thought arriving unbidden. Each is formed in dependence on conditions, and each is vivid in itself. The seeing is not a theory being checked. It is a noticing of a pattern that does not change when attention is applied to it: the vivid appearance is there, and the essence it seems to need is not.

The classic image is ice and fire. The cold of ice is vivid and known. It is felt in the hand and seen in the glint of the cube. But look for the cold inside the ice, as a property the ice owns from its own side, and it does not appear. The ice is cold in dependence on a body that feels it, a temperature relative to other things, a history of touching ice. The heat of a fire is likewise not in the fire taken alone. Warmth arises where a fire meets a body, a room, a need. The point is not that appearance is unreal. The cold is cold, and burnt fingers are burnt. The point is that the vividness has never been in the object as an own-nature. It forms in dependence, and it is complete as formed.

The seeing also shows where the confusion comes from. The mind has learned, through language, perception and emotion, to treat each vivid appearance as a thing with a fixed character, and the habit is so reliable that the designation disappears into what is designated. The inherent framework is not argued for. It is assumed each time the appearance is met. Repeated seeing loosens the assumption. Each time the formation is watched, the framework is seen to be added, not found. What was taken as a fact about the world is recognised as a way of relating to it, one that works without needing to be true from its own side.

## Continuity and the Habit

The same habit makes continuity feel like something carried. The cold seems to belong to the ice, and the self seems to belong to the stream of its moments. In both cases the mind assumes a holder. When the ice melts, the cold does not seem lost, because it was never thought to be in the ice. But when a self or a life seems to end, the mind feels that something held has been taken away, since it was treated as held. The habit makes continuity look like a possession, and possessions can be lost.

Seeing the cold never in the ice is the same seeing as seeing continuity never in a carrier. The melody shows how this works. Each playing is new, and no tune is stored between them, yet the tune is recognised across all of them and can be played well or badly, remembered or forgotten. The flame at midnight, as already said, is neither the flame at dusk nor an unrelated flame. Continuity is designated across moments, and it is real and reliable as designated. Habits, memories, responsibilities and the effects of actions all continue in this way. Nothing is held that could be taken, and what is designated carries on as designated.

## What Changes in the Dying

This matters most at the end of life, because the fear of death is at root a fear that a vividness which seemed inherent will be taken away. The vivid taste of this moment, the sound in the next room and the sense of being someone have been experienced as belonging to a holder, as if the vividness were a property of a self that could be lost along with the self.

The view does not remove the fear of pain, which is a fear about conditions and can be met by care. It does not make the loss of someone we love painless. It does not promise that anything of the person continues as the person, and a book that promised that would be claiming a carrier after all. What it removes is one particular dread: the image of an experience that is held, a holder who is taken away, and a holding left unfinished.

In its place it offers a different relation to the present. Because nothing is being held, nothing is deferred. The value of this moment does not depend on how many moments come after it, since no moment was ever a container for the rest. Attention need not hurry toward a future it cannot have, or cling backward to a past it cannot keep. The luminosity of this appearance, its clarity, its colour and the sound in the next room, is available now, including to the one who is dying. That availability was never conditional on a long life.

## The Candle, and the Weight of Watching

The candle gives exactly as much light as before. Nothing is taken from it by going out, and the room is not darker for the flame's giving. What changes is the weight with which it is watched.

For the person who thought they had a self to lose, the burning down was a countdown to catastrophe. Once the self is seen as designated, the same burning is watched differently. It is not a loss suffered by a holder. It is the fact of appearance, undivided, lit and passing. The light was never something the candle owned, so its dimming does not subtract from anyone.

The view asks nothing of the dying about what comes after. It asks them to see that the thing they were holding was never a thing, and that what remains, the vividness of this moment, has never needed a bearer to be complete.
