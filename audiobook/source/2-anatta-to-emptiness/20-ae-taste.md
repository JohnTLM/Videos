# The Taste, Not the Argument

*The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness* · Part 2

The inadequacy of a purely analytical deduction becomes apparent when contrasted with the phenomenological reality of realization. Logic may correctly arrive at the formal negation of a persistent bearer of experience — demonstrating that without a solid core, ultimate destruction is a conceptual impossibility — yet such an argument remains dry. It leaves one standing outside the threshold, performing an inference rather than tasting the reality directly. When corelessness is actually experienced, the absence of essence is not felt as a loss of security or a descent into bleakness. It is experienced, unexpectedly, as an immense openness.

The absence of an intrinsic nature does not imply the absence of vividness, movement, continuity, or change. Rather, when one looks directly for the hard kernel supposedly moving through these phenomena, nothing is found. The critical experiential reversal lies here: what disappears is not vulnerability in the ordinary, conventional sense — the body remains vulnerable, conditions can be painful and catastrophic — but the metaphysical picture of an intrinsically existing core undergoing absolute annihilation. That specific picture loses its grip, not because an indestructible, eternal substance has been uncovered — which would merely re-establish eternalism under a different guise — but because, when there was never a solid centre to begin with, the imagination of that centre being utterly annihilated has nothing left to attach to.

At this depth, dependent arising ceases to be a cold, mechanical doctrine of causality and becomes a source of quiet astonishment. Experience demonstrates a natural confidence in conditionality itself. Sleep offers a useful phenomenological analogy here, though not a proof — it illustrates how continuity need not be consciously supervised by a persisting witness, without itself establishing that continuity is carrierless; the larger analysis already does that work. Each night, the apparently continuous world disappears from waking consciousness, and a new morning simply appears. No enduring self stands guard through the dark to carry identity mechanically across the interval; the next configuration arises according to conditions. Continuity has never required a substantial entity to continue; continuity, too, is designated within a paradigm, and empty under analysis.

Because nothing is sealed into a fixed intrinsic nature, potentiality is not metaphysically closed off. This does not mean anything whatsoever can happen irrespective of conditions — dependent arising means possibilities remain rigorously conditioned throughout. What it means is that nothing is closed off, precisely because nothing possesses a fixed essence holding it shut. Openness is not an attribute belonging to a hidden subject. It is how a world devoid of fixed essence is met when nothing in it is being fixed. Free from an immutable destiny, phenomena remain dynamic: memory appears, recognition appears, thought appears, and unscripted possibilities unfold according to conditions.

This corelessness is inseparable from a gapless, direct intimacy with experience. When the division between an internal observer and an external world loosens, radiance no longer requires a subject looking across a distance at an object. Colour blazes, sound registers, and thought forms without an intervening mediator. This immediacy feels like a final arrival, not because one has reached an underlying metaphysical ground, but because there was never any distance between appearing and appearance itself. Intimacy does not depend on an ultimate substance such as Awareness, Being, or Presence. It arises from the opposite discovery: nothing stands between appearance and its immediate manifestation. Radiance is immediate, and it is completely empty; neither is the reason for the other.

When death is examined through this lens rather than through formal analysis, the question shifts. Instead of asserting a proposition such as 'there is no self to die', one looks directly at what is presumed to be vulnerable to destruction. The physical sensations of the body, the fleeting movement of thoughts, the present reconstruction of memory, the recurring patterns of personality, and the immediate sense of presence — each, upon inspection, reveals itself to be a vivid, changing, and coreless formation. No independent watcher can be located behind what appears.

The image of death as a solid core falling from existence into absolute non-existence fails simply because the required core cannot be located. Nothing needs to be denied; the body undergoes its conventional end, and conditions transform radically. Yet the terror of annihilation dissolves in the immediate inability to find a vulnerable metaphysical kernel. Experience has never stood on a ground. Nothing has ever required an essence to navigate from one moment to the next. The present moment requires no enduring carrier, nor will the next. The conclusion, then, is neither that death does not happen nor that something survives it. It is narrower and stranger than either: the thing fear imagined could be absolutely destroyed has never been found, while vivid conditional unfolding never depended upon such a thing in the first place. In the absence of a fixed essence, the openness of what may appear remains immeasurable.

The actualization of this insight is rarely a single, static epiphany. Rather, it unfolds through a repeated mutual verification between analytical view and direct phenomenological taste, continuing until the distinction between the two becomes entirely artificial.

Initially, the view corrects experience. A powerful taste of openness or radiance can easily be misidentified through old habits of thought — interpreted as an ultimate Awareness, an underlying Presence, or an enduring substrate. Careful analysis exposes and dismantles these residual substantiations. Conversely, the direct taste corrects the view. Emptiness understood purely as a conceptual argument can grow dry, negative, or detached from lived reality. The direct taste reveals that groundlessness is not a blank void; appearances remain immediate, intimate, and alive.

This produces a continuous dynamic of refinement, moving from initial view to direct taste, leading to a more refined view, a deeper taste, and ultimately an increasing seamlessness. Each side continually authenticates and purifies the other. Over time, realization shifts from a momentary recollection that 'things are empty' to an immediate way of being. It ceases to be an expansive state entered periodically only to vanish upon returning to daily existence.

The conditioning itself begins to transform at the deepest level. The habits of dualistic perception and belief in inherent existence are not merely intellectual positions consciously adopted; they have been rehearsed through countless perceptual cycles until they feel somatic, reflexive, and preconceptual. A sudden threat does not first generate a philosophical proposition regarding a vulnerable self before producing a physical response; the visceral contraction occurs instantly, as though an independently bounded entity were self-evidently real.

To be complete, realization must penetrate to that same depth. Insight becomes so thoroughly embodied that the reflex to organize experience around an assumed solid centre no longer carries the same unquestioned ontological conviction — not that reflexive self-protective organization vanishes outright, but that it no longer secretly confirms a solid, enduring subject standing behind it. At this stage, corelessness is no longer an idea to consider; it is the immediate texture of feeling. Gaplessness ceases to be a metaphysical deduction and becomes the natural manner in which seeing, hearing, and touching occur. Groundlessness is no longer experienced as precarious, but as ordinary.

The absence of an intrinsic essence no longer translates emotionally into danger or loss. Instead, it reveals the freedom of conditionality: nothing is locked within a fixed nature, and nothing possesses possibilities as a property. Possibilities simply articulate themselves continuously according to conditions.

This dynamic illustrates why seamlessness — rather than an altered state — marks the culmination of insight. If a special experience called 'realization' remains set apart from ordinary life, a division persists between the realized and the unrealized, radiance and mundane appearance, emptiness and daily function. Complete actualization implies that these oppositions cease to function as ontological divides. Even emotional contraction is seen as empty; even fear is recognized as radiant appearance; even conceptual thought is known as dependent display. This does not make distraction or fear irrelevant, but it ensures they no longer serve to confirm the existence of a solid, enduring subject.

This movement reshapes the understanding of mortality. Initially, the analytical mind grasps that there is no intrinsic self to be annihilated. Later, direct experience confirms the inability to locate a hard core anywhere within perception. Over time, the repeated rhythm of living, sleeping, waking, changing, and aging continually authenticates the same reality: continuity has never required an underlying carrier, and continuity itself is a designation, not a stream.

Eventually, even that formulation falls away. There is no longer an internal observer reassuring itself that continuity functions without a carrier; life simply proceeds. The old habit of reification grew convincing through endless repetition until it felt like reality itself. Its dissolution becomes complete when empty dependent arising is no longer merely a principle held in mind, but the seamless condition through which existence is spontaneously lived.
