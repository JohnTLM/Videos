# Death and the Bardo

When I die, what ends, what goes on, and what was never there?

Rebirth has its own page. This one is about dying itself: what the fear of death needs, what changes when a life ends, and how to read the tradition's bardo and clear light without a self slipping back in.

## The question

Anyone who has had a general anaesthetic knows a strange fact. You count backwards from ten, reach seven, and then a nurse is saying your name in another room. Three hours have passed on the clock. In experience, none have. No river of time flowed by itself, carrying an unconscious passenger.

The fear of death has a precise shape. It needs a self that exists now, whole and in its own right. It needs an event that will happen to that self. And it needs a thread joining the one who is afraid today to the one who will die. Take away any one of the three and the fear has nothing to run on.

## The substance reading

The substance view treats awareness as one lasting thing that is either on or off. Death is then the final off-switch, and every night's sleep a small rehearsal of it. Or the view swings the other way: the body dies, but something subtle survives, a radiance, a presence, a pure awareness that was the real me all along.

## What the tradition says

> Firewood becomes ash and cannot go back to being firewood. Yet we should not see the ash as after and the firewood as before. Life does not become death, as winter does not become spring.
>
> Dōgen, firewood and ash · paraphrase

> The continuum of becoming, the stream said to link one life with the next, is found in none of the three times.
>
> Nāgārjuna, MMK 21.21 · paraphrase

> Since this continuum of the aggregates goes on like the flame of a lamp, neither endlessness nor having an end makes sense.
>
> Nāgārjuna, MMK 27.22

| 2 · What does dependency say instead? | The person was always the mere I, designated on changing body and mind. It was never a bearer standing behind a life, not even now. ## The bardo and the clear light

Tibetan contemplative traditions describe dying in detail: a dissolution of the elements and of gross consciousness, the dawning of a clear light, and an intermediate state, the bardo, between one life and the next. I follow the tradition here as it teaches, held conventionally. The logic adds only how to read it.

The bardo is one more designated phase of a continuity, like dreaming. In a dream a whole world appears and works, with a dreamer inside it who feels entirely real. On waking it is found nowhere. Nothing crosses the bardo as a traveller, and no link is found at the joints (MMK 21.18–21).

The clear light is not a self, and not a substance that survives. Luminosity, on these pages, is the clarity of appearing itself, never a light behind things, and it is empty too. If the clear light becomes "what I really am, which outlasts death", the whole structure the fear needs is back, with a subtler bearer in the vacant seat. That is eternalism by the most refined route. Where the tradition speaks of recognising the clear light, I read it as recognition always is here: seeing that appearing was never fixed, not a hidden awareness getting free at last.

## Two traps

Too much: eternalism

"Something in me survives: a deeper radiance, presence or awareness." This keeps the self and only moves it somewhere harder to find. It is the I AM view again, by a subtler road.

Too little: nihilism

"Death is the off-switch, so nothing goes on and nothing matters." Annihilation needs a self that exists by itself, ready to be annihilated. None was ever found. Actions still bear fruit.

## In practice

- Notice the next time you wake: light through the curtain, a sound in the street, and then, in a rush, this room, my name, today. The self is put together again each morning, quickly and reliably.
- Sleep and anaesthesia are quieter configurations, not small rehearsals of annihilation.
- Love, sorrow and care stay exactly where they were. The empty chair is still empty.
- The worth of this moment does not depend on how many come after it. Its vividness is here now, for the dying too.

## Words to know

Bardo (*bar do*)  
"In between": in the Tibetan teachings, the state between death and the next life. A designated phase, not a place crossed by a traveller.

Clear light (*'od gsal*)  
The luminosity the tradition says dawns at death. The clarity of appearing, empty, never a self that survives.

Mere I (*nga tsam*)  
Tsongkhapa's term: the person designated on body and mind. It acts, keeps promises and dies.

Continuum of becoming (*bhavasaṃtati*)  
The stream said to link lives. Not denied as a convention, not found as a thing that flows.

Annihilationism  
The view that death wipes out a self that really existed. It needs the very self it denies.
