# Spontaneous Presence

*The Observer and the Taste of Presence* · Chapter 18 · Part IV

The Dzogchen term for this appearing is *lhun grub*, spontaneous presence. The word can slide into causelessness, and an uncaused source is exactly the *svabhāva* that Madhyamaka rejects. That is the risk, and the book should name it openly.

The way to keep the two aligned is to separate two questions. The first asks how an appearance comes about. Every appearance has an answer: it arises from causes and conditions, and the analysis of the Middle Way works at that level. The second asks about the way what appears is unfixed. Emptiness is not an intrinsic nature that appearances possess. It is the absence of any fixing, and it is not confined to mind. Since there is no phenomenon that has not arisen in dependence, there is no phenomenon that is not empty (MMK 24.19).

Spontaneous presence, then, does not name an additional ingredient sitting on top of emptiness. It names the same appearing once every demand for a producer, substrate or ultimate why has been exhausted. Any attempt to divide appearing, including analysis, belongs to dependent arising. When the reifying habit stops, the same appearing is spontaneous presence. Neither is a higher description, and neither grounds the other. What presents is already complete, and it does not wait for construction to become what it is. Both deny production by an essence, and both leave appearance intact.

The phrase "no why or because" is worth returning to. It does not deny causal regularity. Causes and effects hold within conventional truth, and the question "why?" is perfectly usable in ordinary life. What it denies is that the regularity has a ground beneath it, a reason why it is so that is itself uncaused. The question stops applying there, not because the answer is hidden, but because there was never a ground to ask about.

Two safeguards keep this from becoming a new ground. Emptiness is not a separate reality standing behind phenomena. And emptiness must not become a hidden substrate of emptiness either, because emptiness is itself empty.

Held together, the two claims point to a reading that is easy to miss. Appearance and its conditions do not stand in a hierarchy. The conditions are not a ground that the appearance is built on, and the appearance is not a surface that expresses a deeper condition. Neither is above the other, because neither has a place from which it could rule. What follows is the author's reading, not a classical claim. The arrangement is flat: the chariot's parts and the chariot, a flame and its fuel, a seeing and its object each occur within the same web, without one presiding over the rest.

Yet this flatness has no owner. The question "whose appearance is this?" has a referent only if an appearer stands behind the appearing. The first claim removes the appearer, and the second shows that the appearing does not stop for want of one. So the appearing is not a subject showing its contents. It is self-appearing only in a thin sense. The alignment of conditions is the appearing, shown without a layer or an intermediary, as a flame's light is the burning and not a second thing given off by it.

This is the book's insubstantial reading of self-appearing. "Self" here names only that the appearance is not borrowed from another place. It does not name an entity that owns it. The alignment appears without any self that it belongs to. Two cautions keep the reading honest. The phrase "conditions presenting their own appearances" must not be heard as conditions that are agents, each with an inner viewpoint. A condition is a designated relatum, and its appearing is not a second event added to its being a condition. And "own" is the easiest place for a self to return, so the reading holds only if the appearing is understood as belonging to no one, not as a holder's possession. The MMK's examination of self in chapter 18 tests whether a self can be found apart from the aggregates, and finds none. The reading takes that result as given and asks only what appearing is once the search has ended.
