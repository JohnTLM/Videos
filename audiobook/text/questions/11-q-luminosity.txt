The Questions. Luminosity.

Clear and empty, never a hidden substance.

Sit by a window and listen to the rain. The sound is clear, and nothing has to light it up. This page asks what that clarity is, and turns the Logic on the last place a ground likes to hide.

# The question.

The traditions call the mind luminous. An early discourse says "luminous, monks, is this mind" (Aṅguttara Nikāya 1.51–52), and Zog-chen speaks of the clear, knowing nature of mind. The Logic finds nothing that exists by itself, and Step 2 gave the mind no exemption. So is something left over when the search ends: a light that is the one exception?

This question is personal for me. For years my answer was yes.

# The substance reading.

Stated fairly, it says: appearances come and go, but something must know them. That knowing is steady, bright and untouched, like a lamp behind a window or a mirror behind its reflections. Sounds, colours and thoughts pass through it. It does not pass.

The substance picture puts the clarity behind what appears. The dependency picture finds it in the appearing itself, and empty like everything else.

This was my view through phases 1 to 4 (see Step 1). The I AM was real and unmistakable, and I would not deny it now. What I did with it was the mistake: I made it a Witness, then a Source, then a Mirror, each standing on its own side. The taste is real. Making it a Self is the error.

# What the tradition says.

In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen. In reference to the heard, only the heard.

The Buddha to Bāhiya, Udāna 1.10. tr. Tah-nis-sah-roh Bhikkhu.

The basis is called a ground only from a relative point of view. In the true nature of things it is empty of being a ground.

Longchenpa, on the basis (gzhi). paraphrase.

A thought recognised as unfixed releases itself as it arises, like a drawing on water, which needs no eraser.

The Zog-chen teaching of self-release (rang grol). paraphrase.

One thing should be said plainly. Reading Zog-chen together with Nah-gar-joo-nah's Middle Way, as I do here, is my own reading. It is a fair reading of Longchenpa and Mipham, not the only one; some lineages treat Zog-chen as its own vehicle.

# Applying the logic.

1. What would a substance view say? Luminosity is a radiant awareness behind appearances, existing from its own side: the screen everything plays on, the one thing that never comes or goes. 2. What does dependency say instead? Clarity is the appearing itself. A sound is heard as sound. Its clarity is not found apart from the sound, the ear and the hearing, so it arises dependently and is empty like everything else (MMK 24.19). Clarity and emptiness go together, and neither owns the other. 3. What is affirmed conventionally? Vividness, clear knowing and the taste of presence, all genuine. And the Zog-chen words, read without a ground: unobstructed, primordially pure, spontaneously present. 4. What is never found? A light behind the display, a witness looking on, a background awareness, or a store that appearances come out of.

Put the clarity through the test of Step 1. Look for it apart from what is clear. It would have to be one with what it lights up, or other than it, and it is found as neither. And when the witness is not found, experience does not go blank. The tree is more vivid, not less. What goes is the distance, not the shining.

That is all "vivid" means here: nothing in between. It is not extra intensity, and it is not a raw layer found under thought.

The Zog-chen words keep their sense on these terms. Unobstructed means nothing blocks the showing, not that something lasts for ever. Primordially pure means there was never a time when things existed by themselves; nothing was ever there to clean. That does not mean a confused life is already free. Spontaneous presence means appearing is already complete and owes that to nothing hidden. It never means uncaused. It is dependent arising described from the side of experience rather than analysis, and neither description is higher. Rigpa is knowing the nature of things: recognising appearing as unfixed, dependent and empty. It is not a super-awareness that is their nature.

# Two traps.

Too much: eternalism.

"Awareness is the one real thing." The self dissolves, and Presence quietly becomes the new Self. It is hard to see because it feels so complete. I held it for years. The question that turned it was simple: does Presence itself inherently exist?

Too little: nihilism.

"No witness, so nothing is clear." Emptiness taken as a blank, or thoughts pushed down until a stillness forms, which then becomes one more thing to hold. The clarity was never denied. Only its standing on its own.

# In practice.

Thoughts are display, not dust on a mirror. There is no need to fight them. Seen as unfixed, they release themselves.

When a bright, still presence appears, enjoy it, and then ask of it what you asked of the self: does it exist by itself?

Listen to one sound. You will find the sound and the hearing. Look for a listener standing apart from both.

# Words to know.

Luminosity ('od gsal): The knowing clarity of appearing itself. Not a light behind things, and empty too.

Unobstructed (ma 'gags pa): Nothing blocks the showing. Not "lasting", and not a store behind the display.

Display (rtsal): Thoughts and appearances, with nothing standing behind them.

Self-release (rang grol): A thought seen as unfixed frees itself as it comes. No eraser, and no one doing the releasing.

Spontaneous presence (lhun grub): Appearing already complete, owing nothing to a hidden source. Dependent arising met from the side of experience.

Primordial purity (ka dag): From the beginning, never fixed. No cleaning ever happened, because nothing was there to clean.

Rigpa: Knowing the nature of things: appearing recognised as unfixed, dependent and empty. Not a witness and not a ground.
