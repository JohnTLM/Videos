The Canon View.

The Canon View.

Where these notes stand: the positions all four books and the bonus articles follow, what is affirmed and what is never found.

Governs all four books and the bonus articles, together with the governing view of “Nature”. Start with the Logic for the reasoning behind it.

Stance. The view is a rangtong reading of Nah-gar-joo-nah's Moo-lah-mahd-yah-mah-kah-kah-ree-kah (MMK): everything, mind included, is empty of its own nature, and emptiness is a non-implicative negation. On luminous clarity the view follows Nyingma Zog-chen, which takes the MMK as its view. Luminosity is therefore read through emptiness, never as a ground that is "empty of other".

Sources are abbreviated as follows.

SW: The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World.

AE: The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness.

MW: Nah-gar-joo-nah's Middle Way.

OP: The Observer and the Taste of Presence.

Art.: bonus article.

# Part I. The view: phenomena, mind included, emptiness and neer-vah-nah.

Phenomena (dharma) are whatever can be singled out, mental or material; mind is one of them (point 14). Where the canon keeps the pairing "mind and phenomena", it quotes a source (the nature of mind, sems nyid, beside the nature of phenomena, chos nyid) and marks mind as included, never as set apart.

1. One emptiness for all phenomena, mind included. Emptiness is "the nature of mind and phenomena alike" (Two Modes, section 10). There is no phenomenon, mental or material, that has not arisen dependently, and so none that is not empty (MMK 24.19). The nature of mind is not a second or special emptiness. SW section 2.5; OP Ch 18 ("it is not confined to mind"); MW 24.19 ("every cup, thought and breath"); Art. Two Modes, One Seeing section 10.

2. Empty of own nature, nothing more. Emptiness is the absence of intrinsic nature (svah-bhah-vah). It is the absence of any fixing, not a hidden essence, a spacious ground or a pure awareness. Only own-nature is negated: "not found" does not mean "does not happen". SW Interlude ("a new substance with a quieter name"); MW 24.11, 24.18; OP Ch 14, glossary "Emptiness".

3. Emptiness is itself empty. It cannot become a new ground, a substrate of emptiness or a "luminous reality hidden behind" appearances. Negation installs nothing positive. Emptiness is the emptiness of things, designated in dependence like them; a medicine that leaves with the illness. Held as a truth, a substrate or an ineffable remainder, it becomes the one illness no further teaching can cure.

Explanation.

Always the emptiness of something. It is "the absence of own-nature (svah-bhah-vah) in things that appear and function — always the emptiness of something, never a something on its own". "Emptiness does not lie behind the cup. It is the cup's not being a cup from its own side."

A dependent designation. "It is itself empty, a dependent designation (MMK 24.18)"; "not a substance, not a void and not a view; it, too, is empty".

A medicine, not a position. "Those for whom emptiness is a view… are incurable" (13.8). Chun-drah-keer-tee's purgative "clears the blockage and then leaves the body too. If the medicine stays in the body, it becomes a new illness, and there is no further medicine to remove it." "The test of having understood it is not having a new view, but being freed from the need to hold one."

The most refined form of the habit. "The view of emptiness is the most refined form of the habit the whole text is dismantling", hence the warnings against "a hidden openness, potential or awareness behind appearances, to which emptiness is quietly promoted". "Openness, too, is empty. It is not something one has. It is the absence of what one was holding."

A word that does a job. "'Empty' is not a property the Tah-tah-gah-tah has, in the way a stone is hard. It is a word used to undo a grasp"; taken as the final description it "would turn emptiness into a new nature" (22.11; point 19).

Correctives are dropped too. Not-self "is not a thing that replaces the self… Like medicine, they do their work and are not kept once the illness has gone" (23.21–22; point 18).

Empty, and it still works. To the objection that an empty statement of emptiness refutes nothing: "one magically created man can stop another" (Vigrahavyāvartanī 23). "Empty things act on empty things, and that is the only acting there is."

No level left standing. Emptiness singled out is designated and empty (point 14); "beyond identification" means nothing further to identify (point 15). The emptiness of emptiness is not a deeper emptiness but the absence of any level that stays.

The same rule for dependent arising. It "has been used only to demonstrate emptiness, never as a new ground; and emptiness has been offered only as the absence of what was never there, not as something to believe in". Dependent arising itself "is released from ontological privilege". The books hold themselves to it: "The book has tried to leave its reader with no view in need of a cure."

4. Mind is tested like everything else and is not exempt. A substance, a relation, a moment, a mind, a self: each meets the same search, and none is found standing on its own. Mind is not the ground of dependence: "Does the red depend on the mind, and is the mind then the ground? That question leads back to the witness." SW Intro, section 5.3; OP Ch 2.

5. Knower and known are designated together. Consciousness is not a standing light, a container, a homunculus or a witness behind experience. It is always "of something, through something". "Knowing, too, is part of what depends." The knower is not found beyond the knowing. SW section 7.3–7.5; MW 5.6; AE Flower; Art. Dependent Designation section 4.

6. Neither idealism nor physicalism. To deny objects while keeping mind makes mind the last substance. "The dreamed mountain and the dreaming mind appear together and are equally unfound on waking." Mind-Only, idealism, panpsychism and consciousness-as-fundamental are rejected for the same reason as matter-as-substance. SW section 12.10 (Chun-drah-keer-tee); OP Ch 6, Ch 19; AE Science.

7. Two truths, one appearance. The two truths are two aspects of one appearance, not two levels. What is vivid and functional conventionally is unfindable ultimately. Conventional functioning is full validity, not a veneer. Ultimate truth is "not a higher world, but the emptiness of the conventional one".

Explanation.

Non-dimensional. There is no deeper level beneath appearances (no backstage, substrate or ground) and no higher level above them. The two truths are "non-dimensional".

Conventional truth has standards. Tsong-kah-pah's three criteria sort sound conventions from mistaken ones: known to ordinary cognition, not overturned by further ordinary cognition, and not contradicted by analysis of the ultimate. The snake in the rope fails the second; the inherently existing rope fails the third; the rope as designated passes all three.

Each needs the other. Without relying on the conventional, the ultimate cannot be taught (MMK 24.10). Taken apart, each becomes a fixed object: the conventional turns into solid things really causing one another, and the ultimate into an empty ground behind them. Joined, they dissolve each other's solidity.

Speaking positively without a ground. Mipham's second model, concordant and discordant experience, lets vividness be affirmed without being made a ground: "'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous reality hidden behind it".

Wording. "Ultimately" used as an adverb ("unfindable ultimately") is conventional usage and names no level. The ultimate, once singled out, is itself designated (points 14–15).

8. Emptiness is not a producer or a ground. Emptiness is what dependent arising amounts to when examined. It is not a producer, an establisher, a power or a hidden source behind appearances. Nah-gar-joo-nah's own wording is allowed: "because things are empty, they can arise, change and work" (MMK 24.14, 24.20), read as his reply to his critics: something with its own nature could not arise, change or act. Still avoid wording that makes emptiness do the work, such as "established by emptiness" or "emptiness produces appearances". (Relaxed by the owner, 2026-10-25; until then the rule also ruled out "because they are empty, they can arise".) Canon 3, 5; Art. Across the articles, point 17.

9. Designation is the whole status, not a label on top. A thing and its designation arise together; no thing comes with its own edges and oneness, ready-made, waiting to be named. "There is no undesignated layer on which labels are placed": something is always seen as something. Dependent designation is how things are conventionally established: picked out, by perception, concept or language, together with their bases, parts and contexts, with no undesignated layer beneath and no pre-carved world before. Calling a thing designated does not demote it. Convention is constrained by what works; it is the only validity, and it is full validity.

Explanation.

The middle way itself. "Dependent arising is what we call emptiness. That is a designation in dependence, and that itself is the middle way" (24.18). "Designation is the way such things are named and known, and the naming is itself dependent." It "is not a compromise between existence and non-existence. It is what the middle path looks like when it is understood."

Wider than language. Designation is "the picking out of something as this rather than that, by perception, concept or language". "Dependent designation is therefore not fundamentally a theory of naming; language is merely its expression." Innate grasping shows designation at work before words.

Neither labels on a finished world nor creation from nothing. "Dependent designation does not create conventional reality out of nothing. Nor does it merely attach labels to an already completed world." The central theorem: it "is not the conceptual labelling of a pre-existing world, but the simultaneous articulation of dependent arising itself".

The mountain before anyone names it. "Conventionally, the mountain is there before anyone names it… what is denied is a mountain whose boundary and oneness come with it independently of designation." Naming does not make the mountain; no mountain carries its edges and oneness from its own side.

The basis is designated too. "The basis of designation… the parts of the chariot, the aggregates of the person… is designated too." Tsong-kah-pah: "even the basis on which something is designated (gdags gzhi) is itself merely designated, so that designation never bottoms out in something given." There is no real layer of sensation underneath and a merely conceptual layer of naming on top (point 23).

Parts and whole together. "Dependent designation is not parts producing a whole. It is parts-and-whole arising together, mutually, with neither side doing the producing." Sought apart from, identical with, owning or containing its parts, the chariot leaves "nothing… as a remainder; the chariot simply functions" (point 21).

Not arbitrary nominalism. Mere naming would be "an unconstrained label"; designation is "constrained at every step by what holds up under further experience. We cannot designate a shadow as a bridge and walk across it."

Tsong-kah-pah's three criteria. Known to a conventional consciousness; not contradicted by another valid conventional cognition; not contradicted by reasoning that analyses the ultimate. "The snake in the rope fails the second test. The inherently existing rope fails the third. The rope as designated passes all three." "A sound designation is one that works" (point 12).

Designated is not demoted. "Convention is not the opposite of truth. It is where truth, in every usable sense, lives."

The mere I. The person is "merely designated in dependence on the aggregates", and "it is merely designated that it can bear responsibility and also change" (point 18).

Designation is designated too. Emptiness is "itself… a dependent designation" (point 3), and dependent functioning "is itself conventionally designated, not another hidden ontological layer".

10. Relations are as empty as the things related. Relation, structure, field or network does not become the new foundation once things are seen to depend. "Relation and relata are designated together… neither is prior, and neither is a ground." Relational accounts in physics are reliable designations within their domains, not the last word on what appears. SW Intro ("Two Opponents"), Ch 4; Art. Insubstantial Appearances section 1.

11. Change without a changer, continuity without a carrier. Change and changer are designated together, and no process, flow, energy or stream replaces the old subject. Continuity is designated across moments that are neither the same nor wholly different: the flame at midnight is "neither the flame at dusk nor an unrelated flame". The designation is the continuity, not a cover over a hidden flow. MW 18.10; SW Ch 9; Art. The Logic of Dependency; Art. Death and Continuity; OP Ch 9, 11.

12. Function without essence. Nothing acts from its own side, and nothing needs to. A key opens a lock only with the lock, the hand and the turning: "the 'power to open' is not found apart from them". A thing with its nature from its own side could not respond, combine or change. SW Ch 14; MW 24.14, 24.16–20; Art. Insubstantial Appearances section 3.

13. Empty is not flimsy. Empty appearances are vivid and workable, "as vivid and solid in their working as anything we know". Their effects are fully real: a car with no essence still injures, and grief and healing still happen. Two mirrored errors are refused: that the solid must be solid from its own side, and that the empty must be faint. Art. Insubstantial Appearances section 2; SW section 13.3; AE The Taste, Not the Argument.

14. Nothing singled out is exempt. Whatever can be identified, characterized or singled out in any way, by perception, by concept or as "the ultimate", is dependently designated, so it exists only conventionally and is empty: luminosity, emptiness and neer-vah-nah included. These verbs all name one act, picking out as this rather than that. Nothing unidentified is left over beyond it, which would be implicative negation returning. MMK 5 (characteristic and characterized), 13.8, 22.11, 24.18–19, 25.19–20; SW Intro ("A Working Vocabulary"). The sources outside the author's books cited for this point in conversation (Tsong-kah-pah on emptiness existing conventionally; Mipham's categorized and uncategorized ultimate) are unverified and are not cited here.

15. "Beyond identification" means nothing further to identify, never an ineffable something. When the ultimate is called free of the four extremes, inexpressible or beyond concepts, this names designation and grasping coming to rest, with nothing found and nothing installed. It does not name a reality that exists but escapes designation. The phrase is itself a pointer, a designation, and what it points to is not an item standing outside emptiness. Read the other way, as an ineffable reality that concepts merely fail to reach, it would be an ultimate exempt from emptiness: implicative negation, and shentong under another name.

Explanation.

The two kinds of ultimate. Some Tibetan commentators distinguish a categorized ultimate from an uncategorized one. The categorized ultimate is emptiness as a non-implicative negation, an absence known by reasoning; it is still held as a negation. The uncategorized ultimate is free of all four extremes, the negation included: the negation released, as MMK 13.8 asks. The canon accepts the distinction in this sense only. The categorized ultimate is what point 14 already covers (identified, so designated and empty), and the uncategorized ultimate is not a second object but the end of holding the first.

How it relates to the rest of the canon. The canon's theory points make the non-implicative negation the view and guard it with "emptiness is itself empty" (points 2–3). The step of releasing the negation is carried by the practice points: recognition is not achievement, and non-dividing seeing is appearing when no fixing occurs (nature points 4, 8; luminous clarity point 8). This point names that step in the theory as well.

The test. Any wording that leaves something real behind once all identification has stopped fails this point and point 14. "Nothing unidentified is left over" is the safeguard.

Sources to verify before citing in a book. Gorampa (1429–1489) and Mipham (1846–1912) are read in the secondary literature as holding the categorized/uncategorized distinction. Gorampa is read as dividing the two truths by the apprehending mind, treating the conceivable emptiness as only nominal, and criticising Tsong-kah-pah for making the mere negation final; Gelug critics answer that his "inexpressible" ultimate risks becoming an absolute. Mipham is read as agreeing that the mere negation is not final while keeping conventional valid cognition firmly in place. None of this is yet checked against primary texts. Works to check: Cabezón and Dargyay, Freedom from Extremes (2007, on Gorampa); Pettit, Mipham's Beacon of Certainty (1999); Duckworth (2008) and Karma Phuntsho (2005) on Mipham. Do not attribute either position in the books until it is confirmed (OP verification log).

16. Neer-vah-nah is this same appearing with fixing ended. It is not produced by practice and not an uncaused ground that was always there (nature point 5). It is not another place or a level above the world: there is not the slightest difference between sahm-sah-rah and neer-vah-nah (MMK 25.19–20). Liberation "is not a change in what exists. It is a change in how what exists is held." Nothing is added and nothing is removed; what ends is proliferation, which "stops in emptiness" (MMK 18.5). No one holds it: "'neer-vah-nah will be mine' is great grasping", and no refined, selfless self survives to enjoy it. As anything singled out, neer-vah-nah is designated and empty (point 14).

Explanation.

Neither existent nor non-existent. MMK 25 refuses to make neer-vah-nah a thing, an absence, both or neither. The canon reads this as the same refusal it makes everywhere: the question presupposes a bearer with a status of its own, and none is found.

Recognised, not attained. Freedom is recognised, not produced (nature point 8). Where the books keep the verse's word "attained" (MW's rendering of 24.10), it means conventionally realised along a path, never an achievement that produces freedom.

The path keeps its place. The ultimate is taught only through convention (MMK 24.10). Practice, path and fruit stay fully valid conventionally; what practice undoes is the habit of fixing, which takes clear knowing.

The taste is ordinary. "Seen is just seen, heard is just heard": nothing needs to be produced to take the place of the habit that was seen through.

17. Dependent arising: alignment without a producer, and no last "why". "Cause" has three senses, and only one is refused. Intrinsic production, a cause that exists on its own and delivers an effect, is rejected (MMK 1.1). Conditional alignment, factors that let a result appear when assembled, holds conventionally and does real work. "Cause" as a designated relatum, the element attention picks out when it sequences a process, keeps alignment from sliding back into production: the designation "is the whole status of the cause". The demand for why appearances spring forth at all is the last reification. It is not answered by a spontaneous ground; it loses its object and stops applying. "No why and because" marks that point and does not contradict dependent arising.

Explanation.

One fact, two sides. Dependent arising and emptiness are one fact, seen from the side of convention and from the side of analysis (MMK 24.18; point 8). The eight negations describe dependent arising itself: "If a sprout could be found arising from its seed, it would have an arising of its own, and it would not need to depend."

Dependent arising dismantles itself. "Dependence, too, is designated." Dependent arising is not a final truth standing on its own side, and it does not become a network or field beneath things (point 10).

"Beyond cause and effect". The phrase sets aside only the first sense of cause. It is not a place where causes do not operate, and "spontaneous" (lhun grub) never means "uncaused source" (Part II point 5).

The conventional account stands. The twelve links describe how suffering runs while ignorance is present and ends when ignorance ends (MMK 26).

18. Anatta is a genuine and thorough insight, but not the whole view. The observer is fully seen through: no seer behind seeing, no hearer behind hearing, no owner behind experience. "In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen." Anatta alone does not extend that unfindability to phenomena, conditions and dependence itself. Emptiness does. Until it does, the taste of no-self can sit inside a view that is still substantialist: desynchronization. The canon does not call anatta "complete".

Explanation.

No one has disappeared. Seeing, hearing and thinking are found, vivid and working; only a viewer standing apart from them is not. "This does not mean that nobody is seeing, and it does not mean that a person has disappeared." Experience is less mediated, not blank.

The person remains as the mere I. Tsong-kah-pah's mere I (nga tsam) is designated on the aggregates, identical with none of them and found nowhere apart from them, yet it remembers, acts and keeps promises (MMK 18.1, 27).

Not even an egoless one. Liberation "does not produce a refined self that has the quality of selflessness" (MMK 18.3). "No-self" is itself only a designation (18.6): a corrective, medicine, not a new truth to hold.

The last refuge. After anatta, "Presence quietly becomes the new observer… awareness itself becomes the new Self". The canon refuses this (Part II point 9): awareness and presence are as empty as the self they replaced.

Appearing belongs to no one. "Self-appearing" means only "not borrowed from another place", never possession: it is "what ah-nah-tah looks like when no owner is being sought".

The felt self. The Observer Chapter 3 offers, as the author's own claim for inspection, that the seer-seen split is felt in the body as contraction, and that contraction is felt as a self.

19. Elaboration stops in emptiness because what it spins around was never there. Elaboration (prapañca) is "the mind's spinning-out of 'is', 'is not', 'it came', 'it went' around something it takes to be there". When the something is not found, "the spinning stops by itself. That stopping is called peace." Emptiness is not a new object that replaces the old ones: "It is the seeing that the objects of elaboration were never there as elaborated."

Explanation.

The root of suffering. Liberation is the ending of action and afflictions; these come from conceptual construction (vikalpa: "pleasant", "mine", "I am wronged"), and that comes from elaboration, which stops in emptiness (MMK 18.5).

What emptiness is for. Its purpose is to quiet elaboration (24.7, with Chun-drah-keer-tee). The work opens and closes on "the quieting of elaborations, peace" (homage; 25.24). Neer-vah-nah is "the quieting of all taking-as-objects, the quieting of elaborations" (point 16).

Even "empty" can be elaboration. "Taken as a description of what he really is, even 'empty' becomes one more elaboration" (22.11); "those who elaborate about the Buddha… are struck down by elaboration" (22.15). Whatever is singled out and held as there is elaborated again (point 14).

Already free, not freed. Freedom from elaboration (niṣprapañca) is not "a temporary meditative suspension of conceptuality", nor the property of a non-conceptual state. It is "the very nature of dependent arising itself": "Dependent arising has never possessed the elaborations projected upon it." Analysis removes the projection and adds nothing.

Thought is not the elaboration. Freedom "is not the possession of any mode of cognition". Correct conceptual understanding "simply ceases to project elaboration onto that which has always been free" (Part II point 13).

A quiet mind is not the end of it. It is a bypass when "the cessation of conceptual proliferation is mistaken for the completion of wisdom". Stillness with the habit of fixing intact has not ended elaboration.

Peace is not dim. "When elaboration stops in emptiness (18.5), nothing goes dark, and nothing new is installed to shine." Reality is described by what is absent from it: own-nature, elaboration, plurality (18.9).

Wording to watch. AE Ye Shes calls dependent arising "primordially open, non-abiding, effortless" with an "inexhaustible capacity for the manifestation of appearances". The passage itself says these are "not positive realities inferred from emptiness"; quoted without that clause, "openness" and "capacity" read as a ground.

20. Karma and rebirth are affirmed conventionally, with nothing carried and nothing cut off. Actions bear fruit, in this life and beyond it, and one life conditions the next, "as one flame lights another". Both hold as designated, as everything holds. What is refused is the traveller, the self that would walk unchanged from life to life, "and its shadow, the self that would be wiped out at death".

Explanation.

Karma works because it is empty. Agent, action and fruit are empty, "and this is exactly why they work". A karma with its own nature "could never be made, never change and never end"; it would ripen again and again (17.21–25). "Only an empty karma can deliver it."

No bridge between deed and fruit. "The link between action and result is real and reliable, as the link between rice seed and rice is. It is designated across the moments, not carried by something that travels through them": a melody known by heart, with no tune stored between playings (point 11).

Like a magician's creation, not like nothing. A magically made person makes another, which really acts (17.31–32). A mirage "is seen, and travellers turn toward it"; a dream "can make the dreamer weep". Karma is "fully working, and nowhere found standing by itself" (17.33).

Rebirth is not a journey. "The tradition affirms rebirth, but not as a journey. What is denied is the passenger": no soul, no subtle consciousness with its own nature, and none needed as a link, and no "stream of consciousness flowing on like water through a series of pipes: a thing in all but name". The Buddha rebuked Sāti for holding that the same consciousness wanders on (MN 38).

Neither the same nor another. Nah-gah-say-nah's lamps: the flame of the last watch is neither the flame of the first nor a different one; a lamp lit from a lamp, "nothing passes over, yet the second flame is not unconnected with the first" (Milindapañha).

No link found at the joint. If the last moment has ceased, nothing connects; if not, two lives run at once; if one is born exactly as the other dies, they are two (21.18–21). The continuum "is not denied as a convention, but it is not found as a thing that flows".

Everything that matters is kept. "Kind acts tend toward happiness and cruel acts toward suffering. Practice changes a person. One is responsible for what one has done." The mere I "keeps its promises and meets the results of what it has done" (point 18). "None of this denies past lives, future lives, or the moral weight of what we do."

The maker of sahm-sah-rah is not-knowing, not a person. The twelve links keep karma, rebirth and the path; "what is left out is only the owner" (26.9–10). Karma comes from afflictions, afflictions from conceptual construction, and that from elaboration (18.5, point 19). The same chain "can be seen in a single afternoon".

Affirmed, not proven by emptiness. Emptiness removes one objection, that rebirth needs a carrier, since nothing ever has one. It does not show that rebirth happens: "Removing a bad argument against something is not the same as supplying a good argument for it." Whether a given case occurs is a matter of evidence (AE At the Edges of Science, "Ordinary Magic", Stevenson). The canon affirms rebirth as the tradition teaches it, held conventionally, not as a conclusion drawn from emptiness.

21. Non-duality is "not established as two", never "one thing". Knower and known are designated together, each identified only through the other. "Mind and world are not two things joined by a bridge, nor two sides of a single process, which would be one more substrate." Non-duality is a negation of a real division, not the assertion of a single substance, field or awareness in which the two are one. Distinctions remain vivid, and even the awareness in which the two seemed to merge is designated and empty.

Explanation.

Seamless without fusion. "Seamless" means "no seam is found… It does not mean one continuous cloth… there was never a fabric of the kind that seams divide." "Sound never becomes colour, thought never becomes warmth": "intimate without merging, distinct without walls".

Not holism. Holism "usually keeps a whole that determines its parts… and so reverses the direction of priority rather than removing it. The Mahd-yah-mah-kah removes it." The whole is designated on its parts (point 10).

Consciousness as non-dual dependent appearing. Not a container, not a hidden producer, "not found apart from its objects". "Look for the one who tastes and you find only more of the tasting", and the taste stays precise and reliable (point 5).

Non-dual experience can still be substantialist. "A substantialist non-dual experience isn't the same as a non-substantialist one." It takes the openness or awareness it discloses as "a subtle, findable ground": "an implicative negation wearing non-dual clothing". "Duality may collapse, while luminosity quietly assumes the role once occupied by substance" (Part II points 9, 14).

The genuine non-duality includes the ground. It is one "in which even what was taken as the ground-nature, the luminosity, the awareness itself, is seen as dependently designated rather than independently findable, while appearing remains vividly present" (point 14).

The last seam is the analysing itself. "As long as there is still a 'this searching, examining this' structure, one final subject-object seam remains." Prahj-nyah completes itself as ye shes when that seam stops being exempted (Part II point 10).

The same "not two" throughout. Emptiness and appearance are two aspects of one appearance, not two levels (point 7); sahm-sah-rah and neer-vah-nah are not two (point 16); clarity and emptiness are not two, "and neither is the ground of the other" (Part II).

Not a possession. "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground: a confident description of non-duality that the reader is invited to possess." The turn came when practice "no longer attempted to stabilize Presence or refine non-duality" and asked whether anything, awareness included, is inherently established.

22. Realization is recognition, not production. It is the recognition of appearing as already unfixed: decisive, but not produced by practice, not kept as a state, and not a hidden state uncovered. "Freedom is not produced, and practice does not make it. Nor is it a hidden state that a habit covers over." Recognition is "that clear seeing of appearing when the fixing is not operating". It is made stable by view, deepened layer by layer, and recognised again and again wherever the habit of fixing returns.

Explanation.

What practice does. Practice "undo[es] the habit of fixing, which takes clear knowing", and "that undoing is itself a practice". Ethics, meditation and study are "ways of arranging conditions", not attacks on fixed defects; practice works because nothing is fixed (MMK 24.16–20). There is no goer apart from the going: "the practitioner is the mere I, designated on the practising" (point 18).

Not an attainment to keep. "It is not a higher stage on a path, and it is not an attainment. It is recognised, and the moment one tries to keep it as an accomplishment, the fixing has returned." So too "effortless": "the moment one holds it as a thing to maintain, one has left it". Recognition "does not negate. It sees that appearances are already not fixed."

Seen once, recognised again and again. The chariot gives the whole shape of the argument once; after that "it does not need to be seen again for the first time. It only needs to be recognized, again and again, in every place the mind had quietly assumed something had to be standing underneath." "The conviction is not reached once and kept."

In layers. "A mind runs into its own reifications one layer at a time": self, things, process, non-dual presence itself (points 18, 21).

Experience and view can fall out of step. After anatta "the experience has become non-substantialist, but the conceptual framework remains substantialist", and the practitioner "often believes the realization is already complete… The self has dissolved, but the universe has not" (the Great Desynchronization).

View makes it stable. "An experience that cannot be understood cannot be stabilized, taught, or trusted under pressure." "View is what lets an opening become a place to live rather than a place once visited."

But concepts are not the eating. "Concepts are pictures of food; realization is eating." Doh-gen turns it over: there is no unpainted rice cake behind the painted one, so the remedy is not to discard concepts but to see that nothing stands behind them. Prahj-nyah completes itself as ye shes (Part II point 10).

An open question, held loosely. Reasoning and direct recognition converge on one nature; whether analysis alone suffices, or recognition is "usually occasioned by something outside the reasoning itself — direct introduction by a qualified teacher… a discontinuous seeing", is left open.

Nothing gained, nothing lost. Liberation "is not a change in what exists. It is a change in how what exists is held" (point 16). "The bell still rings. Medicine still heals." Reality "is recognized as having always been light".

23. Thusness is dependent arising seen as it is. Each appearance is complete in its own position, with nothing behind it. Thusness is not a hidden essence, not a raw given beneath concepts, and not the one thing left over: it is empty like everything else. (The books do not use "thusness", "suchness" or tathatā as a term; the idea appears as "the way things are" (dharmatā, chos nyid), "appearing as it is", Doh-gen's dharma position and total exertion.)

Explanation.

Dependent arising with the fixing gone. Non-dividing seeing is "appearing as it is when no fixing occurs: presented whole, without a before and an after… This is what the homage calls dependent arising, seen as it is. Nothing is lost by it."

Not a hidden suchness. The standard gloss of dharmatā as the unchanging nature (svah-bhah-vah) of dharmas, beyond all discrimination, is declined; "nature" means "the way things are, when they are not reified, in a narrower sense than that gloss" (points 2–3).

Not a bare, undesignated layer. Dhar-mah-keer-tee's Given, particulars met by perception before designation, is the strongest candidate for a raw "as it is", and it is refused: designation is "already in the seeing", as with the duck-rabbit (point 9; Part II point 8).

Whole in its own position. "Firewood abides in its own dharma position… before and after are cut off… Each appearance is fully itself in its own position, lacking nothing, with nothing carried across." Yet "the positions, too, are designated, not found", and being and time are not two (uji).

Completely spent, nothing behind. "Every appearance completely spends itself in being exactly this appearance. Nothing stands behind it, nothing governs it, and nothing remains outside it." "Hearing is precisely the sounding."

Like neer-vah-nah, not a mystical realm. "The true nature of things (dharmatā), unarisen and unceasing, is like neer-vah-nah. This is not a mystical realm beyond the world… dependent arising itself is without arising and ceasing" (MMK 18.7; point 16).

Empty too. Named and singled out, thusness is designated like everything else (points 14–15). The descriptions of total exertion are "invitations to recognize a taste rather than propositions": "No description is intended as a metaphysical claim."

Ordinary, not exalted. What is seen as it is, is the ordinary world, distinctions vivid and nothing added (points 13, 21); there is no unpainted rice cake behind the painted one (point 22).

Wording fixed (2026-11-04). AE Total Exertion used to say each dharma position is "exhaustive of the entire cosmos" and that "the entire cosmos is spent within the immediate relational configuration". Read literally, that suggests a cosmic whole present in each moment: the holism point 21 refuses. It now says each appearance is "lacking nothing: no wider whole stands behind it to complete it", and that the event spends itself completely, "with nothing held back behind it and no cosmos packed inside it".

24. Compassion works without a self behind it, and emptiness takes nothing from it. The teaching itself arose in dependence on compassion, "for those who suffer". Kindness is felt and compassion relieves suffering with no fixed self found behind either, and only an empty compassion could arise, grow and be learned. Love, sorrow and care "remain exactly where they were". How compassion relates to emptiness and realization is not yet stated in the books; see "Gap" below.

Explanation.

Why the teaching is given. The MMK closes with a bow "to Gautama, who out of compassion taught the true Dharma so that all views could be let go" (27.30). He taught "taking up" (upādāya) compassion, the word used for designation in dependence (24.18): "The teaching, too, arose in dependence: on compassion, for those who suffer."

Function without essence. "When someone we love speaks kindly, we imagine a fixed 'self' behind the words that really means them… Yet the light works, and the kindness is felt, without any such ground being found." After the analysis is complete, "compassion still relieve[s] suffering" (point 12).

Only an empty compassion can work. As with action and its fruit, what had its own nature "cannot be done or made"; "no one could ever act rightly or wrongly" (24.33–35; points 12, 20).

Nothing is taken from love or grief. When neither the self nor the nothing is found, "the fear loses its object, while love, sorrow and care remain exactly where they were". The view "does not make the loss of someone we love painless"; pain "can be met by care". Emptiness is never indifference.

Kindness as the shared ground of the path. "Restraint, kindness and help to others are the dharma, the seed of good results now and later" (17.1–5). "Ethics and practice keep their full force."

Zog-chen's compassion named, not taken up. OP's glossary gives the three aspects of the basis: empty essence (ngo bo), clear nature (rang bzhin) and unobstructed compassion (thugs rje), and glosses only clarity. AE lists "responsiveness" among the ways dependent arising functions, adding that these are "not positive realities inferred from emptiness".

Gap: not yet stated. The books do not say how compassion relates to realization: whether it arises of itself when emptiness is seen (seeing others bound by fixing as itself a source of care); whether it is a practice of its own (bodhicitta, the union of wisdom and compassion); or how thugs rje is to be read on this canon's terms. A reading of thugs rje as the responsiveness of appearing, not a cosmic force, would fit points 12 and 15 and Part II point 1, but it is not the books' and is not adopted here.

Wording to watch. AE Relentless Negation says ethics, medicine and culture flourish "precisely because dependent arising is empty". That uses emptiness as an explanation (point 8); "as dependent arising, which is empty" would keep the sense.

25. Buddha nature is not a hidden, already-perfect awareness. It is the empty, knowing clarity of appearing, recognised rather than uncovered, and empty like everything else. The shentong reading, an ultimate "empty of other" things but not of its own nature and complete with its qualities, is exactly what the stance refuses. (The books do not use "Buddha nature", tathāgatagarbha or sugatagarbha as a term; this point states what the texts say on the nearest terms and what the stance implies. The open questions are marked "Gap" below.)

Explanation.

The luminous mind, read without a ground. For "luminous, monks, is this mind" (AN 1.51–52) and "awareness as the nature of mind", the book "denies only one reading": taken as "an awareness that exists from its own side… it is one more ground". Read otherwise, luminosity says only "that appearing is vivid and unobstructed… and it is as empty as everything else" (Part II points 1–2).

The basis is a ground only relatively. Longchenpa: "a ground only from a relative perspective, empty of being a ground in the true nature of reality. This book follows that line"; the basis (gzhi) is "the emptiness of appearing, not… a ground behind it" (Part II point 4).

"Primordially pure" is not an uncaused source. "Freedom is not produced and is not a result of causes, but also… it is not an uncaused base. The correction is to the self-description of the tradition."

Nothing hidden waiting to be uncovered. Freedom is not "a hidden state that a habit covers over" (point 22); non-recognition is not "a hidden nature going unseen… Nothing hidden is waiting behind it to be found" (Part II point 12).

Mipham's second model, through Duckworth. "Authentic" experience is the same appearance met without reification, which "allows the vividness to be spoken of positively without being made into a positive ground… 'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous reality hidden behind it."

What the stance implies. Buddha nature, on this canon, is the emptiness of mind and phenomena (point 1), met as the knowing clarity of appearing (Part II point 1), recognised (point 22), and, once singled out, designated and empty (point 14).

A trap in the usual rangtong reading. "Buddhahood is possible because the mind is empty" lets emptiness enable something, which point 8 refuses. The canon keeps MMK 24's form instead: if mind had its own nature it could never change, so change and the path make sense only for what has none. That is a reductio, not emptiness as a cause.

Gap: not yet stated. The books take no position on the qualities of a buddha (innate and only uncovered, as in the Ratnagotravibhāga, or not); on what "beings are already buddhas" claims (a claim about nature, as with sahm-sah-rah and neer-vah-nah not two, point 16, or about potential); or on Mipham's Buddha nature as the unity of clarity and emptiness, which is close to Part II but not worked out against it.

26. Effortlessness is what appearing already is when the fixing stops, not a better effort or a state to keep. "'Spontaneous' (lhun grub) means already complete. It is not produced by effort, and so it is not a state one gets more of by trying harder." "When the fixing stops, nothing needs to be produced to take its place." It is recognised, not produced, and it does not excuse the clear knowing that undoes the habit.

Explanation.

A new object to keep. "The moment one treats effortlessness as an accomplishment, one has made it another fixed object." "It is recognised, and the moment one holds it as a thing to maintain, one has left it" (point 22).

Effort hardening into grasping. Under the logic of substance "awakening is a place or condition one arrives at and then keeps", and "effort tends to harden into grasping: at progress, at experiences, at the self that is supposedly being improved". Wanting neer-vah-nah "turns the path into a project of the self. The more earnestly it is held, the tighter the hold" (16.9).

Not suppression. Stillness forced by effort "can become a new object of holding, and the practitioner ends up grasping the absence of grasping". Non-dividing seeing "is not a stillness reached by effort" (Part II point 13).

Not "do nothing". Practice "undo[es] the habit of fixing, which takes clear knowing, and… that undoing is itself a practice". Ethics, meditation and study are "ways of arranging conditions"; Chun-drah-keer-tee accepts śamatha conventionally, and the decisive step is analysis. "The path is not empty words. It is the removal of a misreading." The books warn against using "emptiness or non-duality to excuse the actual work of recognition".

Effortless practice can still be substantialist. The practitioner "often believes the realization is already complete… practice has become effortless; what more could there be?" The answer: "the world itself has not yet been thoroughly deconstructed" (point 22, the Great Desynchronization).

A description, not a property. Dependent arising is "already open, non-abiding, effortless", but these "must not be misunderstood as positive realities inferred from emptiness… they are simply the manner in which dependent arising has always functioned once the false attribution of intrinsic existence has been abandoned" (point 8; Part II point 5).

A by-product, reported, not a target. As the deconstruction went on, "the mind and the body's energy began opening automatically, without effort. Less attachment. Less compulsive thought."

Some effortlessness is the habit itself. Perceptual binding is "so total and so effortless" that the mind throws the result outward as an object already there. The effortlessness that matters is the absence of fixing, not the absence of felt labour.

# Part II. Luminous clarity.

1. Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing itself. It is "not a light behind appearances, nor an awareness standing apart to illuminate them. It is the vividness of the event itself." A sound is heard as sound; the knowing and the known are not two. AE Total Exertion; OP Ch 2; SW section 7.10.

2. Luminosity is empty. Read as a radiant substance behind experience, an awareness existing from its own side, luminosity "is one more ground". Read rightly, it "is not a container but a description of appearance itself… and it is as empty as everything else". Clarity and emptiness are inseparable. Neither owns the other. SW section 7.10.

3. Unobstructed, not enduring. Ma 'gags pa means that nothing blocks the showing. It does not mean a stream that lasts, and there is no store behind the display from which it comes. SW section 3.5; OP Ch 8; Art. Logic of Dependency.

4. The basis is primordially pure (ka dag). Following Longchenpa, the basis (gzhi) is called a ground only from a relative perspective. In the true nature of things it is empty of being a ground. It is "not a subtle existent thing" and not an uncaused source. Say "the basis, primordially pure", not "emptiness is the ground". Primordial purity is: from timeless beginning, purified of inherentness. "From timeless beginning" means there was never a time when appearing had inherent existence; so "purified" names no cleaning that happened at some point, only the absence of what was never there. It is the emptiness of appearing, named from the side of the basis: nothing needs cleaning and no pristine source lies behind it. It does not mean that a confused life is already free.

Explanation.

What the term means. "The Zog-chen description of the basis as pure from the beginning, and a way of signifying emptiness." Higgins: "a term signifying primordial purity as a way of signifying emptiness", unique to Nyingma tantric literature; the Buddha-Nature site: "the empty nature of phenomena"; Tshig mdzod chen mo: "pure from the beginning, free from stain".

The canon's reading. "Read as the unfixed character of appearing, not as an uncaused source." "Primordially pure" says that appearing was never fixed, not that a pristine something stands behind it.

Pure means never having had the stains. "Dependent arising has never possessed the elaborations projected upon it." Conventional reality is "free from elaboration from the very beginning"; it "never required liberation from ontology. Only conceptual thought required liberation" (Part I point 19). The purity is not something cleaned; it is the absence of what was only ever added.

Unproduced, but not an uncaused base. Namkhai Norbu's "uncreated, ever pure, and self-perfected, not something that has to be constructed" supports "unproduced" in one sense: freedom is not produced. But "this book holds that freedom is not produced and is not a result of causes, but also that it is not an uncaused base. The correction is to the self-description of the tradition" (Part I points 8, 17).

Not a pure state hidden under stains. Freedom is not "a hidden state that a habit covers over" (Part I point 22); non-recognition is not "a hidden nature going unseen" (point 12). The traditional pure nature covered by adventitious stains is read without the hidden layer: the stain is a way of holding, not a coating on something beneath.

Not "a confused life is already free". On sahm-sah-rah and neer-vah-nah: "It does not say that suffering is liberation, or that a confused, grasping life is already free. It says that sahm-sah-rah and neer-vah-nah are not two places, two kinds of stuff, or two worlds." Purity concerns the nature, not conduct or experience; the path remains "the removal of a misreading" (Part I points 16, 22, 26).

The pair to spontaneous presence. "Later commentators have argued at length that appearance-emptiness inseparability and Zog-chen's primordial purity and spontaneous presence are identical in what they refer to, however differently they are approached." Ka dag names the empty side, lhun grub the appearing side (point 5); their identity is presented as the author's synthesis (point 11).

Empty too. Singled out, primordial purity is designated and empty (Part I points 14–15). "Primordially open" and similar words describe how appearing already functions, not a property it has (Part I point 26). See point 15 for the basis as a whole.

5. Spontaneous presence (lhun grub) is dependent arising without a producer. It names "appearing that owes its completeness to nothing hidden". It is not an uncaused ground. Spontaneous presence and dependent arising are the same nature under two descriptions, and neither is higher.

Explanation.

Two questions kept apart. The first asks how an appearance comes about: every appearance, mental or material, arises from causes and conditions, and the MMK's analysis works at that level. The second asks about the nature of what appears: that nature is emptiness, which is not produced, so "why?" does not apply to it. Keeping the two apart is what lets "spontaneous" and dependent arising agree. "Spontaneous" never means "uncaused source" (Part I point 17).

Two descriptions, not interchangeable, neither higher. Dependent arising is the analytic description, the appearing as described while dividing is at work. Spontaneous presence is the experiential description, the same appearing when the reifying habit stops: "already complete", not waiting for construction. The two are not interchangeable, and neither is higher than the other.

Not an ingredient on top. Spontaneous presence "does not name an additional ingredient sitting on top of emptiness". It is "not produced by effort, and so it is not a state one gets more of by trying harder".

The subtlest hiding place. It can become "the last and subtlest place substantialism can hide": a producer granted one final exemption. Point 15's test applies.

Named as synthesis. That the two are one nature under two descriptions is the author's reading; some lineages hold Zog-chen as its own vehicle (point 11).

6. Display has nothing behind it. Thoughts are the display (rtsal), not obstacles in front of the nature of mind. Display "must not be heard as the display of something standing behind it… Display here means only this: dependent arising, vivid and unobstructed, with nothing behind it." Recognised as already unfixed, thought self-releases (rang grol). AE Ye Shes.

7. Self-knowing is not a self-standing knower. Rang rig and rigpa do not posit a witness or an awareness that is independently established. "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more svah-bhah-vah." The Yoh-gah-chah-rah self-awareness critiqued by Chun-drah-keer-tee is not adopted. Self-knowing awareness is not posited, not even as convention. What the word points to is kept only as a denial: knowing is not lit by another, and nothing lights it, not even itself. There is no second act and no knower behind; this clarity is as empty as what appears. OP Ch 19, Ch 21.

8. Vividness is the absence of mediation. It is "not intensity added to ordinary perception… not a quality that makes appearances more real". It is not an undesignated floor uncovered when designation stops. What stops is reifying, and appearances are seen without being held at a distance. OP Ch 20; Art. Across the articles, point 30.

9. The taste is affirmed; the reification is refused. Pure awareness, luminous presence and mirror-like clarity are genuine tastes: "neither mistaken nor fabricated". The error is the last refuge, where "the self may dissolve, while awareness itself becomes the new Self… luminosity quietly assumes the role once occupied by substance". AE Starting Point, Spiritual Bypass.

10. Ye shes is prahj-nyah completed. Gnosis is not a second attainment or a separate faculty. It is "the same clarity, no longer needing an object apart from itself to be clear about". AE Ye Shes.

11. The Mahd-yah-mah-kah–Zog-chen union is named as the author's synthesis. Reasoning and direct recognition converge on one nature from two directions. The books present this as a Mahd-yah-mah-kah reading of Zog-chen, not as Zog-chen's self-understanding in every lineage. Some lineages treat Zog-chen as its own resultant vehicle. AE Resonances; OP Note on Method.

12. Rigpa is the knowing of the basis, not an awareness that is the basis. In Zog-chen, rigpa (vidyā, "knowledge") is the knowledge of the basis, and some sources call it the pristine awareness that is the basis itself. The canon takes it in the first sense only: recognising appearing as unfixed, dependent and empty. It is not a witness, not a self-standing knower and not a ground. "Knowing clarity" is the books' gloss, not a translation.

Explanation.

Its opposite. Non-recognition (ma rigpa) is not a hidden nature going unseen: appearing "is taken as fixed, and its unfixed, dependent character goes unrecognised. Nothing hidden is waiting behind it to be found."

Self-knowing without a second act. Against Chun-drah-keer-tee's critique of self-awareness (the blade that cannot cut itself, Mahd-yah-mah-kah-vah-tah-rah chapter 6), the Buddhist logicians' lamp (Dhar-mah-keer-tee, Prahj-nyahkaragupta) reads self-knowing as "not a second act performed on the flow of awareness"; read so, "like the flow it is empty, not an independent awareness". The canon does not take up this lamp as a faculty: self-knowing is not posited, even conventionally, and is kept only as the denial in point 7 (nothing lights knowing, not even itself).

Recognised, not built up. Recognition (ngo shes) is decisive and often identified with awakening. It is not "a state built up through effort, or a result gained by practice", and it still takes clear knowing.

Display and self-release. Thoughts are rigpa's display (rtsal), with nothing behind it; recognised as unfixed, they release themselves (rang grol) (point 6).

Designated and empty. Rigpa, once singled out, is designated and empty like everything else (Part I points 14–15). Ye shes is prahj-nyah completed, not a second faculty (point 10).

Not to be reintroduced (OP verification log): Mipham's lamp reply; "rigpa's luminosity is not a svah-bhah-vah because it is not an independent object"; the rtogs pa / ngo shes distinction.

13. Self-release: a thought frees itself because nothing stands behind it. Thoughts are display (rtsal), not a veil in front of the nature of mind. Recognised as unfixed, a thought releases itself as it arises (rang grol), "like a drawing on water, which needs no eraser". Unrecognised, the same display is taken as solid and binds. "The difference is never the thought. It is whether the thought is read as a thing standing on its own."

Explanation.

The word. The canon says self-release (rang grol), not "self-liberation" (Part III).

Not suppression. Suppression pushes thoughts down and can make a stillness that "becomes one more fixed state": "grasping the absence of grasping". Self-release lets thoughts dissolve because they are not followed or fixed. Mahd-yah-mah-kah says the same from its side: Chun-drah-keer-tee accepts śamatha as a conventional means, but the decisive step is analysis. Practice is not a campaign against thinking.

A stopping, not an achievement. "In not being followed they do not become a wall." The gap between knower and known is constructed, so its absence is a stopping, available wherever the construction is not running.

"Self" means no outside remedy, not a self. No antidote is applied from outside. There is no self-standing knower doing the releasing (point 7), and nothing real is set free. As with neer-vah-nah (Part I point 16), liberation is "not a change in what exists. It is a change in how what exists is held": the ending of the act of fixing.

Empty too. Self-release, once singled out, is designated and empty (Part I point 14). It is not a new state to possess: "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground."

Source caution (OP verification log). Longchenpa's Sems nyid rang grol is cited for its theme only. No passage on thoughts was confirmed in the Guenther or Thondup translations, so the description of thoughts not being followed is the book's own.

14. There is no background awareness: clarity is the appearing itself. "The sound does not need a listener behind it in order to be clear. The visible does not require a background awareness in order to be vivid. The clarity is the manifesting itself, not something it possesses." The taste of presence is genuine; making it the Self, the ground or an ever-present witness is the subtlest form of the same fixing. (The books do not use the phrase "I AM"; they treat it as the witness, the observer, Presence and background awareness.)

Explanation.

The witness is looked for and not found. "You will find a tree and a seeing. You will not find a viewer standing apart from both." Even "the sense of a someone who hears… is itself something that appears… It is not the hearer."

Nobody has disappeared. "This does not mean that nobody is seeing, and it does not mean that a person has disappeared… The one who sees is not found apart from the seeing." The person remains as the mere I (Part I point 18).

No blank either. When the witness is not found, "experience does not go blank… The tree is more vivid, not less." What goes is the distance, not the vividness (point 8).

The taste is real; the reification is the error. Pure awareness, luminous presence, mirror-like clarity and boundless consciousness are "neither mistaken nor fabricated". But "the observer may disappear, while Presence quietly becomes the new observer… The centre has disappeared, yet the background remains", and it is hard to see "because it feels so complete" (point 9).

The decisive question. "Does Presence itself inherently exist? … If the self can be examined, why not awareness? … If inherent existence cannot be granted to the individual, on what basis should it be granted to consciousness itself?" (Part I points 4, 14).

Not an invariant that switches on and off. Deep sleep and anaesthesia are "quieter configurations of appearance", not an awareness "turned off". "If an entity were truly invariant and unconditioned, it could not interact with, perceive, or be affected by changing conditions."

Not a ground or a witness under another name. Luminosity is "not a light behind appearances, nor an awareness standing apart to illuminate them" (point 1). Rigpa is the knowing of the basis, not an awareness that is the basis (point 12). "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more svah-bhah-vah" (point 7). Knower and known are designated together (Part I point 5).

Losing the background is not yet the whole view. In total exertion "the phenomenology is authentic, but the interpretation remains substantialist": the background can return as one "field of luminous immediacy", as process or as a seamless whole. It ends when emptiness extends the same unfindability to appearances, conditions and dependence itself (Part I point 18).

15. The basis (gzhi) is the emptiness of appearing, not a ground behind it. In Zog-chen the basis is the original condition of every being, out of which the path and its fruit are said to unfold; recognising it is rigpa. The canon keeps the word and reads it as the emptiness of appearing: no foundation that appearances are produced from or rest on, no hidden state waiting to be uncovered, and no uncaused source. "Where the traditional language of basis is used, the book means this reinterpreted sense." Point 4 states its primordial purity; this point states what the basis is and is not.

Explanation.

A ground only relatively. Longchenpa "does not treat the ground as a foundation: it is called a ground only from a relative perspective, and in the true nature of reality it is empty of being one." The book "rejects any foundation that appearances are produced from or rest on, and reads gzhi as the emptiness of appearing" (point 4).

Not an uncaused base. "Freedom is not produced and is not a result of causes, but also… it is not an uncaused base. The correction is to the self-description of the tradition, not an interpretation of it" (Part I points 8, 17).

The three aspects, read on these terms. Empty essence (ngo bo) is the emptiness of appearing; clear nature (rang bzhin) is the knowing clarity of appearing itself, not a substance behind it (point 1); unobstructed compassion (thugs rje) is not yet given a reading (Part I point 24, "Gap").

Openness is not a property of the basis. In Zog-chen sources openness (stong, as in rig stong, gsal stong) characterises the ground. "The book instead treats openness as the way unfixed appearing is seen, not as a property of emptiness or of the ground."

Known, not uncovered. Knowledge of the basis is rigpa, the knowing of the basis, not an awareness that is the basis (point 12). Non-recognition is not "a hidden nature going unseen… Nothing hidden is waiting behind it to be found." Basis, path and fruit are not a hidden state, a journey and its unveiling (Part I points 22, 25).

Where a ground comes from. A common feature is abstracted from particulars, "the sameness of 'chariot'… or of 'presence' across many experiences", and then reified: "taken to exist as a thing in its own right, standing beneath the particulars and grounding them… The ground was never there. It was produced by reifying a useful abstraction."

Recognition stays a non-implicative negation. "If 'the nature of each appearance' becomes a positive ground beneath appearances, the disease of reification has returned in a new form." "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground" (Part I point 21).

Not the basis of designation. The Zog-chen basis (gzhi) is not the basis of designation (gdags gzhi), the parts or aggregates a thing is designated on. That basis "is itself merely designated, so that designation never bottoms out in something given" (Part I point 9). Neither is a floor.

Designated and empty. Singled out as "the basis", it is designated and empty like everything else (Part I points 14–15). The "ground-nature" taken as a findable ground is "substantialist non-duality" (Part I point 21). Do not reintroduce "groundless ground" (Part III).

Named as a reading. "Some Zog-chen authors, Longchenpa among them, state that the ground is empty and not a subtle existent. The book's reading draws on that statement. It does not claim that this reading is the only one available within Zog-chen" (point 11).
