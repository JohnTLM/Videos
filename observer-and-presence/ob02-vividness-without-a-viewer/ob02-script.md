# Chapter 2. Vividness Without a Viewer

When the witness is not found, experience does not go blank.

This is easy to miss. The search for a witness often comes with a fear: that without it there will be nothing, a dim and unfelt world. The looking shows the opposite. The tree is more vivid, not less. The rain sounds closer, not further away.

What changes is not the vividness. What changes is the architecture.

Ordinary experience is arranged in pairs. A viewer and a view. A hearer and a sound. A thinker and a thought. Each object is placed at a distance from the one it appears to. Take away the viewer, and the distance goes too. The sound of rain is no longer a sound that reaches someone. It is simply heard, as it is. Nothing stands at the window receiving it, because nothing was ever standing there. The appearances are not less real. They are less mediated.

Think of the colour red. A patch of red, in ordinary light. What is the red? It is not the wavelength of the light; that is a number, measured by instruments. It is not the cells in the eye. It is not the surface, which has no colour in the dark. It is not the attention that picks it out.

And yet the red is not apart from any of these. Change the light, and the red changes. Change the eye, and it changes. Change the attention, and something else stands out.

The same grey looks lighter on a dark ground, and darker on a bright one. The shade is not in the grey alone. It appears in a relation.

This is what dependence looks like when it is seen directly. The red depends on its conditions. And it is vivid in itself. It does not need a viewer behind it. It needs the light, the eye, the surround and the seeing. When they meet, it is complete, as the appearance it is.

The Dzogchen tradition has a word for the clarity of an appearance: luminosity. The word is easily misread. It can sound like a bright substance behind the display, shining through it like a lamp through a window. Looked at closely, the clarity is not behind anything. It is the knowing clarity of the appearance itself. A sound is heard as sound. Cold is felt as cold. The knowing and the known are not two things.

This matters, because it removes the need for a carrier. A soul, a stream of consciousness, a store of potentials: each of these is itself something that appears, and each would need a carrier of its own. Luminosity is not that kind of thing. It is not a ground beneath appearances.

Two things follow. Experience can be vivid and complete without being owned. And it can end without being taken from anyone. Nothing is lost when it passes. It passes because its conditions change.

A grey wall is as vivid as a sunset, in the sense that matters here. It appears, it is clear, and it is not found apart from its appearing.

In the seen, there is only the seen. In the heard, only the heard. And no one stands behind it, to receive it.
