# Chapter 8. Continuity Without a Carrier

What makes a life one life? A habit one habit? A river one river?

The logic of substance has one answer: something persists. A soul, a stream of consciousness, a neural core, the energy that is conserved. Each is put forward as the carrier that runs through time. Take it away, the substantialist expects, and only disconnected flashes remain. Each moment would be sealed off from the next.

The logic of dependency rejects both pictures: the carrier, and the disconnection.

Nāgasena's lamp is the classic image. The flame in the last watch of the night is not the flame of the first watch. Nor is it a different flame. Nothing passes from one to the next. Yet the second is not unconnected with the first. Continuity is designated, named and relied on, across a succession that is neither identical nor wholly different.

Think of a melody known by heart. Each playing is new. None is the one before. There is no tune stored somewhere between playings, waiting to be released. Yet the tune is recognised across them all. It can be played well or badly, remembered or forgotten, changed by practice.

Habit, character, skill and karma are like this. Each moment is conditioned by what went before. The link is real, and it is reliable. But it is designated across the moments. It is not carried by something running through them.

Two things follow. First, nothing is lost. Memory, responsibility, learning, and the karmic continuity the tradition describes all remain, as designations that hold.

Second, nothing is fixed. A continuity with no essence inside it can bend. A habit can loosen. A character can soften. A grievance can end. There was never anything underneath that had to stay the same.

Continuity without a carrier is more reliable than the nihilist expects. And it is more open than the substantialist can allow.
