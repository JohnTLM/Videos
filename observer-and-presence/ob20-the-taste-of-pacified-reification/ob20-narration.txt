What is it like, from the inside, when the habit of making things solid falls quiet?

That habit has a name: reification, turning what appears into fixed, separate things. When it is pacified, something remains. An old formulation names it. It comes from the Bah-hee-yah sutta, in the Oo-dah-nah. In the seen, only the seen. In the heard, only the heard.

These words are sometimes misread. Some take them to reduce experience to isolated flashes of sense. Others take them to describe a blank. Neither reading fits.

The words name a taste. Seeing is just seeing. Hearing is just hearing. No seer stands behind the seeing. No hearer stands behind the hearing. The gap between observer and observed was a designation, a label laid over immediate experience. It was never found in the experience itself.

When the labelling stops, experience has a vividness that is hard to put into words. Colours are vivid. Sounds are immediate. Thoughts lose their heavy sense of being owned.

This taste is a sign. It marks that the reifying habit has been seen through. It is not a higher stage on a path, and it is not an attainment. It is recognised. And the moment one tries to keep it as an accomplishment, the fixing has returned.

The witness shows the difference. If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more svah-bhah-vah, one more thing with a nature of its own. If it is simply the vivid, unobstructed quality of the flow, then nobody is doing the tasting, and nobody is doing the seeing.

Nor is the taste a denial of the world. The seen is still seen. The heard is still heard. Nothing has been taken out of experience.

What has gone is an addition: a viewer, and a ground. And with them, the sense that experience is happening to someone, from somewhere.
