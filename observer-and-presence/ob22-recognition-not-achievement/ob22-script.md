# Chapter 22. Recognition, Not Achievement

There is one thing effort cannot do. It cannot produce what is already complete.

The natural state is not produced by stopping thought. It is not achieved by pushing appearances away. Thought is recognised as already empty, already not fixed. And in that recognition, it stops fixing.

Recognition is the key word. Lhun grub means already complete. It is not produced by effort, so it is not a state one gets more of by trying harder. The moment effortlessness is treated as an accomplishment, it has become one more fixed object. Even the word "effortless" needs watching. It is recognised. And the moment it is held as something to maintain, it has been left.

The same caution applies to negation. The Middle Way warns that those who take emptiness as a view are incurable. A way out that negates appearances until nothing is left is still a fixer. It is only working on a different object.

Recognition is different in kind. It does not negate. It sees that appearances are already not fixed. So there is nothing further to do to them.

Madhyamaka draws a distinction that helps here. One kind of negation denies, and puts nothing in its place. Another kind denies, and installs a positive ground instead. Recognition must stay with the first kind. If "the nature of each appearance" becomes a ground beneath appearances, the disease of reification has returned in a new form.

Practice, then, turns around three recognitions. One is that conditions are what work. Anger is not a thing with its own nature. It reshapes itself when the attention, the interpretation and the circumstances change. Another is that openness is simply what dependence means. Practice works because nothing is fixed. And the last is that there is nothing to hold, and no one apart from the holding. The practitioner is named on the practising, and is not found apart from it.

The trap remains. Openness, non-holding and emptiness can quietly become the new things held. A state to reach. An attitude to keep up. A view to defend. But openness, too, is empty. It is not something one has. It is the absence of what one was holding.

Even writing about this is part of the practice. Words composed from unresolved grasping tend to make a reader who grasps at the words. The most a text can do is point, and then get out of the way.

So the book ends where it began. The observer that seemed to stand behind experience has not been found. Vividness has been seen without a viewer. The substrates, the logics and the ways of seeing have each been looked at. Each was found to be a designation that works, and cannot stand on its own.

And nothing needs to be produced to take the place of the habit that was seen through.
