By a spell, a piece of wood appears as an elephant. There is no elephant. But is there really wood?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

In the Dependent Arising series I say more than once that I part from Yoh-gah-chah-rah, the school of Mind-Only. I said where: making mind the floor. That is fair to Yoh-gah-chah-rah as it is commonly read.

But Yoh-gah-chah-rah has a key of its own, the three natures. Someone who knows it will ask whether I have met it. This is where I meet it.

Vah-soo-bun-doo puts it in three lines. The dependent nature is what appears. It arises from causes and conditions. The imagined nature is how it appears: as a thing out there, grasped by a me in here. And the perfected nature is that it never exists the way it appears.

His image is a magic show. By a spell, a piece of wood appears as an elephant. The elephant is the imagined nature. The appearing is the dependent nature. And that there is no elephant in it is the perfected nature.

Ah-sahng-gah gives another image. Gold-bearing earth, before firing, shows the clay and not the gold. Fired in wisdom, the clay goes, and the gold shows. The dependent nature is the earth. Seen wrongly, it is defiled. Seen rightly, it is pure.

A soo-trah of this school takes the old saying that all things lack a nature of their own. It does not deny it. It explains it, one lack for each nature.

The imagined lacks a defining mark of its own, like a flower in the sky. The dependent lacks a nature of its own in its arising, like a magic show. And the perfected is the absence of a self itself.

And it warns: take the saying as nothing at all, and you fall into nihilism.

Much of this I can walk with. What appears arises in dependence. It is vivid, it works, and it is not what it seems. The elephant is never there. And seeing that is not losing the wood. That is close to what I mean by empty, and not ghostly.

But I part at three places. And they are narrower than mind is the floor.

The first. In the Tibetan presentation of Mind-Only, the dependent nature must exist by its own character. Something real has to be there, it is said, for the imagining to rest on. Otherwise, nihilism.

Here I side with the Prah-sahng-gee-kah reading of Nah-gar-joo-nah. No own character, not even conventionally. Things work because they are empty, not because something real lies under the imagining.

The second. Yoh-gah-chah-rah explains karma across time with a storehouse consciousness, which carries seeds from life to life.

I agree with Tsong-kah-pah in refusing the storehouse. I part from him by not naming any linking factor at all, even a designated one. Neither carried, nor cut. As Chun-drah-keer-tee puts it, an action does not inherently cease, so nothing needs to carry it.

The third. Yoh-gah-chah-rah holds that each moment of awareness knows itself. Otherwise, it asks, how could I remember seeing?

Chun-drah-keer-tee answers that memory arises in dependence on the earlier seeing. No extra knower is needed. Clarity is never absent from what appears. But made into something that knows itself apart from what appears, it is the knower coming back.

These three come to one thing. Each keeps something real underneath. A real basis. A real carrier. A real self-knowing. Take them away, and what is left is not less. The bell still rings.

The commonly read Yoh-gah-chah-rah does keep mind as the floor. Vah-soo-bun-doo used the hungry ghosts and the river of pus. Shared karma ripening in the mind is enough, he said, with no outer river.

Chun-drah-keer-tee turned the story around. Mind and object stand or fall together. The dream proves nothing. If waking memory shows the dreaming mind was there, it shows the dream objects were too. In the absence of an object, there is also no intellect.

His answer is my position. Idealism is materialism turned inside out.

Some scholars read Yoh-gah-chah-rah not as only mind exists, but as an account of how experience is built. On that reading the gap is smaller, and the three places I named are where it remains.

There are two keys here. The soo-trah calls its own key the third turning, the definitive one. It calls the plain saying, that all things lack a nature, provisional. Mahd-yah-mah-kah says the reverse.

I take Nah-gar-joo-nah's reading as final, for one reason. It leaves nothing standing underneath, and it does not need to. What appears is dependent all the way. And that is exactly why it works.

Shahn-tah-rahk-shee-tah joined the two. Yoh-gah-chah-rah for everyday seeing, and the test of one or many for analysis. I borrow his test. I do not borrow the everyday mind-only.

This is Yoh-gah-chah-rah: the three natures, and where I part.

The elephant was never there. And nothing real was needed under the wood.
