# Undeniably There, or Formed?

"If you think you understand quantum mechanics, you don't understand quantum mechanics." Richard Feynman is said to have quipped that.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

The same holds for dependent arising. If you think you understand it, you don't.

I can agree that nothing has an essence of its own. I can say so fluently. And still I hold the world exactly as before. Solid. Out there. Mine.

Agreeing is not living in it.

So I will not argue first. I will set two ways of seeing side by side, three times, as sharply as I can. Then we can ask which one holds.

First, an apple.

Look at it. I can toss it from hand to hand. Hold it tightly. Bite into it. Nothing seems more plainly there.

It appears undeniably objective. External. Out there. Existing on its own. It was in the bowl before I walked into the kitchen, with its own weight, its own hardness, its own edge, drawn from its own side.

That is innate grasping. The naming disappears so completely into what appears that the apple seems to stand by itself.

Or, and it is a big or, look at how this apple is formed.

Six streams of consciousness, each dependently originated, meeting and interacting, and named apple in the meeting.

Touch. Coolness, firmness, the pull of weight against my palm. These are not stored in the fruit, waiting to be found. They arise as skin meets fruit.

Seeing. Red, which turns orange under candlelight, and grey at dusk. It arises with light, eye and attention.

Hearing. The crunch as my teeth break the skin. That sound was never sitting inside the apple, waiting to be let out.

Smell and taste. Sweetness and tartness, only when flesh meets tongue and breath.

And mind. Recognising it. Remembering other apples. The name, apple.

This is not what the apple really is. It is how it forms.

Each stream is complete in its own conditions. None of the six ever meets an apple standing on its own. What ties them into one solid thing out there, with a solid me in here tossing it, is formation running on habit. So quick and smooth that it goes unnoticed.

And the streams are not a bare layer underneath, given first and named later. The coolness, the red and the crunch are designated too. They are no more found than the apple.

Nor are the six streams a factory in here, producing the apple. Seeing arises with eye and colour. Touch arises with skin and contact. Consciousness is as dependently arisen, and as unfindable, as the apple. The apple is not found apart from the streams. And it is not made by them either.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. The apple can still be tossed, eaten, and nourish me. Seeing how it forms takes nothing away from it.

Second, a mountain.

It is massive and still. It was there long before me, and it will be there long after. It does not move when I look away. It stops the climber, whether or not he believes in it.

If anything is undeniably objective, external, and existing on its own, surely it is the mountain.

Or, and again it is a big or, look at how this mountain is formed.

From the valley, it is a blue shape against the sky. On the path, it is the ache in my legs, the thin cold air, the crunch of scree underfoot. On the map, it is contour lines and a height in metres.

And the name mountain draws an edge where nature drew none. Where does the mountain stop, and the plain begin?

Look for the mountain among the rocks, the soil, the slopes and the tree line. It is not among them. And it is not apart from them.

When no one is looking, is it there?

Two claims sound alike, and are not. It is still there when I look away and look back. That is true. It is checked the ordinary way. I look again. Others see it. The road is still there to drive on.

It exists from its own side, whether or not anything ever meets it. That is a different claim. And nothing has earned it. It slips in because the first claim works so reliably.

But being reliable is not being independent.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. The mountain still stops the climber, and still holds up the road.

Third, a bat, a bee and an ant.

Surely, though, the mountain is one. A human, a bat, a bee and an ant all stand before the same mountain. Each sees it in its own way. But what they see is one mountain, really there. The differences are only in the seeing. One world, many pictures of it.

Or, and it is the biggest or, ask each of them: is there a mountain there? The answers will not match, because the worlds do not match.

I see a bounded shape against the sky, with a summit, slopes and a base.

A bat, finding its way by echo, meets returning sound, with no clean edge where mountain stops.

A bee, seeing in ultraviolet among flowers and light, may hardly meet the mountain as one thing at all.

An ant takes a day to cross one boulder. It meets ridges of grain, warmth, and the scent of its nest. Never a mountain.

This is not four creatures looking at one finished mountain from four angles. A different body singles out a different world. The mountain, the one who meets it, and the channel of meeting are designated together. None of them is the bedrock under the others.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. A rockfall can still crush the ant. Contact runs along its own channels, gravity here and scent there, whether or not the creature knows it.

Two ways of seeing, three times over. Which carries more valid truth?

I test them by experience. Then by logic. Then I ask which one explains how things work.

By experience. Toss the apple again, and look honestly. What is actually met? Coolness. Red. Weight. The crunch. The name. At no point do I meet an apple standing on its own behind them.

The self-standing apple is never found. It is supplied.

The feeling that it is undeniably there is real, as a feeling. But a feeling of solidity is not a finding.

The same goes for the mountain. I meet the view, the climb, the map. Never a mountain in itself, apart from all of them.

By logic. Take the first view at its word.

An apple with its own fixed nature could not ripen. It could not bruise, or turn orange in candlelight. A fixed nature does not change with conditions.

A mountain with edges drawn from its own side would show those edges to the bat and the ant. They meet none of them.

And the one hidden mountain behind all four creatures cannot be described at all. If it has features, a summit and slopes, why do the bat and the ant meet none of them? If it has no features, a featureless something cannot be what any of them meet.

Finally, anything put forward as self-standing undermines itself at once. Even picking it out needs contrast, context and a name. The very dependencies it claims to escape.

By working. The first view says things work because they are solid, and real from their own side. But what is fixed cannot interact. And what cannot interact cannot work.

The second view explains the working fully. An apple is tossed and eaten. A mountain holds up a road. A rockfall crushes an ant. These are dependently arisen patterns, meeting other patterns reliably.

Working never needed an essence.

So the first view is not the truer one. It is the default one. It carries the weight of habit, not of evidence. The second carries both experience and logic.

This does not make the everyday claims false. The apple is an apple. The mountain is still there when I look back. And I still say so.

What fails is only the extra claim: from its own side.

Nothing that works is lost. Only a floor that was never there is gone.

This is Part 1 of a series on dependent arising. Here I have only set the two ways of seeing side by side, and asked which one holds.

In Part 2, The Illusion of the Given, I take apart the belief left standing: that one world is already there, finished, and that beings only see it differently. It starts from one bowl of liquid. A human sees water. A hungry ghost sees pus. A fish sees its home. A god sees nectar.
