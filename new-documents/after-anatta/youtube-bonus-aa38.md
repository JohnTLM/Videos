### Title

Dependent Arising Section 1 Part 4: Empty, Not Ghostly | Thusness

### Description

If nothing has an essence, is anything really there? Under the first three parts sits a fear: without essence, the world must be hollow, ghostly, a kind of void. Set beside what happens, it falls apart. Without essence, the car still knocks you down. It still rains. And you still get sick, and the medicine still works.

The ghostly void is a misunderstanding, born of a habitual way of understanding (real means having a core), of analysing (finding no core, it reports "nothing there" instead of "nothing missing"), and of orienting (a solid me, facing solid things). The force of the car never came from a core. The room was never standing on that floor. Nihilism is not the opposite of the inherent view; it is the inherent view in mourning.

Then what the first three parts have shown, the test one last time, and why I keep using the apple, the car and the rain: to correct a misconception with impact, gradually, from rejection to acceptance, from counter-intuitive to highly logical and tenable.
