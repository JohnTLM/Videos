## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 6: Complete, and Dependent (AA45, 10:16)

### Title

Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 6: Complete, and Dependent | Thusness

### Description

Ash is just ash, completely. And ash is the wood, the fire, the air and the time, appearing as ash. How can both be true?

In the seen, just the seen: complete, with nothing behind it. Yet whatever appears depends. Held alone, each half is misheard: as islands sealed off, or as links in a chain and knots in a web. The Zen master Dōgen called the union total exertion: each event spends itself completely, complete as its conditions, with nothing held back behind it and no cosmos packed inside it.

Two guards before Dōgen's images. First, a key in a lock: key, hand, lock and door work as one opening, with no seam, but not because they are one substance or parts of one Whole. They work seamlessly because none has a nature of its own to block the meeting: neither the same nor different (MMK 18.10). Second, "designated together" is not labels stuck on things, nor one stuff cut up by names: it is how appearing singles itself out, needing no words and making nothing. Then three ways of seeing, all at once: substantialist, anatta, dependent arising.
