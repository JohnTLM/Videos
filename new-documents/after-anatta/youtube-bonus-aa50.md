## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 10: Total Exertion: Huayan (AA50, 10:43)

### Title

Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 10: Total Exertion: Huayan | Thusness

### Description

Worlds in a grain of dust. A rafter, and the building it makes. A net of jewels, each shining in all the others. What do these images mean, and what must they not mean?

The classical home of total exertion in Chinese Buddhism: the Huayan school and its Flower Ornament sūtra (Avataṃsaka). Worlds in a grain of dust are about non-obstruction: what lacks its own core does not block; not worlds crammed into a tiny place. Fazang's rafter is a rafter only in the building, and lying in a yard it is a piece of wood; I say where I part from his "the rafter IS the building": neither the same nor apart (MMK 18.10). Indra's net: seamless without fusion, and Chengguan's four realms, the fourth (things and things without obstruction) closest to total exertion. What total exertion is for me: "the whole universe appears to be completely occupied with this one event", keeping "appears". Huayan gives the taste its colour, Dōgen its precision, Nāgārjuna its grammar. And four guards: not about space; principle is not a ground; the net is not found either; one is all is not one thing.
