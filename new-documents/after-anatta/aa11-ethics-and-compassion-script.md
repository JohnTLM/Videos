# Ethics and Compassion

A harsh word at breakfast can sour a whole day. A kind one can carry someone through it. So what happens to that, when no self is found behind the words?

This is one of the question pages of After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness. Its question is simple. Why does emptiness make care matter more, and not less?

Here is the worry. Step three found no self standing behind the person. So who is harmed, and who is kind? And if nothing exists by itself, why not simply shrug? Many people fear that emptiness makes ethics a convention we might as well drop. Step two called that negating too much: taking away the world along with the fixed self. This page shows why it does not follow.

First, the substance reading, stated fairly. For an act to matter, there must be a real doer who means it, a real victim who suffers it, and a real deed that stays until it bears fruit. Take away the fixed selves, and morality loses its footing.

Its mirror image sounds quite different. If everything is empty, nothing really happens, so nothing really matters. But it rests on the same idea. Both assume that mattering needs a solid core. One keeps the core. The other mourns it.

The tradition does not share that idea. Nagarjuna begins his chapter on action with kindness. A mind that restrains itself, that helps others and that is kind: that is the dharma, the seed of fruit, both after death and here. That fruit after death is the tradition's teaching. The author does not draw it out of emptiness.

Then Nagarjuna turns the worry around. If things were not empty, no one would ever do right or wrong. What could be done by what is not empty? An own-nature is not something that is made. A fixed thing cannot be acted on, and cannot act.

And his whole work ends with compassion. Out of compassion he taught the true Dharma, for the letting go of all views. To him, to Gautama, I bow. The word he uses there for that taking up, upādāya, is the same word he uses for designation in dependence. So the teaching itself arose in dependence: on compassion, for those who suffer.

Now run the four questions. What would a substance view say? A real doer, a real victim, and a deed that lasts until it ripens. Without them, nothing matters.

What does dependency say instead? Doer and deed are designated together. There is no doer without the doing, and no deed without a doer. A kind act arises from conditions, and bears fruit through them, as seed, sprout and fruit follow one another. Nothing is carried across from one to the next. And a deed with its own nature could never be done, could never change, and could never end.

What is affirmed conventionally? Right and wrong. Harm and kindness. Responsibility, promises, and the fruits of action. The person who acts, and who meets the results. Love, sorrow and care, exactly where they were.

Karma here means intentional action, and the way it bears fruit. It is real and reliable, with nothing carried across. And the one who acts is the mere I: the person, designated on body and mind. It acts, keeps its promises, and meets the results of what it has done.

What is never found? A self behind the kindness that really means it. A victim fixed as a victim. A store that holds the deed until it ripens.

This is Step seven at work. Something that existed by itself could not change, combine or act. A cruel person fixed in their nature could never learn, and could never regret. A kindness fixed in its nature could never be given. But the person is designated on what they do. So a person who acts differently becomes, in a real sense, a different kind of person. That is why change is possible at all.

So the word "more" has a precise sense, and only that sense. Emptiness does not produce care. It does not cause anything. What it does is remove the excuses. It isn't really happening. They are only appearances. There is no one there. Each excuse needs something fixed to hide behind, and nothing fixed is found.

A car with no essence still injures. The grief of the family is not an illusion. Nothing waits outside the chain of conditions to absorb what we do. What we do goes into the world, and the world answers. So ethics becomes the plain work of arranging conditions: a word, a pause, a kindness. The author's bonus article on the logic of dependency puts it that way.

Here the author is honest about a limit. Compassion is the wish to relieve suffering. His books say that emptiness takes nothing from compassion. Kindness works, and is felt, with no fixed self behind it. But they do not say how compassion relates to awakening. Does care arise by itself when emptiness is seen? Or is it a practice of its own, as in bodhicitta, the awakened heart that joins wisdom and compassion?

Dzogchen names compassion, thugs rje, as one aspect of the basis. The author has named it, without yet taking it up. And he leaves the question open, rather than answer it by claiming that emptiness causes compassion. It does not cause anything. So the question stays open, and the page says so.

There are two traps, one on each side. Too much is eternalism. Kindness needs a real self that means it. Then good and bad become verdicts on what someone is. A cruel person is cruel for ever. And my own goodness becomes something to defend.

Too little is nihilism. It is all empty, so nothing matters. Emptiness is used to excuse harm, or to skip care. If a reading of emptiness makes cause and result matter less, it has aimed past the target.

So, in practice. When an insult lodges and repeats, notice that it seems to sit in the words themselves. It still hurts. Then look for what it landed on, apart from the moment, the voice and the hearing.

When someone is cruel, the harm is real, and it should be met. And the person is not fixed as cruel. Conditions can change.

The view does not make the loss of someone we love painless. The pain is real. It can be met by care.

And notice a kindness you receive today. It is felt fully. No fixed self is found behind it. And it is no less kind.

So back to breakfast. The harsh word still sours the day. The kind word still carries someone through it. No self is found behind either one, and nothing fixed stands between us and what we do. That is why care matters more.
