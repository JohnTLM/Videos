# Designation: The Fist, the Wind and the Chariot

Make a fist. Feel it, tight and hard. Now open your hand. Where did the fist go?

No one has ever asked that question seriously. And a fist can hit.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is Part 3 of 7. In Part 2, conditions worked with no power in them, and no doer behind them. That left a question. What, then, is the thing we name? What does a name actually do?

Start where the question first opened for me: with anatta, no-self.

Anatta is the insight that there is no doer behind seeing. In seeing, only the seen. In hearing, only the heard. The person does not disappear. What falls away is one picture: someone standing behind the action, making it happen. And when the witness is not found, experience does not go blank. The tree is more vivid, not less.

But anatta, as I have known it, usually sees through one side. The seer has gone. The seen has not been looked into. The tree still seems to stand there from its own side. The chariot still seems to push the load. So a person can have a clear taste of no-self, and still picture the world in the old way.

Dependent arising is the same seeing, turned outwards. Nāgārjuna makes the same move everywhere. A goer does not go. There is no seer behind seeing, no fire standing apart from its fuel. Anatta takes the doer out of seeing. Dependent arising takes it out of everything else. The emptiness of self, and the emptiness of phenomena.

So here is the first insight. The name is drawn from the working. It is not added to it.

Habit puts things in this order. First there is a chariot, and then it carries. First there is a wind, and then it blows.

In experience, the order is the other way round. There is the carrying: wheels turning, the load moving along the road. Chariot is the name drawn from that carrying. There is the blowing: coolness moving across the skin, leaves stirring, a door creaking. Wind is the name drawn from that blowing.

So the blowing is the wind, completely. The cold on my skin is not a sign of a wind somewhere behind it. It is all the wind there ever was. The flashing is the lightning. When it rains, there is no it that rains. There is the raining.

This naming does not even need words. It needs only singling out. This sound, this colour, this ache, picked out as this. A this stands out only against what it is not, and only with these conditions. In a world of only blue, no one would see blue. So the singling out is not done to an appearance that was there first, waiting to be named. It is part of the appearing.

The second insight. You already live this logic.

None of this is strange in easy cases. A fist can hit, hard. When the hand opens, no one asks where the fist went. Fist names the hand, clenched.

Your cat sits on your lap. Stand up, and the lap is gone. It did not go anywhere. Lap names sitting.

A wave can knock you over. Look for the wave apart from the water moving, and there is nothing. No one feels the wave is any weaker for that.

A traffic jam makes you late for work. It has no core, no edge and no owner. It is cars, roads, the hour and the drivers, meeting. And you are late.

A melody moves you. A rainbow is photographed. A team wins the cup.

In every one of these, you already accept that something works fully with no essence of its own. And you are not disturbed at all.

The chariot, the apple, the body and the self have the same structure. The only difference is that they are more stable, and come back more often. A fist lasts seconds. A chariot lasts years. A body, a lifetime. Because they are stable and repeated, the habit has hardened around them, and the name has come to feel like a thing.

So nothing new is being asked of you. Only that you notice where you stopped using a logic you already use.

The oldest case is the chariot. The monk Nāgasena asks King Milinda where his chariot is. It is not the pole, not the axle, not the wheels, not the frame. Not their sum, and not something apart from them. Chariot is a name, used in dependence on these.

Candrakīrti makes the search complete, in seven ways. The chariot is not the same as its parts, and not other than them. It does not possess them, is not in them, and does not contain them. It is not their collection, and not their shape. Nothing is found.

And the parts are named the same way. The chariot is designated on its wheels. A wheel is singled out in turn, on rim, spokes and hub. They on their wood, and the wood on its grain. No bottom layer is ever reached, where something finally stands by itself and receives the first name.

The Buddhist tradition itself once set out to find that bottom layer. The Abhidharma tried to list what is finally there. Tables, people and chariots, it said, are only designated. What remains are the dharmas, the last units of experience. A moment of hardness, of heat, of sound. Each defined by its own mark. Earth by hardness. Fire by heat. And each mark was taken as its own nature, there from its own side.

Nāgārjuna aims at exactly this. Can space be there before its mark? No space at all, he says, is found before the mark of space. And the mark cannot apply to what has no mark, nor to what already has one. So mark and marked stand or fall together. Neither comes first. Which is to say, designated.

This leads to the third insight. An essence would stop the working.

Suppose the chariot did have an essence, a nature of its own, complete and independent. What could it do?

It could not be built, because something complete in itself needs no parts and no builder. It could not move, because moving is changing, and a fixed nature does not change. It could not carry, because carrying is meeting the load, the road and the horse. And something sealed in its own nature could meet nothing.

Nāgārjuna says it of every dharma. Own nature is not made, and depends on nothing. A nature that arose from causes would be something made. A nature becoming otherwise never makes sense. If you see things as existing by their own nature, you see them as needing no causes or conditions. And if all this were not empty, there would be no arising, and no passing away.

So the carrying does not happen in spite of a missing essence. An essence would make it impossible.

I am careful with the wording here. I do not say, because the chariot is empty, it carries. Emptiness is not a power behind the carrying. Emptiness does nothing. I say: the chariot carries, and what carries is found empty. An essence would have stopped it.

None of this denies that fire burns, or that earth is solid. A doctor still reads a fever. A builder still tests a beam. Take the hardness of the apple in my palm. It looks like the last thing left, earth's own mark, found at last. But hardness is singled out on touch, skin and pressure. It stands out only against softness. It arises with the hand that meets it. It is not a mark something carries from its own side.

So what is a chariot that is merely designated? It is not only a name. You cannot point at a heap of sand, call it a chariot, and ride it to town. Designation and working arise together. A name holds only where it works.

And it is not only in the mind. Just a label in the mind sounds like the humble answer. But it quietly sets up a mind that is found, standing behind, painting names onto a world. That mind is singled out too. No labeller stands behind it.

The chariot carries you to town. Chariot names that carrying. Looked for, no chariot is found behind it. None is needed.

And still, the mind is not satisfied. It asks: fine, but then what is it that carries?

That question is the subject of Part 4. It is the question behind every question.
