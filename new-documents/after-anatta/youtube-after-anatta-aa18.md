## 1. The Lamp That Does Not Light Itself (AA18, about 12:22)

### Title

The Lamp That Does Not Light Itself: Luminosity and Nāgārjuna | After Anatta

### Description

Sit by a window and listen to the rain. The sound is clear, and nothing has to light it up. Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing itself. But in the seventh chapter of the Middle Way, Nāgārjuna takes apart a lamp that lights up both itself and other things, and later teachers use the same move against a mind that knows itself. Does "clarity is the appearing itself" bring that lamp back?

No. Read slowly, the verse that refutes the lamp shows that clarity is never missing: "there is no darkness in the lamp". No appearing ever waited in the dark, so nothing lights it up, not another light and not itself. Then the four corners applied to clarity, the dark room (darkness is clear as dark), six lamps instead of one, the luminous mind of AN 1.51, and the subtle clear light, which is not a lamp under a basket. Last, why awareness is not kept on day and night: maintenance is the split, recognition unbinds the habit of seeing inherently, and in sleeping, fully sleep. A caution, from experience: practices that try to stay aware through sleep have brought many people sleeplessness and energy imbalances; they need a qualified master, and they are not needed for the insights of anatta and emptiness.
