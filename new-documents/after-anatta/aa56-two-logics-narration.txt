Watch a candle burn, and the answer comes before any thought. The flame is changing. Something is there, and something is happening to it.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Section 3 looked at one question, mind and matter, and found the gap between them made by the starting point. Underneath it was a default mode. Faced with what appears, we ask what it is, really, and cannot rest until a something is found.

This section takes up the way of reasoning that the default mode runs on. Not one question, but the logic that decides which questions seem natural, and which answers seem possible. I call it the logic of substance. Beside it, I set the logic of dependency. The two use the same words. Change. Cause. Identity. Continuity. The path. But they begin in different places, and the same words lead them into different worlds.

First, the candle.

Watch it for a minute. The flame leans, steadies, shrinks when the door opens, and recovers. Ask what is happening, and the answer is there before any thought. The flame is changing.

Notice the shape of that answer. There is a flame, and something happens to it. The flame is the stable one. The leaning and the shrinking are what befall it. If I ask what really changed, I go looking for the one that stayed the same, while its states came and went.

Even after all the arguments have been followed and accepted, this picture comes back the moment attention is off duty. It is not a belief that a counter argument can remove. It is a pattern of inference, working below argument. That is why I call it a logic.

So, two starting points.

The logic of substance starts with things, and then asks what happens to them. Do they arise? Do they last? Do they move, end, stay the same? The things are taken as given. The questions are about their fate.

The logic of dependency starts one step earlier. It does not begin with things. It asks how anything is identified as a thing in the first place. And it finds that a thing is identified only in dependence. On its parts. Its causes. What it is contrasted with. And the name under which it is picked out. No thing is found standing there before that identification.

So the two do not disagree about the candle's fate. They disagree about whether there is, before any identification, a candle whose fate it is. Everything else follows from that.

Now, the word logic needs care, because it is easy to hear in another sense.

I do not mean that the Middle Way has a special logic of its own. And I do not mean that emptiness is reached by going beyond logic. I mean the necessity of a consequence. Whoever proposes something that exists from its own side must say what it is. To say what it is, they must pick it out, by contrast and by name. And in picking it out, they have made it dependent. The proposal undermines itself.

Three things follow, and they matter.

It is not a survey. The logic does not look around the world and report that no essences have turned up so far. It shows what anyone proposing an exception must already be doing.

It ends in a negation without a thesis. It removes inherent existence, and puts nothing in its place. Not a void. Not a ground. Not a better something.

And it keeps the ordinary rules. It does not suspend non contradiction. It relies on it, throughout. The chariot cannot be both identical with its parts and different from them. Fire cannot be both one with its fuel and other than it. The analysis has its force because it holds the other side to exactly these rules.

I say this plainly, because it marks the difference between the Middle Way and its imitations. A view that claims to transcend logic can say anything, and so says nothing. The logic of dependency says one thing exactly. What is proposed as standing on its own is not found to do so.

What, then, of the famous refusals? Neither existent nor non existent. Neither arising nor ceasing. Are these not contradictions?

They are neither. They refuse a presupposition.

Ask: is the present King of France bald? There is no answer, yes or no. Not because the question is too deep. Not because logic has broken down. But because there is no present King of France. The question fails, before either answer could be true.

Does the self exist, or not? Does this moment arise, or cease? These fail in the same way. Each one presupposes a bearer with a status of its own. Something there, to exist or not exist, to arise or cease. When no such bearer is found, both answers fall together. The Buddha told Kahch-chah-nah that the world mostly leans on two notions, it exists, and it does not exist. The one who sees arising and ceasing as they are takes hold of neither.

We have met this move before. In Section 2, the question of what a thing is in itself fell away with the given. In Section 3, the hard problem fell away with the fixed natures it needed. Logic was never suspended. The question had nothing to be about.

Now go back to the candle, and look for the carrier.

The flame at this moment is wax vapour, air and light, that were not there a moment ago, and will be gone the next. Nothing in the flame is the flame across the minute. Take away the burning, and no flame is left over, to have been burning. The noun is never found apart from the verbs.

But the verbs are not found apart from the noun either. A flickering with nothing flickering is no more available than a flame that does not flicker. The walker and the walking, Nah-gar-joo-nah showed, are named together, and neither is found first. The same holds for the changer and the change.

So the logic of dependency does not replace the subject with a better subject. Not a process. Not a flow. Not an energy. That would be the habit again. A new passenger, in the old seat. It notices that the changer and the change are named together, within a frame of before and after. And that the frame itself does not stand on its own.

What remains is change, met without a thing that changes. The candle still burns down. The wax is shorter at midnight than at dusk. You can say correctly when it was lit, and when it went out. Nothing is lost from the burning. Only the passenger is let go. The something that was supposed to ride underneath, staying the same while everything about it differed.

So these are not two worlds, one false and one true, the second hidden behind the first. They are two ways of reasoning about the same candle.

The logic of substance is not stupid. It is how the habit reasons, and for most practical purposes it is fast, and good enough. What it cannot do is survive being looked at. The logic of dependency does not throw away the candle, the burning, or the clock. It only stops supplying the one who was supposed to be undergoing all of it.

This can be tested at once. The next time the picture of something changing comes, look for the something. Not in an argument. In what is in front of you. The sound that is fading. The thought that is passing. The flame that is leaning. Look for what is changing, apart from the changing.

The picture insists it must be there.

The looking finds more change, vivid and exact. And no changer behind it.
