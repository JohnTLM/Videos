### Description

Dependent arising, without arising. It sounds like a contradiction. Nagarjuna opens his whole work with it.

Section 6 reads the twelve links with Nagarjuna. The same sentence, "when this is, that is", heard two ways: as a handover from this to that, or as a pattern, two things found only together. His homage praises dependent arising as without arising and without ceasing: if a sprout could be found arising from its seed, it would not need to depend. Not from itself, not from another, not from both, not without a cause: what is refused is production, not causes. A condition is named from the effect's side, as long and short are named together; what is mutual is the defining, not the timing. Three senses of cause: production refused, alignment and designation kept, as a bell rings from bronze, strike, air and ear. Leave out the yeast and the loaf is flat, with nothing handed over. Dependent arising and emptiness have one meaning, and in the discourse to Kaccana the Buddha set aside both "it is" and "it is not".
