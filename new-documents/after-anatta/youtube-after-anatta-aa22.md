## 1. Three Descriptions, One Taste (AA22, 18:08)

### Title

Three Descriptions, One Taste: Anatta, Dependent Arising and Spontaneous Presence | After Anatta

### Description

Something always seems to stand behind an action, doing it: a seer behind seeing, a cause behind its effect, a ground behind appearances. Looked for, it is never found, and nothing is put in its place. Anatta, dependent arising and spontaneous presence are three names for that one finding. They are not three teachings or three stages, but three descriptions of one taste: no one behind it, nothing standing on its own, nothing missing.

Part One is the journey. Right after anatta comes a first taste: seeing is just colours, hearing just sound, with no effort, not even a process, and no "one" that everything is made of. It widens into Dōgen's total exertion. But a taste is not yet freedom: the habit of reifying stays, until Nāgārjuna's logic, checked against the taste, brings it up and unwinds it, hinge by hinge (parts, the whole, qi, energy, field, relations, substrate). Then the same taste is met again, wider and stable, as spontaneous presence; and then in the marketplace, with dirty hands. Part Two is the map: the three descriptions and their guards, the framework that is not the prison, the many faces of the taste, where the doer comes back, and where I stand with the masters.
