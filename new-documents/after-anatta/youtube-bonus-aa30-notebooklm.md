## When Thinking Drains the Qi (AA30, NotebookLM version, 8:44, YouTube Wmecpti2j4s)

### Title

When Thinking Drains the Qi: Sitting, the Brain and Suwen 39 | Thusness

### Description

One morning of hard thinking leaves me short of qi, and half an hour of exercise brings it back. I asked two AI tools why, and whether moving three times a day would help, then checked both answers myself. The observation holds, and so does the plan. But the popular explanation, that thinking pulls a flood of blood to the head, does not.

What the research shows instead: the brain uses about a fifth of the body's energy all the time, and hard thinking adds only a few percent. Long sitting slightly lowers blood flow to the brain, and a two-minute walk every half hour stops the drop (Carter and colleagues, 2018). A day of hard mental work raises glutamate in the front of the brain (Wiehler and colleagues, 2022): less like an empty tank, more like a jam. The Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic already said it: 思则气结, thinking knots the qi. Stuck, not empty. Then jing, qi and shen as a candle, why qi is not a hidden stuff, my three sessions a day, and a note on checking AI answers: praise is not proof.

These are my own notes and my own plan, not medical advice.
