## 1. What the Logic Is Looking At (AA12, 8:58)

### Title

What the Logic Is Looking At: Dharma, Designation and the World of Only Blue | After Anatta

### Description

Listen to a sound nearby: a car, a bird, the hum of a fridge. It is vivid and clear, and it needs no help to be known. Every step of the logic looks at something: a self, an object, a cause, matter, a person. They are not many topics. Each time, the logic is looking at a phenomenon, what Buddhism calls a dharma.

This video says what a phenomenon is: whatever can be experienced or known, conditioned or unconditioned. It shows why there is no bare appearance under the names, why designation needs no words, only singling out, and why singling out is already dependence. Picture a world of only blue: no one there would ever see blue. So by appearing, a thing is already empty. Then hold an apple: the hardness, the red, the crunch and the sweetness each arise in dependence, and the habit of the inherent framework ties them into one solid apple out there, with a "me" in here holding it. Untied, nothing is missing. Then the clarity: not a light behind the scene, and not a Self, but the appearing's own, as empty as everything else.

Then time itself: raise one hand and lower the other, and the raised hand ages a little faster. Carlo Rovelli's two watches, and the GPS satellites that must allow for it. And the ship of Theseus, and your own weight, met two ways.

## 5. The Four Questions (After Anatta 8, 7:33)

### Title

The Four Questions: The Koan's Method, Made Portable | After Anatta 8 · Nāgārjuna's Middle Way

### Description

Here is the koan once more. The last step of The Logic does not answer it. It gives four questions to ask of anything that appears.

What would a substance view say? What does dependency say instead? What is affirmed conventionally? What is never found? Each question is run on the koan, with its cautions: ask it of the one asking, and check what has been put in the place of what was negated. Why the koan is a paradox only inside the logic of substance, and how Nāgārjuna's Middle Way gives another way to meet it. Then the four questions on the self, an object, a cause, the person, and your own weight.
