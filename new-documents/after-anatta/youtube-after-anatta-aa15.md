## 1. Neither Carried Nor Cut (AA15, about 12:25)

### Title

Neither Carried Nor Cut: Continuity Without a Carrier | After Anatta

### Description

You are seeing, hearing, touching, thinking: six streams at once, and it all feels like one life, lived by one someone. If no self is carried from moment to moment, is everything cut off? And at death, do I simply end?

The fear of being cut off clings to the same self as the hope of being carried over: both need a thing with a being of its own. Nāgārjuna answers both with one reason: whatever comes to be in dependence on something is not that very thing, and not other than it, so it is neither cut off nor permanent.

Instead of outside images, this video looks where it can be felt directly: the six streams of consciousness, each arising with its own conditions, working together so smoothly that the mind pulls out of them one self, or one pure awareness, to carry it from sense to sense (the monk Sāti's mistake, and the Buddha's answer: consciousness is named by its condition, as fire by its fuel). In real time there has never been a carrier: each instant is complete. Lined up in time, the instants are neither same nor different, neither carried nor cut, and that is the continuity itself. No riverbanks, no gap, no bridge. Then: the same knot as in holding an apple, clarity never missing; one complete stroke and the sequence in Nāgārjuna, Dzogchen and Dōgen; sleep; death; and why "no carrier" never means "nothing goes on".
