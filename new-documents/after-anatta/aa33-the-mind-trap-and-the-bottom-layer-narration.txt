In The Fist, the Wind and the Chariot, the chariot carried you to town, and no chariot was found behind the carrying. None was needed.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Even after that is seen, the habit looks for a floor somewhere else. In my experience it hides in three places. Under the parts. Inside the mind. And in the world, when no one is looking. This video looks at all three.

The first place is under the parts.

The chariot is named on its wheels, axle and frame. Fine, the mind says. The chariot is only a name. But the wheels are surely real.

Look again. A wheel is singled out in turn, on rim, spokes and hub. They on their wood. The wood on its grain. No bottom layer is ever reached, where something finally stands by itself and receives the first name. There is nowhere for the search to end.

The Buddhist tradition itself once set out to find that bottom layer. The Ah-bee-dar-mah tried to list what is finally there. Tables, people and chariots, it said, are only designated. Take them apart, and they are gone. What remains are the dharmas, the last units of experience. A moment of hardness. Of heat. Of sound. Of feeling.

Each was defined by its own mark. Earth by hardness. Water by cohesion. Fire by heat. Space by not obstructing. And in the Ah-bee-dar-mah's own gloss, a dharma is so called because it bears its own mark. That mark was its own nature, there from its own side, whether or not anyone names it. So the chariot was designated, but its floor was not.

Nah-gar-joo-nah aims at exactly this. He takes the hardest case, space, which some schools held to be unconditioned. Can space be there before its mark? No space at all, he says, is found before the mark of space. A space with no mark would be a thing with no mark, and no such thing is found anywhere.

Then the mark must apply to something. But it cannot apply to what has no mark, nor to what already has one, and there is nowhere else for it to apply. So mark and marked stand or fall together. Neither comes first. Each is set up only with the other. Which is to say, designated.

And a dharma with its own nature could not arise, could not change, and could not cease. A nature becoming otherwise never makes sense. Grant the dharmas their own nature, and dependent arising stops working.

None of this denies that fire burns, or that earth is solid. A doctor still reads a fever. A builder still tests a beam. What goes is only their standing as the final building blocks.

Take the hardness of an apple in my palm. It looks like the last thing left, once the apple is seen through. Earth's own mark, found at last. But hardness is singled out on touch, skin and pressure. It stands out only against softness. It arises with the hand that meets it. It is not a mark something carries from its own side. The floor under the parts is not found. And the apple is hard, and the hand knows it.

The second place is inside the mind.

Strike a bell. The ringing is not made by the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear, and then sent out. It is their meeting. No power passes from the strike to the sound.

When the producing cause falls away like this, one part is almost always left over. The resounding ting is consciousness, I want to say. It is my very own clarity.

That reading is not wrong. It is an important insight. No hearer stands apart from the ting. The ting is the hearing.

What tends to happen next is the trap. Many, almost without fail, then fold everything into consciousness. The producer is gone, the hearer is gone, and mind is left standing as what everything is. The bell, the air and the ear become contents of one clarity. That is the ground coming back, this time as mind. A universal Awareness. A Presence with a capital P.

Dependency is the way out. The ting is there only with the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear. Being dependent, it is not mind from its own side, any more than it is bronze from its own side. Clarity is never absent from what appears. But lifted out, and made what everything is made of, it is one more thing.

When matter is seen through, the habit does not give up. It changes its stuff. Matter traded for mind is still stuff. What appears is not made of anything.

The same trap waits in a humble-sounding answer. The chariot is just a label in the mind. It sounds modest. But it quietly sets up a mind that is found, standing behind, painting names onto a world. That mind is singled out too. No labeller stands behind it. So the chariot is no more made of mind than it is made of chariot stuff.

The third place is the world, when no one is looking.

Take a mountain. It reliably stops the climber. It was there before anyone named it. But look for the mountain among the rocks, the soil, the height, the tree line. It is not among them, and not apart from them. No mountain comes with its edges drawn by nature.

Does a bat see a mountain? A bee? An ant? I see a bounded shape against the sky, with a summit, slopes and a base. A bat, finding its way by echo, meets returning sound, with no clean edge where mountain stops. A bee, seeing in ultraviolet among flowers, may hardly meet the mountain as one thing at all. An ant takes a day to cross one boulder, and never meets a mountain.

This is not four creatures looking at one ready-made mountain from four angles. Body and world arise together. A different body singles out a different world.

The usual reply is that the mountain is there all the same, and only the experiences differ. That reply slips in a mountain, finished before anything meets it. If that hidden mountain has a summit and slopes, why do the bat and the ant meet none of them? If it has no features at all, a featureless something cannot be what they all meet.

Nor is measuring a view from nowhere. Metres and maps are a human channel of contact, as reliable as any. When we agree on where the mountain is, it shows that our bodies and instruments are alike. It does not show one mountain standing there by itself. And the ant is not cut off. A rockfall can crush it.

So, when no one is looking, is the mountain there? Two claims sound almost the same, and are not.

The mountain is still there when I look away and look back. That is true. It is checked the ordinary way: looking again, others seeing it, the road still there to drive on.

The mountain exists from its own side, whether or not anything ever meets it. That is a different claim. Nothing has earned it. It slips in because the first claim works so reliably.

But reliability is not independence.

That line keeps me out of two mistakes. The mountain is an illusion, which no one is claiming. And the mountain exists on its own, which was never shown.

So the habit's three hiding places turn out to be empty. No floor under the parts. No mind under everything. No world standing there by itself, waiting for no one. And the apple is hard, the bell rings clear, and the mountain stops the climber.
