### Description

A toothache. A sunrise. A key in a lock. Each one hides a big idea. Where do the big ideas come from?

The close of Section 2, What is a phenomenon? What appears is vivid, not found and dependently arisen, and the mind fills the gap with big ideas. Each starts from a real feature of appearing and adds a thing that is never found. A toothache: privacy is an asymmetry of conditions, not a hidden inner room. A sunrise: objectivity is the reliable coherence of dependent arising, not a world given before any seeing. A key: causal power is found only where the lock, the hand, the turning and the door meet, never stored in the key. Inside and outside: two cuts in one appearing, not two sealed boxes. Then what Section 2 has shown, the two traps on either side (too much: "it is vivid, so it really exists"; too little: "it is not found, so it is nothing"), and recognition rather than search, ending in wonder.
