## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 7: Dōgen: The Shore and the Boat (AA47, 9:17)

Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 7: Dōgen: The Shore and the Boat | Thusness

### Description

You sit in a boat, and the shore slides past. Is the shore moving? Or the boat? Or is that the wrong question?

The first two of four images from the Zen master Dōgen (1200–1253), each with its own taste of total exertion. The shore that seems to move (Genjōkōan): the usual reading takes the boat as the self that sets itself up as the still centre. Here, "it is really the boat" is still the habit: the motion has only changed owners. Motion is always relative; the seer is in the seen and the seen in the seer (MMK 3); no mover apart from the moving (MMK 2). The taste: no seat outside. Rowing the boat (Zenki, "Total Dynamic Working"): "Although you row, the boat gives you a ride, and without the boat no one could ride. But you ride in the boat and your riding makes the boat what it is" (tr. Kazuaki Tanahashi). Rower and boat designated together in one act (MMK 8.12), with Dōgen's own guard: the boat's moment is not the same as a moment that is not the boat. The taste: no doer. Where I part from the reading of one universal life. Then intimacy beyond relationship: never two to bring together, and not fusion.
