### Title

Rainbow Body: If Nothing Is Found, What Turns Into Light? | After Anatta

### Description

In eastern Tibet, the story goes, a master dies, and over a week his body grows smaller until only the hair and nails are left. Yet the same tradition says nothing is found from its own side: not the self, not the body, not even the mind. So what turns into light?

Dzogchen and the Gelug school of Tsongkhapa both hold a view that leaves nothing standing on its own, and both say practice can change the body. The answer: the body is not a lump that stands by itself. It is held together by grasping, through the wind grasping rides on. When grasping ends at its subtlest level, the form it held is freed as well.

Dzogchen: the five lights, the body as light not recognised, cutting through and direct crossing, the four visions, and why the rainbow body is in essence a realization, not the body disappearing. Gelug: mind and wind as one, the dissolution at death, the illusory body and union, and tukdam. The five lights and the five aggregates, read through holding an apple: the knot that ties the appearings into a solid thing. Read with dependency: no ground, nothing produced. And outside Buddhism: the Taoist yang spirit, Lü Dongbin and the "corpse-guarding ghost", and why only the ending of the taints frees.
