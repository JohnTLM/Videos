## Three rules for every part

1. **One home per idea and per example.** Each idea is explained once, in the part listed below. A later part may
   recall it in one sentence ("the chariot of Part 3"), never explain it again.
2. **No gap.** Every term is explained where it first appears. Each part ends on the question the next part answers,
   and opens by naming that question.
3. **The corrected wording throughout.** No "despite", "yet it still", "not found, yet it carries". Say it in the
   order of experience: it works; the name is drawn from the working; looked for, nothing is found behind it, and
   nothing is needed. Never "because empty, it works".

## The eight parts

Each about 12 to 15 minutes, in Andrew's voice, title card "Dependent Arising · Part N of 8".

### Part 1. If You Think You Understand

*The puzzle.*

- Dependent arising sounds simple, even safe. It is not.
- The apple tossed from hand to hand: where exactly is the solid apple? Looked for, it is not found, and it works
  perfectly in the hand.
- The mountain, and the bat, the bird, the surveyor: not four views of one ready-made mountain. When no one looks,
  is it there? Physics points the same way, but the view does not rest on it.
- The "undeniable" is a habit, not a fact: so familiar it goes unexamined.
- The two words as the tradition defines them: dependence and designation, one fact seen from two sides. Long and
  short; subject and object.
- **Ends on:** if the apple is not found, and it works, how does it work? The usual answer, "no essence, yet it
  works", is a trap. First, what dependent arising actually says.

*From:* AA23 (apple, mountain, definitions), AA31 §1 (the undeniable, physics).

### Part 2. What It Says: Conditions, With No Doer

*What dependent arising says, and its five mishearings.*

- Five mishearings: a web, a machine, only concepts, a lower truth, the shape of ignorance.
- What it says: these conditions, met together, and this appears. A bell struck; a match in slow motion.
- Causal efficacy without causal power: conditions work, and no power sits in any of them. Three senses of cause.
- Continuity with no carrier: the lamp through the night (MMK 18.10). Nothing lost, nothing fixed.
- Meeting with no glue: the hand and the ice, defining each other in one stroke (MMK 14.8).
- It leans neither to mind nor to matter.
- **Ends on:** if no doer stands behind the conditions, what then is the thing we name: the chariot, the apple, the
  self?

*From:* AA24 (mishearings, causal efficacy, mind and matter), AA21, AA31 §§4–6 (cause, continuity, meeting).

### Part 3. Designation: The Fist, the Wind and the Chariot

*What a name does.*

- Anatta found no seer behind the seeing. It usually sees through one side only; the seen remains. The same seeing
  turned to the seen: the emptiness of self and of phenomena.
- Designation does not need words. The name is drawn from the working, not added to it: carrying, then "chariot";
  blowing, then "wind". The blowing is the wind, completely.
- Nāgasena's chariot; Candrakīrti's sevenfold search. The basis is designated too.
- You already live this logic: the fist, the lap, the wave, the traffic jam. The chariot, the body and the self are
  the same, only more stable.
- An essence would stop the working: the Abhidharma's dharmas and MMK 15. A thing with its own nature could not be
  built, move or meet anything. It works, and what works is found empty.
- Not only a name, and not only in the mind.
- **Ends on:** and still the mind asks, "but then what is it that carries?"

*From:* AA24 (anatta to dependent arising, designation, chariot, dharmas), AA32 §§2–5.

### Part 4. The "It"

*The question behind every question.*

- "No essence, yet it still carries": the sentence keeps the "it" and empties it. A ghost that acts. "Despite",
  "yet", "still" are the trouble, not the insight.
- "Then what carries?" asks for a carrier behind the carrying. The "it" is in the question before any answer.
- "What is it?" is invalid without a substrate: not hidden, not nothing, not beyond words.
- So the eight extremes do not apply, all at once: arising or ceasing, permanent or annihilated, coming or going,
  same or different. Not a middle answer between them.
- Even "how does it form?" slips the "it" in. The "it" is supplied, a grammatical ghost, the same habit as the self.
  The "it" is what is formed, not what forms.
- Nietzsche: the lightning and its flash; where I part from him.
- The breeze check: what would a wind add? The cup: drop the "it", not the cup.
- As a practitioner, in my journey and journals, I found the "despite" shape a major disservice; many teachers and
  writers fall into it, and so did I. How I say it now.
- **Ends on:** with the "it" gone, what is left? Is there anything to say at all?

*From:* AA32 §§1, 6–8, AA31 §3 (the five steps).

### Part 5. Formation: Negation Is Only Half

*What appears, when nothing stands from its own side.*

- Two halves: negation finds nothing from its own side; formation shows how a world appears vivid, stable and
  working, with no essence anywhere.
- Every appearance is three things at once: vivid and clear; not found, not even "nowhere"; arising with its
  conditions and validly functioning.
- Empty does not mean weak: the car injures, medicine heals, a cup holds tea, and no essence is found in any of them.
- The apple again, with candlelight and dusk: hardness, coolness and red arise with their conditions; nothing missing.
- Mind and matter: two cuts in one appearing; neither is the floor.
- Two pictures creep back: the conditions as a floor underneath; the web of relations. Neither is found either.
- Relentless negation: why negating alone becomes a disease.
- **Ends on:** so what is all this for? And does seeing it mean falling silent?

*From:* AA31 §§3 (three features, apple, mind and matter), AA24 (two pictures), *Nothing Left to Negate*.

### Part 6. What It Is For: Freedom in Conventions

*Two ways of knowing, and no leap into silence.*

- Two ways of knowing rightly: through concepts within the framework, and freedom from all elaborations. Neither
  cancels the other.
- The framework held three ways. Two frameworks kept apart: the default cutting (parts and whole, before and after),
  and the inherent habit. Dependent arising lives in the first and unbinds the second.
- Within it, dependent arising does three kinds of work: it explains, it dismantles, it describes the taste.
- Ignorance: dependent arising is not born of it; as a view, it is the antidote. Candrakīrti on the "veil".
- Freedom from elaborations is not the end of speech. Many say the eight extremes do not apply, then leap into
  spontaneous presence as silence. Silence keeps the "it", only out of reach. Fair to Dzogchen, read with Mipham.
- Freedom in conventions: milk, curds, butter (DN 9); "I speak" (SN 1.25); Tsongkhapa, appearance and emptiness never
  apart; Dōgen's painted rice cake, which feeds.
- **Ends on:** all this is view. How is it actually met?

*From:* AA25, AA21, AA31 §7.

### Part 7. The Taste

*How it is met, in my own journey.*

- The taste was met three times: right after anatta; when every hinge was exhausted; in the marketplace.
- After anatta: seeing is just colours; no one doing the seeing. A first taste of spontaneous presence. How it differs
  from a refined non-dual state: no "made of". Total exertion (Dōgen).
- A taste is not yet freedom. Years with the taste, and the view still treating each thing as standing alone.
- Three strands, working together again and again: confirm in experience, equip the view with tools, taste the
  loosening. The tools meet each hinge: parts, whole, energy, field, relations, substrate. When all are exhausted, the
  mind is free from elaborations, and the same taste is met again, stable.
- The marketplace: dirtying my hands. Who decides, if no one is there? Why help, if others are not found?
- **Ends on:** one taste. How many faces does it have?

*From:* AA26, AA22, AA31 §2 (three strands).

### Part 8. One Taste, Many Faces

*The map, the guards, and an open hand.*

- Three descriptions, one taste: anatta (no one behind it), dependent arising (nothing in it stands alone),
  spontaneous presence (nothing missing, nothing making it). None higher.
- Six ways it is tasted: vividly appearing and unfindable; centerless; mutual; seamless; effortless; intimate.
- Many faces: recognising the same shape in a death, a thought, a flash of irritation; how recognition can misfire.
- Every mistake on the path has one shape: the doer put back somewhere else, a vast Awareness, a helper, a state to
  keep. The guards.
- The masters, and where I part.
- Try it: the hearer behind the hearing; the conditions; change one; look for the "between".
- **Ends:** with the open hand. No fist was ever hiding in it.

*From:* AA27, AA22, AA32 (the closing image).
