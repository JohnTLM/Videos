## 1. What the Logic Is Looking At (AA12, about 14:30)

### Title

What the Logic Is Looking At: Dharma, Blue and Red, and the Side of Light | After Anatta

### Description

Listen to a sound nearby: a car, a bird, the hum of a fridge. It is vivid and clear, and it needs no help to be known. Every step of the logic looks at something: a self, an object, a cause, matter, a person. They are not many topics. Each time, the logic is looking at a phenomenon, what Buddhism calls a dharma.

What a phenomenon is, conditioned or unconditioned, and how Nāgārjuna turns the search on the dharmas themselves (MMK 7.33). Why there is no bare appearance under the names: designation needs no words, only singling out, and singling out is already dependence. A world of only blue, where no one would ever see blue; then a spot of red, and at once both appear, in one stroke. Hold an apple, sense by sense, and see the knot the habit ties. Then the same knot told from the side of light: the five aggregates, the five lights that harden into a solid world when grasped, and, untied, display. Last, the clarity that is the appearing's own, as empty as everything else, and where the taste and the logic meet.
