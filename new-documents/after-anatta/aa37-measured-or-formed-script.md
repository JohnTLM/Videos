# Dependent Arising Part 3: Measured, or Formed?

A detector clicks. Up. Was the electron up all along?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

In Part 2, one bowl of liquid was seen as water, pus, a home and nectar. And no neutral liquid was found underneath. Beings still agree. Not because a hidden floor holds them together, but because they meet along shared channels, and correct one another.

A careful listener may say: that is fine for ghosts and fish. Physics is different. Physics measures the world as it is, from no one's side. Surely there, at least, the given is solid.

So in this part, I turn to physics.

I am not a physicist. I report two views as fairly as I can. Carlo Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics. And Karen Barad's agential realism.

I use them as echoes, not proofs. Emptiness does not depend on physics. And physics does not need my approval.

But it is striking. Two careful thinkers, starting from the laboratory and not from meditation, arrive close to what Nāgārjuna saw.

The pattern is the same as before. Two ways of seeing, side by side, three times. Then: which holds?

First, a measured value.

Take one small particle, an electron. Measure one of its properties, say its spin along one direction. The detector clicks. Up.

Surely the electron was up all along. The measurement only revealed it. The value sat there, finished, from the electron's own side. And the detector read it off, the way I read the time from a clock.

Or, and it is a big or, look at how the value is formed.

Quantum mechanics has long resisted that picture. Before the measurement, the theory does not give the electron a definite value at all. Only chances.

Rovelli draws a plain conclusion. A physical value is not a property a thing carries on its own. It is a fact that holds relative to another system, in an interaction.

Up is a fact for the detector that met the electron. For a system that has not interacted with either of them, it is not yet a fact at all.

So the value is not stored in the electron, waiting. It forms in the meeting. Rovelli says it plainly. The world is not made of things with their own properties. It is made of events of interaction.

That is the same move as the apple in Part 1. The red was not stored in the fruit. It arose with light, eye and attention. Here, even the most exact measurement is a meeting. And what is measured is what forms in that meeting.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. The click is real. The record stays. And the experiment can be repeated tomorrow, with the same result.

Second, two observers compare notes.

I measure the electron, and get up. Later you check, and you get up too. We compare notes, and they match.

Surely that settles it. If the value were only relative, why would we agree? We agree because the electron really was up, from its own side, before either of us looked. Agreement proves the given.

Or, and again it is a big or, look at how the agreement is formed.

Critics put exactly this question to Rovelli. His answer, worked out with the philosopher Emily Adlam, is a rule they call cross-perspective links.

If I have measured the electron, and you then interact both with the electron and with me, with my record, my notes, my words, then your result will match my record.

Agreement is real, and reliable. But it is formed through the meeting between you, me and the electron. It does not need a hidden value that existed before anyone met it.

This is Candrakīrti's answer from Part 2, met again in a laboratory. There, healthy human eyes agreed that the conch is white, and corrected the jaundiced eye, with no judge standing outside every view.

Here, observers agree by interacting. And errors are caught by checking again. Shared reality is built by coordination and cross-checking. Not by standing on a floor.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. Scientists still agree. Papers are still checked. Bridges are still built from the numbers.

Third, a stick in a dark room.

Barad starts from Niels Bohr, one of the founders of quantum mechanics, and an image Bohr liked.

Picture walking through a dark room with a stick. If I grip it firmly, the stick becomes part of me. I do not feel the stick. I feel the wall at its tip.

If I hold it loosely, the stick becomes something I feel. Its weight. Its roughness in my hand.

Surely there is a fixed line between me, the one who observes, and the stick and the wall, the things observed. The line is simply there. I only find it.

Or, and it is the biggest or, look at how the line is formed.

With the same stick and the same hand, the line falls in a different place, depending on how I hold it.

Barad calls this the agential cut. A measuring setup, she says, does not slice up a world already divided into observer and observed. It enacts the cut.

It settles, for this meeting, what counts as the instrument, and what counts as the object. And what a word like position can even mean here. Change the setup, and the cut moves.

Bohr showed that an experiment set up to find which path a particle takes cannot also show the interference that needs both paths. These are not two windows onto one finished particle. They are two different phenomena, each with its own cut.

So Barad says that things do not exist first, and then interact. They emerge through what she calls intra-action. Action within one phenomenon, not between things already there.

That is close to the bat, the bee and the ant in Part 1. A different body singles out a different world. The mountain, the one who meets it, and the channel of meeting are designated together. Barad's cut is that togetherness, seen in the laboratory.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. I still find my way across the dark room. And I still do not walk into the wall.

Now, where I part from them.

Here I must be careful. Because this is where the echo can turn into a new floor.

It is easy to read both views this way. Things are not real, but relations are. Interactions are the true stuff of the world. Phenomena are the real building blocks.

Whatever the authors themselves intend, that reading only moves the floor. Before, solid things sat underneath. Now, solid relations do.

Nāgārjuna does not allow that. A relation needs what it relates. Between needs two sides. Take away the electron and the detector, and no interaction is left standing on its own.

Relations and the things related arise together. Neither comes first.

So the interaction is as unfindable as the electron. And the cut is as unfindable as the two sides it divides.

What I meet is not a web of real relations under the appearances. It is the appearances themselves. Vivid, working, and empty all the way through. The relations included.

Rovelli himself has written about reading Nāgārjuna, and finding in him a way to think about a world with no ultimate things. I am glad of the meeting.

But I do not need physics to be right for dependent arising to hold. And I do not need dependent arising to settle what physics will say next. Scientific models are designations too. Among the most reliable ones I have, inside their own fields.

Two ways of seeing, three times over, now in the laboratory. Which carries more valid truth?

I test them as before. By experience. By logic. And by how things work.

By experience. What does any physicist actually meet? A click. A trace on a screen. A number on a display. A colleague's report.

Never a value standing on its own, before any meeting. That value is never met. It is supplied. By the same habit that supplies the apple standing on its own in the bowl.

By logic. If the electron's value were fixed from its own side, it would be the same for every system, met or not. And the cut would not move with the setup. Both fail.

And a relation put forward as the new floor fails too. Picking it out needs the things it relates. And they need it. Nothing in the chain stands alone.

By working. The given view says physics works because a finished world sits underneath, and measurement reads it.

The formed view explains the working fully. Measurements repeat. Observers agree. Predictions hold. Because these are patterns meeting other patterns, reliably, along their channels.

Working never needed a floor. Not in the bowl. And not in the laboratory.

So the given is not the truer view, even in physics. It is the default view, carried by habit, and by the way we talk.

What fails is only the extra claim: a value, a thing, or a relation, standing there from its own side.

Nothing that works is lost.

This is Part 3 of the Dependent Arising series.

In Part 4, I draw some conclusions. Without essence, does everything turn ghostly and void? The car still knocks you down. It still rains. And you still get sick.
