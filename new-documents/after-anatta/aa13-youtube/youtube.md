## Title

The Limits of the Tools: Why Words Can't Hold What Vividly Appears | Nāgārjuna & Dzogchen

## Description

Listen to rain on a roof. Every drop is different, and the sound never holds still. Now try to say exactly what you heard: the words come out flat.

Concepts, words and diagrams were made for daily life, and they do that job well. But turned on what actually appears, they are clumsy, and worse, they quietly show something else, shaped like the tool. This video looks at three features of appearing that the conceptual framework cannot hold: it is vivid and moving, it is not found when looked for, and it follows its conditions without being produced by them.

Along the way: why there is no raw experience under the concepts; the three senses of "cause" and why only one is refused (Nāgārjuna, MMK 1); why "beyond cause and effect" and spontaneous presence in Dzogchen never mean an uncaused source; how something so clearly present is not found here, there, in my mind or yours, and not even "nowhere"; why drawing dependence turns it into a web of relations, and why the line is as unfindable as the dot (MMK 14.8); and how to use the tools anyway: say what they cannot do, show dependence by change rather than maps, draw and then undo the drawing.

It ends with a verse by John Tan:
The more you taste the appearances,
the more you see the nature of appearances.
The more you see and understand the lack and limitations of the tools,
the clearer the mind becomes,
and the opening turns effortless and relaxed.
