## 1. The World Itself (AA10, 7:44)

### Title

The World Itself: A Worldview With No Ground Floor | After Anatta · Nāgārjuna's Middle Way

### Description

Watch a whirlpool in a river. It has a shape you could sketch and a place you could point to, yet not one drop of its water stays for more than a moment. Ask what the whirlpool is apart from the water, and nothing is found. Ask whether it is therefore unreal, and the leaves go on spinning.

This video asks the same of the whole world. Every picture of the world seems to need a bottom layer: atoms or energy, a universal mind, an empty space, a field or web of relations. Each candidate is put through the same test, with Nāgārjuna on space, Candrakīrti on outer objects and the mind, and Tsongkhapa on what is never established by its own characteristic. Then Dharmakīrti's momentary particulars and Candrakīrti's pot, why the world is still orderly, and why "why is there anything at all?" stops applying. Not a final picture of reality, but freedom from needing one.

## 2. Ethics and Compassion (AA11, 7:34)

### Title

Ethics and Compassion: Why Emptiness Makes Care Matter More | After Anatta · Nāgārjuna's Middle Way

### Description

A harsh word at breakfast can sour a whole day. A kind one can carry someone through it. What happens to that when no self is found behind the words? If nothing exists by itself, why not simply shrug?

This video shows why that does not follow. The substance reading says an act matters only if there is a real doer, a real victim and a deed that lasts; its mirror image says that if everything is empty, nothing matters. Both assume that mattering needs a solid core. Nāgārjuna begins his chapter on action with kindness, turns the worry around, and ends his whole work with compassion. Then the four questions: doer and deed designated together, karma bearing fruit with nothing carried across, and why a person fixed in their nature could never learn or change. Emptiness does not produce care, and it does not cause anything; it removes the excuses. The video also keeps open what the author has left open: how compassion relates to awakening.
