### Description

When did it start? Where did it go? Is it the same one? Each question asks where a thing is.

From Section 4, The Logic of Dependency. Nagarjuna's eight extremes, arising and ceasing, permanence and annihilation, coming and going, identity and difference, are eight ways of asking where a thing is. The logic of dependency does not answer them in a subtler way: when nothing with whereabouts is found, the questions lose their object, like a lamp whose light went nowhere and came from nowhere. Dependence and unfindability are one fact: not emptiness in one hand and dependent arising in the other, but one hand. The web of relations is not found either. Identity moves from essence to criteria (a cracked phone, a ship of new planks, an old photograph), and conventional truth is not "merely" conventional: with Tsongkhapa's three tests, it is where truth lives. One appearance, seen two ways.
