## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 9: Dōgen: The Fish and the Bird (AA49, 11:17)

### Description

A fish swims, and the water has no end. Must it reach the end of the water, to know the water?

Dōgen's image of place, the last of four images of total exertion. The fish and the bird (Genjōkōan): however far they go, the water and the sky have no end, yet neither has ever left its element; each covers its whole range completely. From a boat, the ocean looks round only as far as the eye reaches: no one meets the whole as a sum. Fish and water are designated together; the swimming is complete wherever it is. The taste: no elsewhere, and the end of external conditions. Where I part: the water is not a ground or a medium. Then Baoche's fan: if the wind is everywhere, why fan? The wind is this fanning. Then the four images together, the tastes that run through them, what total exertion does not mean (no Whole, no substance, no cosmos packed inside, not islands, not a medium, not the parts as the real stuff), and the taste that cannot yet explain itself, until the chariot gives it its grammar.
