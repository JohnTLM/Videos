The chain ends when ignorance ends. But what was ignorance? Not a thing to be destroyed. A way of taking things.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Part 1 set out how Nah-gar-joo-nah reads when this is, that is. A pattern holding, with nothing handed over. Part 2 followed the twelve links through his chapter on them. Karma and rebirth kept, and only the owner left out.

Section 5 took up, in the tradition's own terms, where the chain is cut. Here I take up the same ending, as Nah-gar-joo-nah reads it. This part closes Section 6.

When ignorance has ceased, formations do not arise. And ignorance ceases through cultivating this very knowledge. By the ceasing of each link, the next does not arise.

So far, this is the tradition's own teaching. The difference is in what is ending.

If the links were real events, each with its own nature, the reverse order would mean stopping a chain of real things, as one stops a machine. And it could not be done. A link with its own nature could not be removed.

For Nah-gar-joo-nah, nothing real has to be destroyed. When this is not, that is not, is the same pattern, seen from its other side. The links were never handed over from one to the next. They held together as a pattern while ignorance was present. When ignorance is absent, the pattern does not hold.

Nothing is broken, because nothing was ever standing on its own to be broken.

What is the knowledge that ends ignorance? Chun-drah-keer-tee reads it as seeing dependent arising as it really is. Which is seeing its emptiness.

The tradition sees things arising and passing, and lets go of a self among them. Nah-gar-joo-nah does not set that aside. He takes it one step further. He asks about the arising and passing itself. Is arising found, when it is looked for?

So the seeing that ends ignorance is not only, this arises and passes, and is not mine. It is, this was never found to arise, and never found to pass, as a thing on its own. That is why he praises dependent arising as without arising, and without ceasing.

Nah-gar-joo-nah also gives the order in which the chain takes hold. Action and affliction come from conceptual construction. And these come from elaboration. Elaboration stops in emptiness.

Elaboration is the mind's spinning. It is. It is not. It arises. It ceases. Mine. Not mine. It is the same habit as asking of anything what it really is, and needing a something to be there.

Notice where elaboration stops. Not in silence. Not in a blank. In emptiness. In seeing that what elaboration keeps fixing was never fixed.

A quiet mind can rest from elaborating for a while, and then begin again, because nothing has been seen. When emptiness is seen, elaboration has nothing to stand on. Not because thought is stopped. Because what thought was building on is found not to be there.

Here I part from one way of hearing the teaching. Some say that dependent arising begins with ignorance, since the twelve links do. On that reading, dependent arising is simply the pattern of confusion, something to get out of.

That reading needs refining. The twelve links, in forward order, describe how suffering runs while ignorance is present. But dependent arising, as a view, is not the shape of ignorance. It is the antidote to it.

Otherwise its praise would make no sense. Nah-gar-joo-nah bows to the Buddha for teaching it, as the quieting of elaborations, peace. He says dependent arising, emptiness and the middle way have one meaning. And it is said that whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dhahm-mah.

So the same sentence does two things. Heard as production among real things, when this is, that is, describes the trap. Seen as Nah-gar-joo-nah reads it, it opens the way out.

The tradition calls the ending nir-vah-nah, the ending of becoming. It is easy to picture this as a place. The round of birth and death here, nir-vah-nah there, and a journey from one to the other.

Nah-gar-joo-nah does not let that picture stand. Nothing at all sets the round apart from nir-vah-nah. And between their limits, not even the finest gap is found.

They are not two places, one to leave and one to reach. To picture the ending of suffering as the destruction of something real is itself the inherent view. Something can be cut off only if it was there from its own side first.

What ends was never a thing. It was a way of taking things. Appearances do not end. The bell still rings when struck. The seed still gives rice. What ends is taking them to stand on their own.

One caution, and Nah-gar-joo-nah gives it himself. Emptiness was taught as the way out of all views. But those for whom emptiness becomes a view, he calls incurable. Emptiness wrongly seen ruins, like a snake grasped the wrong way.

If emptiness becomes a new thing, a void behind appearances, a state to reach, a view to defend, then elaboration has simply found a new object.

And seen rightly, emptiness takes nothing away. For whom emptiness is possible, everything is possible. It is because nothing has a nature of its own that the links work, that karma holds, that practice changes a person, and that the chain can end.

Return once more to the harsh word at work. In Section 5, the chain was watched instead of obeyed. The first arrow landed, and the second did not follow.

Seen as Nah-gar-joo-nah reads it, there is something further. The word, the hearing, the feeling and the one who is hurt are found only together. The hurt is vivid, and it follows its conditions exactly. But it was never a thing that arrived from outside. And never a thing that will be carried away.

The afternoon goes on. The words were heard, the feeling felt, a reply perhaps given. Nothing is taken from it.

The tradition and Nah-gar-joo-nah agree on the chain, on karma, on rebirth with no traveller, and on the path. Where they part is in what the pieces are. For the tradition, the person is empty. For Nah-gar-joo-nah, everything is.

This is Section 6, Part 3 of the Dependent Arising series, and the close of Section 6.

There was nothing to cut, because nothing was ever tied on its own.
