# What It Says: Conditions, With No Doer

In Part 1, the apple was not found behind its appearings, and it was caught all the same. How does anything work, with nothing found behind it? Start with what dependent arising actually says.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is Part 2 of 7.

Dependent arising is easy to agree with, and easy to mishear. Most people already accept that things depend on other things. Then they hear one of five things.

First, a web. Things are unfindable, but surely the connections are there. A network, a field of relations, the floor at last.

Second, a machine. Causes working in the dark, each pushing the next.

Third, only concepts. If it is designated, it is a story, and causes are not really there.

Fourth, a lower truth. Fine for the world, to be left behind when one sees the ultimate.

Fifth, the shape of ignorance. The twelve links begin with ignorance, so dependent arising must be the pattern of confusion.

Each takes something true, and sets it on the wrong foot. I will take them in turn, across this series. The machine first.

What does dependent arising say? A bell rings when it is struck. A seed of rice gives rice. A harsh word, met with a certain mood, brings anger. The order is reliable. Actions have results.

It says: these conditions, met together, and this appears.

What it never says is that a cause, standing on its own, makes an effect and hands it across. The ringing is not made by the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear, and then sent out. It is their meeting. Nothing passes from the strike to the sound. Nothing stands behind the sound, producing it. And without the strike, there is no ringing.

Strike a match, and slow the film down as far as you like. At every frame, something is happening. At no frame does a finished flame cross over from nowhere. And still, she lit the fire at eight is perfectly valid.

This is what I call causal efficacy without causal power. Conditions condition, fully. And no power is ever found sitting in a cause.

So I keep three senses of cause apart. A producer with its own power: refused. Conditions coming together: kept. A link that attention singles out and names: kept too. Conditions are not a basement under the appearing. They are the appearing, cut into parts for explaining, and for getting things done.

Two pictures tend to creep back here.

The first puts the conditions underneath, as parts lined up below that then produce the appearing on top. But the cook, the chemist and the physicist cut the same loaf differently. Each cut works within its own setting. None is the loaf from its own side.

The second keeps the relations when the things are gone. A web, a network, a field. That is the first mishearing again. But the line is as unfindable as the dot. Relation and what it relates are designated together. Neither comes first. Indra's net is a fine image, so long as the net itself is not taken as what is finally found.

So nothing stands alone is only half of it. The other half is that the relations are not found from their own side either.

Now the bell again, from the inside. When the producing cause falls away, one part is almost always left over. The resounding ting is consciousness, I want to say. It is my very own clarity.

That reading is not wrong. It is an important insight. No hearer stands apart from the ting. The ting is the hearing.

What tends to happen next is the problem. Many, almost without fail, then fold everything into consciousness. The producer is gone, the hearer is gone, and mind is left standing as what everything is. The bell, the air and the ear become contents of one clarity. That is the ground coming back, this time as mind.

Dependency is the key. The ting is there only with the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear. Being dependent, it is not mind from its own side, any more than it is bronze from its own side. Clarity is never absent from what appears. But lifted out, and made what everything is made of, it is one more thing.

So what appears leans neither to mind nor to matter. When matter is seen through, the habit does not give up. It changes its stuff. Matter traded for mind is still stuff. What appears is not made of anything.

The same holds across time. If nothing persists from its own side, what carries me from this moment to the next?

The question rests on a gap that thought drew first. In the living moment, it never comes up. And a carrier would never solve it anyway. Whatever links one moment to the carrier, and the carrier to the next, needs bridges of its own. Without end.

As Nāgārjuna says, whatever arises in dependence on something is neither that very thing, nor wholly other than it. Like a lamp burning through the night, continuity is named across moments that condition one another reliably. Nothing travels from one to the next.

Nothing is lost. Memory, learning, responsibility and karma hold. And nothing is fixed. Habits can loosen. Character can change.

And the same holds for meeting. If things had sealed edges of their own, nothing could ever meet. Two sealed things could never touch. And if they merged, they would collapse into one.

In dependent arising, meeting means something else. The two sides define each other in one stroke. A hand touches ice. Hand and ice meet, with no gap crossed, and no fusion. And the meeting itself is not a third thing, a glue between them.

So the same words can be heard two ways. Heard by the logic of substance, dependent arising means things that already exist, linked in a vast web, and emptiness means a blank void, or a hidden reservoir behind things. Meant by the logic of dependency, dependent arising means things identified only in dependence, and emptiness means simply this: no nature of its own is found, in appearings that are vivid and working.

So: the bell rings, with no power in the strike. The lamp burns through the night, with nothing carried. The hand meets the ice, with no glue.

And still something is left. If no doer stands behind the conditions, what is the thing we name? The chariot. The apple. The self. What does a name actually do?

That is Part 3: the fist, the wind and the chariot.
