### Description

If all this is empty, nothing arises and nothing ends. Nagarjuna put that objection in his own book. Then he answered it.

The close of the series: six objections to this view, each stated at its strongest, then answered on its own terms. This is nihilism: but it is the world of own natures that is frozen, not the empty one. Then it is all mind: matter stuff traded for mind stuff is still stuff. Then it is only concepts, and anything goes: designation works, and it can be wrong, as Tsongkhapa's three tests show. Dissolving the Hard Problem is not solving it: partly right, and what is left is not a mystery but a field of study. The physics is being stretched: I agree; physics gives echoes, not proofs. Then emptiness, or the web, is the new absolute: emptiness is not a thing behind things, and the relating is as empty as what it relates. And most of these objections arise in my own mind, each with a small relief, as if a floor had been found at last.
