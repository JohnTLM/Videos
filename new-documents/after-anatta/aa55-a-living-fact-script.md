# Dependent Arising Section 3 Part 3: A Living Fact

Divide mind from matter, and the gap can never be bridged. Reach for a cup, and no gap is ever crossed.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Part 1 set out the habit that there has to be something. Part 2 took it to the hard problem, and showed a gap made by two fixed natures. Here I want to set two pictures side by side. The gap we drew. And the living fact we never stopped living.

On paper, mind and matter cannot meet. In life, they never once fail to.

First, a useful division.

Dividing mind from matter is not foolish. It is one of the most useful cuts we make. The doctor asks whether a pain is in the knee, or in the head. The neurologist separates what a scan shows from what the patient reports. The law separates what you did from what you intended. In ordinary speech, I say I changed my mind, and I moved the table, and everyone understands.

Each of these works, within its own agreements. As a convention, the division is fine. It singles out two aspects of what appears, so that we can talk, treat, measure and judge.

The trouble begins when the convention is taken for two things.

Mind becomes an inner stuff, whose nature is to be felt. Matter becomes an outer stuff, whose nature is to lack feeling. Each is now complete on its own side. And then every ordinary event turns into a puzzle that cannot be solved.

How does a thought move an arm? How does light on the eye become a seen colour? How does coffee make me alert, or a sad memory make my chest heavy?

Asked between two fixed natures, each of these needs a bridge. And no bridge can be built. That is the gap of Part 2. It is unbridgeable. But only because we made the two banks first.

Now drop the picture, and look at what actually happens.

I want a drink. My hand reaches. My fingers close around the cup. I feel its warmth and weight. I lift it, and I drink.

At no point is there a moment when a thought has to travel over to a hand. Nothing is sent across. Wanting, seeing, reaching, feeling the weight. They arise together, each with the others, in one seamless movement.

It is the same everywhere I look.

A door slams behind me. The sound, the jump, the racing heart are one event. I do not first hear, then decide to be startled, then send the order to my body.

I walk over uneven ground, talking all the while. The feet find the stones without any instructions from me.

I drink coffee, and the dullness lifts. A sad memory comes, and the chest grows heavy. The mental and the physical are never found waiting on opposite banks.

And it is effortless. No one has ever had to work at joining their mind to their body. We have never once felt a bridge being crossed. The interaction is simply how living goes. It is a living fact. Not a theory.

So the order is the other way round from how we usually think. The seamless interaction comes first, as lived. The gap comes afterwards, as a picture laid over it.

Why is it seamless? Not because mind and matter are secretly one stuff. Because neither has a nature of its own.

Almost every way of thinking about relation tries to settle it by collapsing one side into the other. Reductionism collapses the whole into its parts. Holism collapses the parts into a Whole. Materialism collapses mind into matter. Idealism collapses matter into mind. And a ground, energy, a field, or awareness, collapses both into a third stuff.

Each one needs one side to be really real, so that the relation has somewhere to rest.

Dependent arising and emptiness give another way of understanding relation, connection and interaction. Without the need to resolve one into the other.

The intending and the moving. The light and the seen colour. The key and the opening door. Each arises with the other, and neither is the floor. Nothing has to be reduced, because nothing had a nature to be reduced to.

And the relation is not a third thing either. Relation is what is really real, would only be one more ground. The relating is as empty as what it relates.

Seamless does not mean merged. Sound stays sound, and colour stays colour. The warmth of the cup is not the wanting. Distinctions stay perfectly vivid. What goes is only their standing apart, each on its own side, needing to be joined.

In a world without a substrate, this is understood in a way very close to Mipham's two models of the two truths.

Mipham, the great Nyingma scholar of the nineteenth century, presented the two truths in two ways.

In the first, the conventional truth is appearance, and the ultimate truth is emptiness, and the two are inseparable. This is the Middle Way's account of how things are.

In the second, the two truths are told apart by whether experience accords with how things are. Experience that accords, non-dual, and without reification, is on the side of the ultimate. Experience that does not accord, dualistic and reifying, is on the side of the relative.

The substrateless view meets both.

Ontologically, the two truths are emptiness and dependent arising. Not two facts, but one. What arises dependently is empty. And being empty, it arises.

Experientially, it is a non-substantialist, non-dual experience, against a reified, dualistic taste of action and agency, of an object and its characteristics.

That reified taste is what Part 1 called the habit. A flash happens, and the habit supplies the lightning that flashes. A reaching happens, and the habit supplies a mind over here that sends orders to a hand over there. When the habit is seen through, the same reaching is tasted as it is lived. Seamless. Without effort. No one standing behind it. Nothing crossing over.

One caution, and here I am careful. Some readers worry that the second model can make the authentic experience sound like a positive ground. A pure awareness that is finally, really there. I do not take it that way. The non-dual taste is itself dependently arisen, and empty. It is not a new floor, laid where the old one was taken away. Otherwise the experiential side slides straight back into a presence standing behind things, and the whole problem returns.

This can be noticed in any ordinary moment.

Pick up a cup. Look for the point where the wanting hands over to the hand. There is no such point.

When startled, notice how sound, jump and heartbeat come as one.

And when the thought comes, my mind is moving my body, see that it comes afterwards. As one more thought. A picture of the event. Not the event.

The convention stays useful. The doctor still asks where it hurts. What drops away is only the belief that the convention is two things, needing a bridge.

This is Section 3, Part 3 of the Dependent Arising series, and the close of Section 3.

On paper, the gap could never be bridged. In life, it was never there to cross.
