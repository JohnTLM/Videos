# Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 9: Dōgen: The Fish and the Bird

A fish swims, and the water has no end. Must it reach the end of the water, to know the water?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

In Part 7, the shore and the boat took away the seat outside the event, and the doer behind it. In Part 8, firewood and ash took away a past or a future, needed to complete it. One more way something could stand outside the event remains. A wider place. Here is only a small part of the world, and the rest lies further on. Surely the event is complete only when the rest is added.

Dōgen's image for this is the fish and the bird. And then, in this part, I bring the four images together.

First, the fish and the bird.

Near the end of the Genjōkōan, Dōgen writes: a fish swims in the water, and however far it swims, the water has no end. A bird flies in the sky, and however far it flies, the sky has no end. Yet neither has ever left its element.

When the need is large, the use is large. When it is small, the use is small. Each covers its whole range completely. A bird that left the sky would die at once. And so would a fish that left the water. Water is life, and sky is life.

A fish or a bird that tried to reach the end of the water or the sky before moving in it would find no way, and no place. When you find your place right here, practice happens.

Just before this, Dōgen notes that from a boat far out at sea, with no land in sight, the ocean looks round. But it is neither round nor square. To a fish it is a palace. To a heavenly being, a string of jewels. It looks round only as far as the eye can reach.

How is this usually read? It is a teaching on practice. You do not need to understand everything, or reach the end of the water, before you begin. Practise where you are, and the Way is realised there. The water and the sky are often read as the Way, or as buddha nature. And the fish and the bird as the practitioner who lives in it.

Here is how I see it.

The habit sees a fish inside a container of water. And a bird inside a box of sky. It wants to know where the edges are. And it thinks it must reach them, to know the whole.

Anatta takes away the seeker who stands apart from the swimming. There is just the swimming.

And dependent arising shows the rest. The fish is a fish only with the water. And the water is life for the fish only in the swimming. They are designated together. Wherever the fish is, the swimming is complete. It does not need the whole ocean added up to be whole. Large or small, it covers its range completely.

And Dōgen himself guards against the big reading. The ocean looks round only as far as the eye can reach. No one ever meets the whole as a sum. So completeness is here. Not in a grand total, somewhere else.

That is the taste of this image. No elsewhere. Being at home where I am. There is nowhere further to go to find what is missing, because nothing is missing.

And the conditions are not outside influences, pressing in from beyond. The water does not surround the fish as a foreign thing. It takes part as the swimming. The air does not surround the sound. It takes part as the sounding. This is what I call the end of external conditions.

And where I part. I keep the reading on practice. But I do not make the water a ground, such as buddha nature as a vast medium the fish swims in. The water is as dependent as the fish. Neither is the floor.

Second, the fan.

The Genjōkōan ends with a story. A Chan master was fanning himself. A monk asked: the nature of wind is permanent, and there is no place it does not reach. Why, then, do you use a fan?

The master said: you know only that the nature of wind is permanent. You do not yet know the meaning of its reaching everywhere. The monk asked what that meaning was. The master just went on fanning. And the monk bowed.

Dōgen comments: if you say that because the wind is everywhere there is no need to fan, you know neither permanence, nor the nature of wind.

How is this usually read? Buddha nature is everywhere, yet it must be realised in practice. Practice and realisation are not two.

Here is how I see it. The monk's mistake is the one this whole section has been taking apart. He takes the nature of wind as something already there, everywhere. A given, waiting, needing nothing. Then the fanning seems pointless.

But there is no wind waiting everywhere, behind the fanning. The wind is this fanning. The hand, the fan, the air, the cool on the face, all at once, complete. Reaching everywhere does not mean a substance spread through all places. It means that wherever it is realised, it is wholly there. As the fish's swimming is wholly there. The master does not explain. He fans.

Now the four images, together.

Each image takes away one way that something could stand outside the event, and complete it.

The shore takes away a seer outside. What is left is one moving, owned by no one.

The boat takes away a doer behind. What is left is the act, its conditions wholly at work.

Firewood and ash take away a past or a future to complete it. What is left is this position, complete.

The fish and the bird take away a wider place to complete it. What is left is here, the whole range covered.

Take away all four, and the event lacks nothing. No wider whole stands behind it, to complete it.

And each image is complete as its dependence. The moving arises between boat and shore. Rower and boat make each other. The ash comes from the wood. The fish lives by the water. That is why total exertion is not a row of islands, each sealed in its own completeness. Each event is whole. And its wholeness is its conditions, fully spent as it.

Some other tastes run through all four.

Seamless, without fusion. Boundaries lose their solidity. Yet distinctions never disappear. Sound never becomes colour. The shore never becomes the boat. Difference is, if anything, more vivid. But the invisible walls are gone.

Activity, without things behind it. Nouns turn into verbs. Rowing. Burning. Swimming. Fanning. And a trap waits here. The mind wants some subtle medium underneath the verbs. Awareness, or energy, or a universal process, flowing through everything. That is the substrate again, only liquid. The activity has nothing flowing under it.

Luminous. None of this is merely functional. It is vivid and clear. The clarity is the event appearing, not a light added to it.

Light, and trustworthy. Because nothing stands on its own, nothing has to hold itself up. And without any essence, things still work, reliably. The world holds together, without anything holding it.

What total exertion does not mean.

It is very easily heard in a bigger way. The whole cosmos is present in each moment. The entire universe rings in this bell. That sounds profound. But it quietly brings back a ground. If a cosmic Whole is packed inside each moment, or stands behind it as what it really is, then the Whole has become the floor, instead of the parts. That is holism. It changes which comes first. But it does not remove the floor.

So, to gather the guards. Not one Whole. Not one substance. No cosmos packed inside. Not islands. Not a line, or a web. Not a medium. And not the parts as the real stuff either. Neither the whole nor the parts are found standing on their own.

The rower stays a rower. The boat a boat. The sky the sky. Nothing has merged into one thing. There is just this stroke of the oar, fully exerted. And then the next.

A taste that cannot yet explain itself.

Total exertion perplexed me. Because it was complete as lived experience, while its meaning stayed unresolved. I knew with certainty that the world did not consist of independently existing things. Yet I could not say why. How can the rower be the rowing? How can nothing stand behind the event? Why does this not collapse into one big Whole?

The taste had changed. The grammar of thought had not. This is not a failure of practice. It is how this matures.

And the grammar did not come from deeper absorption. It came from seeing that the old chariot is not an analogy about a vehicle. It is an exact description of this very moment. Not the wheels. Not the axle. Not apart from them. And yet it carries its passenger. The rowing, the boat and the river are designated together in just that way. And so are the wood, the fire and the ash. And the fish, and the water.

So, which holds?

By experience. When I swim, or walk, or hear the bell, is the event met as a small piece of a larger world, lying further on? Is a Whole met behind it, or a cosmos met inside it? What is met is the activity, here, complete. With its conditions coming through as it, and nothing outside it.

By logic. If the fish were a fish apart from the water, it would not die when taken out. If the event were complete only when the whole world was added up, it would never be complete. Because no one ever meets the whole as a sum.

By working. Nothing works the less for this. The fish swims. The bird flies. The fan cools the face.

So separate things linked by conditions, or one great Whole showing itself in parts, is not the truer view. Both are the default, carried by habit, and by nouns.

None of this makes my everyday words false. The fish swims in the sea. I fan myself on a hot day. What fails is only a fish, a sea, a wind, or a cosmos, standing on its own.
