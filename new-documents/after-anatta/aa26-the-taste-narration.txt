This is Part 4 of 6: The taste.

So far I have looked at why dependent arising is stranger than it sounds, what it says, and what it is for. One of its uses is to describe a taste. This part is about that taste.

Dependent arising did not come to me first as a view. It came as a taste, and the view came to sync with it later.

Looked back on, the taste was met three times: right after anatta, again when every hinge was exhausted, and again in the marketplace.

Start with anatta. In anatta there is only seeing, no seer. Hearing, no hearer. The agent is seen through. It was never there from its own side.

But then: what are seeing and hearing? We say they are events, processes. That is true, but it is still words. So look, in real time.

Seeing is just colours. No effort at all. Hearing is just sound. No effort at all.

Nothing is doing the seeing, and the seeing is not being done. Even what we called a process does not seem to be a process at all. Nothing runs underneath the colours, carrying them from one moment to the next.

And the colours are not dark. They are clear, known and vivid. The knowing is right there in the colour, and nothing stands behind it, knowing. Clarity is not added to the taste. It is just not lifted out and made into a knower.

So here, right after anatta, is a first taste of spontaneous presence. Immediate, effortless and spontaneous. Remember this taste. I will come back to it.

How is this different from a refined non-dual state? A refined non-dual rests in oneness. It is still haunted by substance. It thinks of everything as made of one luminosity, one clarity that colours, sounds and thoughts are made of. The seer has merged, but the "one" it merged into is still a something.

That is what anatta changes. Oneness merges the seer into everything. Anatta finds no seer there to merge. With nothing merged, no "one" is left for everything to be made of.

In this taste there is no "made of". The colour is clear as colour. The sound is clear as sound. Each is complete with its own conditions: light and eye for the colour, air and ear for the sound. Six clarities, one for each stream: seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching and thinking. Not one lamp lighting them all.

So the test is simple. Is there a "one" that everything is made of? If so, substance is still there.

At the same time, the factors are intimate. The colour is there only with light, with the eye, with attention. The sound only with the air, the ear, and the car going by. None of these stands outside, feeding it in.

So three things are found together. Effortless: no one is doing it. Seamless: nothing strings it together from moment to moment. Intimate: the factors are not outside it. The alignment of these factors is the appearing.

Three cautions. "Just colours" does not leave an object standing out there; the colour too is singled out, in dependence. "Alignment" is not two steps, factors first and then a lining up. And "not a process" does not deny change. What is not found is a process as a thing, running underneath.

This taste does not stay in seeing and hearing. After anatta it widens, often by itself, before I had any view for it. I found myself living within it.

Each event seems to spend itself completely. The sound of a car going by is wholly the sound. Nothing is behind it, holding back. Boundaries lose their solidity, yet distinctions never disappear. The car is still the car, more vivid than before.

Doh-gen called this total exertion: each thing spending itself fully in its own place. Firewood abides in its own place, and ash in its own. Each is complete. No wider whole stands behind it.

Here I have to be careful. Total exertion is easily heard as "the whole cosmos is present in each moment". That is holism. It only makes the whole the ground instead of the parts. The event spends itself completely, with nothing held back, and no cosmos packed inside it.

It is not a second taste beside the first. It is the same taste, widening. In seeing and hearing I spoke of it as spontaneous presence: nothing making it. Spreading through each event, it is total exertion: nothing standing apart. Already two descriptions of one taste.

But a taste is not yet freedom.

The taste is effortless, and still the mind has not released its habit of reifying. Ignorance has not been unbound.

How can both be there at once? Because they are not in the same place. The taste is in the immediacy of the senses. The habit is in how the world is understood around that moment.

While I see a flower, the seeing is just colours, with no one behind it. Yet the moment I turn away, the flower is still assumed to be sitting there by itself, its red belonging to it. A cause is still felt to push its effect. Death is still the end of a someone. The self had dissolved, but the universe had not.

For years after anatta, the taste was there, and the view still treated each thing as standing by itself. That gap is what I call the desynchronisation. The experience was free of substance. The framework was still full of it.

The habit does not announce itself. It does not feel like a view. It feels like the world itself.

It is only when it is challenged by Nah-gar-joo-nah's verses that these habits come to the surface. That is what the logic is for. Not to give a better taste, but to bring the habit up, and then to unwind it, case by case.

The logic does not do this alone. The habit is deeper than thought. It is in perception itself, and even in the body. No argument unbinds that by being won.

The logic points to the place to look. Then I look, and check it against the taste. Is a whole found apart from its parts? Is a cause found pushing? Each time the answer is seen, not only agreed to, the habit loosens a little. Insight without the logic drifts back into nouns. Logic without the taste stays a clever argument about words.

Nah-gar-joo-nah makes the same move as anatta, again and again. No seer behind seeing. No goer behind going. No fire apart from its fuel. And in his first chapter, no producer behind arising. A self is the doer behind the doing. A causal power is the doer behind the causing. Neither is found.

The bridge for me was Nah-gah-say-nah's chariot. The chariot is not found apart from its parts, and not as the parts either. It is designated in dependence. In that, the taste of total exertion recognised itself. What I had tasted as "each event wholly this" is what the logic says as "nothing here stands from its own side".

And then the logic turns on itself. Dependence too is not found. The conditions are not a web, or a basement on which appearances are built. Conditions are the appearing.

Before that freedom, the mind will always hinge on something.

First it hinges on parts. A thing is made of its pieces. When the parts are seen through, it does not let go. It hinges on the whole. When the whole is seen through, it finds something finer to stand on. Qi, energy, a field, relations, a substrate. Each time, it is looking for a foundation.

The tools meet each hinge in turn. The chariot is not found among its parts, nor apart from them. Qi and energy are not a stuff under appearances. They are singled out and designated like everything else. A field is the whole again, in a softer form. Relations are not found either. And a substrate is the foundation the mind was looking for all along. It is not found.

When all of these are exhausted, neither parts nor whole, with nothing left to hinge on, the mind becomes free from all elaborations, and in a stable way. The habit itself has unwound.

Stable does not mean the habit can never stir again. When it stirs, it is met by the same seeing, and it does not take root as it did.

Then appearance is again tasted as vividly present, spontaneous and free. This is the first taste, revisited.

It is the same taste. What has changed is the understanding around it, and two things more. Reach: at first the taste lived in seeing and hearing. Now it covers thought, the body, things, causes, space, time and death. And stability: at first the habit took it back when I stopped looking. Now it no longer depends on looking.

Take away the doer from everything, and put nothing in its place. Not even dependence kept as a new thing. Then nothing is making the appearing. The sound of a car going by is not a product. It shows up whole and vivid, exactly as its conditions meet.

That is what spontaneous presence means to me. It is not uncaused. Every appearing has its conditions. And it is not a source. Nothing stands behind appearing, giving it off.

Mind and matter lose their weight, and become light, magical and plastic. Ignorance sees the coming together of conditions as production: something there first, making something else. But nothing is made, and nothing is handed on. And what appears is neither mind nor matter from its own side. Each word cuts it from one side.

After freedom from all elaborations, I re-enter the marketplace, and dirty my hands.

The marketplace is ordinary life. Work, family, money, arguments, other people's troubles, choices. The last of the Zen ox-herding pictures shows it: barefoot and bare-chested, smeared with mud and ashes, he comes into the market with helping hands, smiling broadly.

Why after freedom? Because one can only re-enter a place one has been free of. Freedom does not stay apart. It goes back to work.

Here dependent arising changes its role. Before, it was the tool that brought each hinge up and took it apart. Now I deal in causes and conditions all day: what helps, what harms, what follows from what. Dependent arising is how help works. And a freedom that holds only in quiet is not yet stable.

Who decides, if no one is there? Nobody disappears. I still act, choose, keep my promises and answer for what I do, as a conventional person. What is gone is the doer behind the doing, pulling the levers. Responsibility is answered first, and nothing in the taste cancels it. "No one is there, so nothing matters" is not freedom. It is the annihilation view.

And why help, if others are not found? Their suffering still functions, and it arises in dependence. Because it is not fixed, it can be eased. A suffering that stood by itself could not be touched.

So, looked back on, the taste has been met three times. First, anatta, around 1997: no seer, in seeing. Then the desynchronisation, and the work of syncing the view. Second, freedom from all elaborations, when every hinge is exhausted: the same taste, stable and wide. And third, dependent arising and emptiness, in the marketplace.

These are not stages of the taste. The logic does not produce the taste. It unbinds the habit. And it is not a ladder climbed once. It goes round more than once, wherever the habit of fixing returns. Each turn clarifies one seeing, rather than adding a floor.

All views and understanding here are my own, from my practice and contemplation. I do not represent any tradition, school or teacher.
