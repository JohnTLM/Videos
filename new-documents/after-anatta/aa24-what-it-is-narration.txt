This is Part 2 of 6: What it is.

In Part 1, I asked whether the apple and the mountain are as straightforward as they look. They are not. Dependent arising is at the centre of almost everything I have written. Now I set out the view itself, whole.

That matters, because dependent arising is easy to agree with, and easy to mishear. Most people already accept that things depend on other things. Then they hear one of five things.

First, a web. Things are unfindable, but surely the connections are there. A network, a field of relations, the floor at last.

Second, a machine. Causes working in the dark, each pushing the next.

Third, only concepts. If it is designated, it is a story, and causes are not really there.

Fourth, a lower truth. Fine for the world, to be left behind when one sees the ultimate.

Fifth, the shape of ignorance. The twelve links begin with ignorance, so dependent arising must be the pattern of confusion.

Each takes something true and sets it on the wrong foot.

So what does it say? A bell rings when it is struck. A seed of rice gives rice. A harsh word, met with a certain mood, brings anger. The order is reliable, and actions have results.

Dependent arising says: these conditions, met together, and this appears. Designated, and working.

What it never says is that a cause, standing on its own, makes an effect and hands it across. The ringing is not made by the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear, and then sent out. It is their meeting. Nothing passes from the strike to the sound. Nothing stands behind the sound, producing it. Yet without the strike, there is no ringing.

This is what I call causal efficacy without causal power. Conditions condition, fully. And no power is ever found sitting in a cause.

Conventionally, we say the strike produces the sound, and that is fine. The analysis does not take it away. It shows only that things do not function the way the inherent mind thinks they do.

But when the producing cause falls away, one part is almost always left over. The resounding ting is consciousness. It is my very radiance clarity.

This reading is fine. It is an important non-dual insight, and a recognition of anatta. No hearer stands apart from the ting. The ting is the hearing.

What I want to stress is what tends to happen next. Many, almost without fail, then fold everything into consciousness, much as Yoh-gah-chah-rah is commonly read. The producer is gone, the hearer is gone, and mind is left standing as what everything is. The bell, the air and the ear become contents of one clarity. That is the ground coming back, this time as mind.

Here dependency is the key. The ting is there only with the bronze, the strike, the air and the ear. Being dependent, it is not mind from its own side, any more than it is bronze from its own side. Clarity is never absent from what appears. But lifted out, and made what everything is made of, it is one more reification.

So a phenomenon leans neither to mind nor to matter. It is vivid, empty appearing. The ting, clear and resounding, arising with its conditions, and found nowhere when looked for.

When matter is seen through, the habit does not give up. It changes its stuff. Matter traded for mind is still stuff. A phenomenon is not made of anything. That is what mere convention means to me: appearing that works, with no stuff underneath it.

Dependent arising did not come to me as a theory. It came as the same seeing as anatta, turned outwards.

Anatta is the insight that there is no doer behind seeing. In seeing, only the seen. In hearing, only the heard. The person does not disappear. What falls away is one picture: someone standing behind the action, making it happen.

Nah-gar-joo-nah makes the same move everywhere. A goer does not go, and a non-goer does not go. There is no seer behind seeing, no fire standing apart from its fuel, and no producer behind arising.

Dependent arising is this: no doer behind anything. The wind is blowing, and there is no wind apart from the blowing. It is raining, and there is no "it" that rains.

When the witness is not found, experience does not go blank. The tree is more vivid, not less.

But anatta does not do everything. It removes the one who watches. It does not, by itself, remove the things watched. The observer has disappeared, but the world has not. Things, causes, time and space can still be held as solid.

So anatta and dependent arising are not two subjects. Anatta, met first, takes the doer out of seeing. Dependent arising takes it out of everything else: the cause, the object, the whole, the field.

Then designation. It is not a second teaching beside dependent arising. It is the same seeing, turned on the "this" itself.

Designation does not need words. It needs only singling out. This sound, this colour, this ache, picked out as "this". A "this" stands out only against what it is not, and only with these conditions. In a world of only blue, no one would see blue.

So the singling out is not done to an appearance that was there first, waiting to be named. It is the appearing. Because it is already dependence, it is already empty.

The clearest case is the oldest. Nah-gah-say-nah asks King Mih-lin-dah where his chariot is. It is not the pole, not the axle, not the wheels, not the frame. Not their sum, and not something apart from them.

Chun-drah-keer-tee makes the search complete, in seven ways. The chariot is not the same as its parts, and not other than them. It does not possess them, is not in them, and does not contain them. It is not their collection, and not their shape. Nothing is found.

Yet the chariot is not denied. It carries you to town. Walking is the chariot. Hearing is the chariot.

And the basis is designated too. The chariot is designated on its wheels. But a wheel is singled out in turn, on rim, spokes and hub. They on their wood, and the wood on its grain. No bottom layer is ever reached, where something finally stands by itself and receives the first name. There is nowhere for the search to end.

Now, the Buddhist tradition itself once set out to find that bottom layer. The Ah-bee-dar-mah, above all the Sar-vah-stee-vah-dah school, tried to list what is finally there. Tables, people and chariots, it said, are only designated. Take them apart, and they are gone.

What the analysis comes down to are the dharmas, the last units of experience. A moment of hardness, of heat, of sound, of feeling. Each is defined by its own mark, its svah-lahk-shah-nah. Earth by hardness. Water by cohesion. Fire by heat. Space by not obstructing.

In the Ah-bee-dar-mah's own gloss, a dharma is so called because it bears its own mark. And that mark is its own nature, its svah-bhah-vah, there from its own side, whether or not anyone designates it. So the chariot was designated, but its floor was not.

Nah-gar-joo-nah aims at exactly this definition. In chapter 5 of his Middle Way verses, he takes space, the hardest case, since some schools held it to be unconditioned. Can space be there before its mark? He answers: "No space at all is found before the defining characteristic of space." Space without its mark would be a thing with no mark, and no such thing is found anywhere.

Then the mark must apply to something. But it cannot apply to what has no mark, nor to what already has one, and there is nowhere else for it to apply. So mark and marked stand or fall together. "The characterised is not found, and the characteristic is not found." Neither comes first. Each is set up only with the other. Which is to say, designated.

Chapter 15 shows why it had to be so. Own nature, by definition, "is not made, and does not depend on anything else." But the dharmas were said to arise from causes and conditions. And "own nature that came about from causes and conditions would be something made."

A dharma with its own nature could not arise, could not change, and could not cease. "A nature becoming otherwise never makes sense." Grant the dharmas their own nature, and dependent arising, the Buddha's own teaching, stops working.

None of this denies that fire burns, or that earth is solid. Fire is still hot. The marks keep working, by convention. A doctor still reads a fever. A builder still tests a beam. What goes is their standing as the final building blocks. And Nah-gar-joo-nah does not put a better definition of a phenomenon in their place. He shows that any attempt to ground things in self-contained, uniquely marked units is reification.

This is where the apple comes back. The hardness in my palm looks like the last thing left, once the apple is seen through. Earth's own mark, found at last. But hardness is singled out on touch, skin and pressure. It stands out only against softness. It arises with the hand that meets it, and the knowing of it. It is not a mark that something carries from its own side.

For me, a phenomenon is just this. Vividly appearing, singled out in dependence, its mark and what it marks designated together, and nothing found that holds it there. The dharmas were meant to be the bottom layer. They are designated too.

Here is where the expression is hardest. The chariot is not found, and still we ride it to town. So what is a chariot that is merely designated? It is not only a name, and it is not only in the mind. Four points hold it.

First, designation and functioning arise together. We do not first have a name and then stick it on a heap. The chariot is called a chariot because it carries. That is why the name is not free. You cannot point at a heap of sand, call it a chariot, and ride it to town. A designation holds only where it works.

Second, the search removes only a chariot standing from its own side: a chariot inside the parts, or a power that makes them carry. That was never found, and the carrying never needed it. The wheels turn, the load moves, you arrive.

Third, it is not only in the mind. "Just a label in the mind" sounds like the humble answer. But it quietly sets up a mind that is found, standing behind and painting names onto a world. That mind is singled out too. No labeller stands behind it. So the chariot is no more made of mind than it is made of chariot stuff.

Fourth, the basis has no bottom layer, and functioning never needed one. Because nothing stands by itself, the chariot can be put together, carry you, break down and be mended.

In one line: being unfound removes the chariot that stands by itself, and leaves the chariot that carries you, whole.

Two pictures creep back, once the things are seen through.

The first puts the conditions underneath, as parts lined up below that then produce the appearing on top. But the conditions are the appearing, cut into parts by a framework for explaining and getting things done. The cook, the chemist and the physicist cut the same loaf differently. Each cut works within its own setting. None is the loaf from its own side.

The second keeps the relations when the things are gone. A web, a network, a field. But the line is as unfindable as the dot. "Coming into contact, having come into contact, and the one who brings them into contact are not found." Relation and what it relates are designated together. Neither is prior.

Indra's net is a fine image, so long as the net itself is not taken as what is finally found. And the order is fully valid. It is not a hidden code, or a set of laws standing behind appearing.

So "nothing stands alone" is only half of it. The other half is that the relations are not found from their own side either. What is left is not a web. It is this, appearing.
