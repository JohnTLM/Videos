### Title

Dependent Arising: An Overview of the Series | Thusness

### Description

Seven sections. Twenty-nine parts. One thread: nothing appears with a nature of its own, and what appears still works.

If you think you understand dependent arising, you don't. Agreeing is not living in it. So this series sets two ways of seeing side by side, one that takes things to stand on their own, one that looks at how they form, and asks which one holds: by experience, by logic, and by how things work. This overview says what each section asks. Is the world already there, or formed? What is it that appears? Is mind or matter worth leaning towards? How do we reason without own natures? How does the tradition teach the twelve links, and how does Nāgārjuna read them? And what does this view do for others, and does it survive its strongest objections? Then how to read the series: in order if you can, or where your question is, with the tests from every part.
