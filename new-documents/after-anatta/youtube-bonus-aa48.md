## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 8: Dōgen: Firewood and Ash (AA48, 10:04)

### Title

Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 8: Dōgen: Firewood and Ash | Thusness

### Description

Firewood burns, and there is ash. Does something pass from the wood into the ash? Or does nothing?

Dōgen's image of time, the third of four images of total exertion. Firewood and ash (Genjōkōan): each abides in its own dharma-position, with a before and an after, yet cut off from both. Ash is just ash, completely, and ash is the wood, the fire, the air and the time appearing as ash. Nāgārjuna's "neither that thing nor different from it, so neither cut off nor permanent" (MMK 18.10) fits Dōgen's two phrases: "cut off" means nothing permanent travels across; "a before and an after" means the ash really comes from the wood. The taste: nothing carried over. Then being-time (Uji): the pine is time, the bamboo is time; spring passes as spring, with nothing outside it passing through; time is not found apart from things (MMK 19.6). And birth and death (Shōji): when birth comes, just birth; when death comes, just death. Where I part: no cosmos or all of time stored in the moment, and no one great life behind all lives.
