## The Fist, the Wind and the Chariot (AA32, 11:46)

### Title

Dependent Arising, Part 1 of 4: The Fist, the Wind and the Chariot | Thusness

### Description

For years I have said it this way: the chariot has no essence, yet it still carries. There is no wind, yet the blowing is felt. I have come to see that these sentences disturb more than they open. They keep the chariot as the subject while emptying it, and leave a ghost that acts. The problem is not the insight. It is the grammar I have been saying it in. And no-self, seen only on the side of the seer, can leave the seen untouched.

Four insights close the gap. The name is drawn from the working: the blowing is the wind, completely. You already live this logic with a fist, a lap, a wave, a traffic jam. An essence would stop the carrying, as Nāgārjuna shows; emptiness is not a power behind it. And "then what carries?" is the it question again. Then a breeze and a cup of tea to look at directly, how I now write instead, Nietzsche on the lightning and its flash, and Nāgasena's chariot.
