## Yogācāra: The Three Natures, and Where I Part (AA68, 5:36)

### Title

Yogācāra: The Three Natures, and Where I Part | Thusness

### Description

By a spell, a piece of wood appears as an elephant. There is no elephant. But is there really wood?

Yogācāra has its own key to "all things lack a nature of their own": the three natures. What appears is the dependent nature; how it appears, as a thing out there grasped by a me in here, is the imagined; that it never exists that way is the perfected. Vasubandhu's magic show and Asaṅga's gold-bearing earth. Much of this I can walk with. I part at three places, narrower than "mind is the floor": the dependent nature as truly established, the storehouse consciousness, and self-knowing awareness. Each keeps something real underneath. Then Candrakīrti: mind and object stand or fall together, and idealism is materialism turned inside out. Two keys, and why I take Nāgārjuna's reading as final.
