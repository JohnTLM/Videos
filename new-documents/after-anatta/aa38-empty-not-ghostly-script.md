# Dependent Arising Part 4: Empty, Not Ghostly

If nothing has an essence, is anything really there?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Three parts, one pattern. An apple, a mountain, and a bat, a bee and an ant. A bowl of liquid, a tick at a pool, and the box of space. A measured value, two observers, and a stick in a dark room.

Each time, two ways of seeing were set side by side. Undeniably there, given, measured from its own side. Or formed, in dependence, designated together with whoever meets it. Each time, the formed view held. By experience, by logic, and by how things work.

Before the series goes on to other sides of dependent arising, I want to stop, and draw some conclusions.

And first, there is a fear to face. It sits quietly under every part so far. If it is not faced, everything else will be heard wrongly.

The fear goes like this. If nothing has an essence, then nothing is really there. The world must be hollow. Ghostly. A kind of void. Things become dreamlike and thin. Nothing can really hurt. And nothing really matters.

Let me set this beside what happens. Three times.

First, a car.

Surely, if the car has no essence of its own, it is only a shape in the mind. It cannot really touch me.

Or look. I step off the curb without looking, and the car knocks me down.

The brakes. The speed. The weight. The road. My not looking. Conditions meet, and I am on the ground. No essence was needed for that.

The car was never dangerous because of a core inside it. It is dangerous because of how it meets a body.

Second, the rain.

Surely, if the rain is empty, it is a kind of picture. Nothing gets wet.

Or look. The rain still falls. The clothes still soak through. The river still rises, and the rice still grows. Clouds, cold air, the slope of the land. The rain works exactly as before.

Third, a fever.

Surely, if this body is without essence, sickness is a sort of illusion. I can think it away.

Or look. I still catch the flu. My head still aches. My temperature still climbs. And I still need rest, and medicine. The medicine still works, because it too meets the body along its own channels.

So without essence, the car still knocks you down. It still rains. And you still get sick.

That is the big contrast. The inherent view expects that without essence, everything turns ghostly and void. It is just the other way round. Nothing turns ghostly. It is all exactly as vivid, as forceful, and as reliable as it was.

Where does the fear come from, then? Not from looking.

It is a misunderstanding, produced by confusion. By a habitual way of understanding, of analysing, and of orienting oneself in the world.

Understanding. The habit links being real with having a core. Real is heard as there from its own side. So when the core is denied, reality seems to be denied with it.

Analysing. The habit analyses by looking for what something is made of, underneath. When analysis finds no core, it reports: nothing there. Instead of: nothing missing.

Orienting. The habit stands as a solid me in here, facing solid things out there. Take away the solidity of the things, and that me feels it has lost its footing. As if the ground had dropped away.

But the force of the car never came from a core. Remove the essence, and nothing is removed from the working. Because the working never came from the essence.

The ghostliness is the old view looking at emptiness through its own glasses. It sees the floor gone, and expects the room to fall. The room was never standing on that floor.

This is why I call it a misunderstanding, not a discovery. Nihilism is not the opposite of the inherent view. It is the inherent view in mourning.

So what have the first three parts shown?

The given is the default, not the finding. That things are simply there, finished, before anything meets them, is how they seem before any thought. When looked for, the self-standing apple, the neutral liquid, and the value standing before any measurement are never found. They are supplied.

What appears is formed. The thing met, the one who meets it, and the channel of meeting are designated together. None is the finished layer under the others.

Agreement needs no floor. Beings of one kind agree, and correct each other, along shared channels. Observers agree by interacting, and checking again.

No new floor, either. Not the mind, holding the world inside it. Not relations, as the true stuff underneath. Each time the floor was moved, it was the same habit, in a new place.

Nothing that works is lost. The apple is still eaten. The water is still drunk. The experiment is still repeated. What fails is only the extra claim: from its own side.

And now a fourth. Without essence, nothing turns ghostly. The car, the rain and the fever are exactly as real as they were. What goes is only the floor that was never there, and the fear that grew from imagining it.

One last time in this section, the test.

By experience. In four parts, an essence was never met. Not in the apple, the bowl or the electron. And not in the car that knocks me down. What was met was always the appearing itself. Vivid, exact, and found only in its conditions.

By logic. Anything put forward as standing on its own has to be picked out. And picking it out makes it dependent. A thing, a mind, a relation: each undoes itself the moment it is named. And the ghostly void is no better off. Nothing is really there still pictures a there that has gone empty.

By working. The car knocks you down. The rain falls. The fever rises, and the medicine works. An essence could not do any of this, because what is fixed cannot meet anything. Dependence explains all of it.

So the given, the inherent, is not the truer view. It is the default one. And the ghostly void it fears is its own shadow.

I want to say plainly why I keep using these examples. An apple tossed from hand to hand. A mountain. A bowl of liquid. A tick at a pool. A click in a laboratory. A car, the rain, a fever.

They are not decorations for an argument. The whole purpose is to correct a misconception, and to do it with impact.

The misconception does not live in our theories. It lives in how the apple feels in the hand. There. Finished. From its own side. An argument alone rarely reaches that far.

So I put the two ways of seeing side by side, as sharply as I can, with things everyone has held, climbed, drunk, or been knocked down by. The contrast has to be felt, not only followed.

I know it is not easy to turn around and see this. The habit is older than any thought. And it returns the moment attention is off duty.

The first response is usually rejection. Surely the apple is just there. Surely without essence it must all be ghostly. That is natural. I do not expect one reading, or one video, to undo it.

But it is gradual. Each time the old picture is set beside what is actually met, it loosens a little.

What first seems counter-intuitive starts to make sense. What makes sense starts to look logical. And in the end, it is the old view that looks strange. And the dependent view that is simply tenable. The only way the apple, the car and the rain could work at all.

From rejection to acceptance. From counter-intuitive, to highly logical and tenable. That is the turn these examples are for.

This is Part 4 of the Dependent Arising series, and the close of its first section.

Next, the series turns to other major sides of dependent arising. What is a phenomenon? Mind and matter. And the logic of dependency.

What is left when essence goes is not less than before. It is exactly this. Vivid and working, with nothing behind it, and nothing missing.
