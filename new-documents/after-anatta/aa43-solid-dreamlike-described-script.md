# Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 4: Solid, Dreamlike, Described

A car. A dream. A wave function. Which of them is the real one?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

In Part 1, a bell, a red flower and a passing thought were looked for, and not found. In Parts 2 and 3, the habit retreated to the smallest moment, the smallest piece, and space itself. And found no floor in any of them.

So the habit tries something else. It stops digging down, and starts ranking across. What appears, it notices, appears in very different ways.

Some of it appears solid, and undeniable. A ball can be tossed and caught. A car can knock a person down.

Some of it appears dreamlike. A thought. A feeling. A memory. A dream at night. No weight, and no edges.

And some of it is never met at all. Only described. Physics speaks of fields, and probability waves, and wave functions. Nobody has ever seen one. And yet they predict what we will see, better than anything else we have.

The habit cannot leave these side by side. It picks one as the real one, and makes the others its shadow. For one person, the solid is real, and the mind is a shadow of it. For another, the mind is real, and matter is its projection. For a third, the equations are the reality, and the apple is only how they look to us.

So here I set the two ways of seeing side by side, three times. Once for each candidate. Then I look at a fourth, quieter candidate. The web of conditions itself.

First, a car.

In Section 1, the car knocked me down without needing an essence. Here the question is different. Is the solid the most real of all?

Surely it is. The car is hard, heavy and fast. It does not care what I think of it. Thoughts come and go. Dreams fade by breakfast. But the car is there in the morning. And if it hits me, I am hurt. Whatever else is real, matter is real. And everything else, mind included, is something matter does.

Or, and it is a big or, look at how the solid appears.

The hardness is met in the hand. The weight is met in the muscles that push. The speed is met by the eye, and the impact by a body that bruises. Take away the hand, the muscles, the eye and the body. What would hard or heavy still mean?

Solidity is not stored in the metal. It is a resistance that forms when two systems with the right coupling meet. For a body, the car is a wall. For a neutrino, it is almost open space.

And look for the car itself, as Candrakīrti looked for the chariot. Not the wheels. Not the engine. Not the frame. Not their sum. Not their shape. It is not found. Yet it carries me to work.

So the solid is not more real than anything else. It is one way of appearing. The way that pushes back. Vivid, not found, and dependently arisen, exactly like the bell.

And nothing is missing. The car still knocks you down. I still look both ways before I cross.

Second, a dream.

Take a dream. In it, someone dear who has died is alive again. I speak with them, and wake with tears on my face.

Surely, then, the mind is what is real. The dream proves it. There was no one in the room. Yet they were vivid, and the grief was real. If the mind can make a whole world at night, perhaps the daytime world is made the same way. Matter is only how the mind's contents appear. The mind is the floor.

Or, and again it is a big or, look at how the dream appears.

The dream was vivid, and it worked. It moved me to tears. But look for the one in the dream. They are not in the room. Look for them in the mind. Where is that mind? It is not found, any more than the car is.

And the dream came from conditions too. The years together. The missing of them. A song heard the day before. The body asleep. Take those away, and that dream does not come.

So the dreamlike is not more real than the solid either. A thought is as unfindable as a stone, and as dependent.

Losing the solid floor does not hand the floor to the mind. Making mind the floor and making matter the floor are one mistake, made in two directions.

And nothing is missing. The grief was grief. The tears were wet. And the missing is still there.

Third, a wave function.

Then the third candidate. The one many careful people trust most. Physics does not ask how the car feels, or what the dream means. It describes. And its descriptions are astonishingly reliable.

Surely, then, the description is the reality. Underneath the solid car and the dreaming mind are fields and wave functions. Exact, and impersonal. The apple and the bell are just how these look to creatures like us. The equations are the floor.

Or, and it is the biggest or, look at how the described appears.

Nobody has met a wave function. It appears as symbols on a page. And as predictions that match clicks on a detector. It is built from measurements, models, instruments, and the agreement of many people over centuries.

Section 1 looked closely at this. A measured value is a fact relative to the system that interacts. And the cut between the thing and the instrument is drawn by the whole setup, not found already drawn.

So the scientific descriptions are among the most reliable designations we have, within their domains. I honour them, and I use them every day. But they are designations too. A field is no better a candidate for what is really there than an apple.

And one caution belongs here. Physics does not show that things are empty. And emptiness does not need physics. At most, it is an echo.

And nothing is missing. The equations still predict. The satellites still guide my car. And the medicine designed from them still works.

So the three ways of appearing have each been looked for. And none of them is the floor. The solid, the dreamlike and the described are all ways of appearing, and all three meet the same search.

That is why this view is neither realism nor mind only. And it is why I do not rank them.

But here a quieter trap opens. And it catches careful people.

When the mind tries to say what an appearance is, it reaches for dependence. The red depends on the light, the eye and the surface. The sound depends on the bell, the air and the ear. An appearance, it seems, sits at the centre of a web of relations.

Surely, then, the web is what is real. The apple is unfindable, granted. The bell is unfindable. But the conditions are real. The relations are what is really there. Dependent arising itself is the floor.

Or look at the web.

Nāgārjuna examined the conditions first. The own nature of things, he said, is not found in their conditions. And when things have no existence of their own, when this exists, that comes to be cannot describe one real thing producing another. He examined contact and connection themselves. The meeting, what has met, and the one who brings them together, are not found.

So the conditions are as unfindable as what they condition. They are not a solid network hidden behind the appearance. They too are appearances, as dreamlike as the apple.

A relation needs what it relates. And what it relates is not found apart from the relation. Relation and the things related are designated together. Neither is first, and neither is the ground.

This matters, because the web is the most respectable floor of all. It sounds like dependent arising itself. But dependent arising made into a floor is no longer dependent arising. It is the old essence, spread thin across the gaps between things.

And nothing is missing. The seed still needs water. Conditions still meet, and what meets still works. Only really there, from their own side, is taken from them.

If the solid, the dreamlike, the described, and even the conditions are all unfindable, are they unreal?

The tradition answers with images. A magic show. A dream. A city of gandharvas seen in the clouds. A mirage.

The images are chosen for their two sides. A magician's elephant walks across the stage, and the audience gasps. It is seen. It frightens. It is not an elephant. A dream can make the dreamer weep. A mirage is seen, and thirsty travellers turn towards it. None of these is nothing. What they lack is a nature of their own, found when looked for.

Nāgārjuna presses the point. Suppose the Buddha conjured a magical person, and that person conjured another. The second is made, and it acts. In the same way, actions, agents and their fruits are like magical creations. And they work.

And when someone objected that an empty argument cannot refute anything, he answered: one magically created man can stop another.

So the effects are fully real. The car injures, and no essence is found doing it. The grief is grief. The medicine still heals.

One way of putting this needs care. It is tempting to say that things can work because they are empty. As if emptiness were a power that makes function possible. Emptiness does not do anything. It is not a cause, a condition, or a ground.

The point is only this. Function never needed an essence in the first place. And the reasoning runs the other way. If a thing had a nature of its own, fixed from its own side, it could not arise, change or end. Nothing could be done with it.

Longchenpa built a whole book on these images. Finding Rest in Illusion. A dream, a magical illusion, a trick of the eye, a mirage, an echo, a city in the clouds, a reflection, and an apparition. Each is vivid, and each is not found. As the title says, the point is not to make the world seem thin. It is rest. Nothing in it needs to be held.

So, which holds? The ranking, or none of them privileged?

By experience. I meet the car's hardness, the dream's grief, and the physicist's prediction. Each is met vividly, each in its own way. At no point do I meet one of them standing underneath the others, holding them up.

By logic. Whichever is made the floor meets the same search as the others, and fails the same way. Matter breaks into parts. The mind is not found when looked for. A description depends on the describing. And the web cannot be the floor either, because a relation needs what it relates.

By working. The car, the dream and the equation each work in their own domain, reliably. None of them drives the others from underneath. The dream moves me. The car stops me. The equation predicts. Conditions meet, and things work. Nothing needed to be the floor for any of it.

So ranking is not the truer view. It is the default one, carried by habit, and by two little words. Really, and just. Really matter. Just mind. Really equations. Just relations.

None of this makes my everyday words false. The car is solid. The dream was a dream. The physics is good physics. And conditions are conditions. What fails is only the extra claim. One of them is what is really there, and the rest are its shadow.

And something eases when this is seen. I no longer need to decide which world is the real one before I can live in it. The car, the dream and the equation can each be met as they appear. Fully. Without one swallowing the others.

This is Section 2, Part 4 of the Dependent Arising series.
