# What Is Negated

This moment ceases as it arises. Does it arise, or does it cease?

The answer to the koan is that no "it" is found. But what it denies is narrow. A moment with a nature of its own, standing between its arising and its ceasing.

This is the second step of The Logic, from After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness. Step one set out two logics, substance and dependency. This step is about aiming the denial exactly. Aim it short, and the "it" survives somewhere else. Aim it too far, and the koan destroys the very moment it was meant to free.

Tsongkhapa made this the first step of the whole analysis. Unless the target is found as it actually works in your own mind, the refutation misses, however sound the reasoning.

The author learned this the long way. In his words: "In the years of I AM I was good at negating. Thoughts, sensations, the body, the world: not this, not that." What he never put through the test was the one doing the setting aside. "The more I negated, the more solid the Witness became."

Start with what the koan leaves alone. The bell is struck, and the ringing fades. The sound is vivid while it sounds. This is fully valid, and nothing in the analysis makes it fainter. What differs is how the same sound is seen.

With the logic of substance, there is a sound first, and arising and ceasing happen to it. So every answer needs the sound to stand still, for at least an instant, between the two. With the logic of dependency, the sounding is heard, and nothing standing behind it is found. "Arising" and "ceasing" are how the sounding is picked out, against what came before and what comes after. Nothing is missing from it.

What is denied is one thing: own-nature, svabhāva. It is what a thing would have if it were what it is apart from everything else, including the mind that picks it out. Nāgārjuna gives its marks plainly. Own-nature is not made, and does not depend on anything else. Anything made from causes and conditions cannot have it. That is the whole target. Nothing else is negated. In the koan, own-nature is the moment that abides, there on its own, so that arising and ceasing could happen to it. That is what is not found.

There are two ways to fall short.

The first is to refute only theories. The tradition separates acquired grasping, shaped by philosophy, from innate grasping: the felt sense, before any words, that what appears is there from its own side. Animals and infants have it. One can follow every step of the koan and leave that felt sense untouched. The theories were never the real problem. The habit was.

The second is to negate the self, but keep something just behind it. A pure Awareness, a Source, a ground of being. This is subtle, because the negation feels complete. The author held this position before 1997. He never turned the test on that. In the koan, the "it" is dropped, and a timeless Now takes its place, or an awareness in which moments come and go. Each is the "it" moved one level down. So ask the koan again. Does the Now arise, or cease? Does the awareness?

The opposite mistake is quieter, but as harmful. It hears that no "it" is found, and concludes that nothing happens at all. Actions bring no results. Emptiness becomes a void to rest in. Nāgārjuna refuses both sides: "'It is' is the grasping of permanence. 'It is not' is the view of annihilation. So the wise should not take their stand on existence or on non-existence."

Yet a harsh word still hurts, and a kind one still helps. If a reading of emptiness makes cause and result matter less, it has aimed past the target. Only because things are empty can they arise, change and work at all. A moment with own-nature could never pass.

When own-nature is negated, nothing is put in its place. The tradition calls this a non-implicative negation. It removes, and implies nothing further. No timeless Now steps in, no awareness behind the passing, and no void. The sound is simply sounding. And emptiness is not exempt. It is not a substance, a void, or a ground beneath appearances. It is empty too.

Presence was never the target. After anatta, presence did not fade. It became more vivid, because it was no longer pushed behind experience. In the author's words: "What went was the standing, not the shining."

The author adds that the logic of substance is not about matter. With every word, thought, sensation and experience, we take it that there must be something there. And the habit is quicker than any experience. Whatever we taste, it rebuilds into a something.

We taste luminous clarity, and we invent a Witness: a knower behind the knowing. We taste the vividness of appearances, and we make it objective: a world out there. We taste unfindability, and we make it private: "it is all in my mind." Three genuine tastes, three inventions. The experience was true, and the reading was false. So better experience cannot cure it.

The author met all three on his own path. It was the logic of dependency that got him out. A knower that exists only together with the known is not an inside looking out. And an inside that depends on an outside is not truly inside. In his words: "The taste was right. The something we made of it was not."

The koan is only the sharpest case. The same aim works wherever the habit lands.

The self. When you are falsely accused, an "I" appears, vivid and solid. Look for it apart from the heat in the face and the racing thoughts, and it is not found. Yet the one who speaks, decides and answers is not denied.

An object. Hold a cube of ice. Look for the cold: in the ice, in the hand, or between them. It is not found in any of them. Yet the hand is still cold.

A cause. Plant a seed, with soil, water and warmth, and a shoot comes up. The cause is not denied, only a shoot already packed inside the seed. Because the seed is empty, it can grow.

The person. The person you love is not negated. What is denied is a core that would stay the same if body, mind, history and name all changed. The love is real, and it is love for someone who is changing.

So try it. Listen to a sound until it ends, and ask: does it arise, or does it cease? The sounding is not in doubt. What is not found is a sound standing between its start and its end. Let only that go.
