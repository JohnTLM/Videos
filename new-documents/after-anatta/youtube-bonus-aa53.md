### Description

It is raining, and a question comes almost by itself: what is it, really?

The opening of Section 3, Mind and Matter. Before any argument about mind and matter, the mind that will hear every argument has to be seen. Faced with what appears, we ask what it is made of underneath: matter, mind, or a third stuff such as energy, a field or qi. Such a mind cannot rest until it posits a ground, and it takes "there has to be something" as reality itself. Language carries the view: the rose is red, the lightning flashes, it is raining. What is the "it"? The question is left open: is it ever necessary to ask that way, and must the answer always be a something? The Middle Way of Nagarjuna takes a completely new approach: no rival something, the something examined on its own terms, the question changed, emptiness not made the new answer, and the world kept. Taken apart, the default contradicts experience moment to moment, and contradicts itself on its own premises. It is a habit, not reality.
