# Dependent Arising, Part 3 of 6: What It Is For

This is Part 3 of 6: What it is for.

In Parts 1 and 2 I asked whether dependent arising is as plain as it sounds, and set out what it says: no power in the cause, no doer behind the doing, whatever appears singled out and designated, and conditions that are the appearing itself, not a web.

Now the other question. What is it for?

First, there are two ways of knowing rightly here. It helps not to collapse them.

The first works through concepts, within the framework. Dependent arising is how the whole of Indra's net comes about. The net is conventional and empty. It keeps presenting itself upon conditions, within the framework, for functional purposes.

This is not a lesser knowing. It is how a doctor treats, how a cook cooks, how a promise is kept, how karma is understood. In this mode, dependent arising also shows emptiness. It corrects the inherent reading from inside the framework.

The second is freedom from all elaborations. It is not within the framework at all. Nothing reified is added. Nothing is cut into before and after, part and whole, cause and effect.

Without the framework, there is one complete stroke. One here does not mean one stuff, the oneness of a refined non-dual. It means not cut up. The colour is still the colour, and the sound is still the sound, each with its own conditions, and nothing they are all made of.

Not within the framework does not mean somewhere else. It means not known by cutting the appearing into concepts. It is the same appearing, known without the cutting.

Neither cancels the other. The first is not a ladder to throw away. The second is not a place outside the world to retreat to. Freedom from elaborations, kept apart from the framework, would become a new ground. That is why, after it, the taste is met again inside the framework, in ordinary life, with the parts and conditions now light.

So the framework can be held three ways. Without it: one complete stroke. Within it, with the wisdom of dependent arising: the same parts and conditions, now light. And in between, where we mostly live: within it, reified. That middle one is the matrix.

Within the framework, dependent arising does three kinds of work.

It explains. It answers the question: how does this come about? These conditions, met together.

It dismantles. Because a thing arises in dependence, it is not there from its own side. So dependence is the reason that takes apart the self, the object, the cause, the whole, the field, the substrate, energy and the web, and shows emptiness. Tsongkhapa's tradition calls it the king of reasons.

And it describes the taste. Seen through, it is the taste itself: nothing standing on its own, nothing missing.

The three hang together. It can dismantle because it explains. What validly depends cannot also stand by itself. So the reasoning does not destroy the world it takes apart. What goes is only the "from its own side".

Nāgārjuna joins all three in one verse. What arises in dependence is what we call emptiness. That is a designation in dependence. And that itself is the middle way.

Two guards go with the dismantling. It puts nothing in place of what it removes, or emptiness becomes the next thing to hinge on. And it dismantles itself too, since dependence is not found from its own side either. A reason held as the final truth would be one more construct.

Dependent arising is an enlightened view. By that I do not mean a view only the enlightened can hold. I mean the right view of how things are, which we do not yet live in.

We live reading the matrix as inherent. Things seem to stand by themselves. Causes seem to push. A self seems to sit behind the seeing. That seeming is not a thought we hold. It is a deep habit of seeing, in the body, before thought.

Have a second thought. If you think you do not think inherently, have a second thought.

Toss the apple again. Is the solid apple simply there, out there, before any thought at all?

For almost all of us, it is simply there. That is the habit at work. It does not announce itself. It is just the way things look.

Do not swap it for a subtler picture either: a hardness, a red and an arc that are given first, with the name "apple" stuck on them later. The hardness, the red and the arc are no more given than the apple. Nothing bare waits underneath for a label. The naming is part of the appearing, arising in dependence like the rest. The name and what it names are designated together.

So agreeing with dependent arising is not living in it. We do not live in the wisdom of dependent arising. We live in the confusion of thinking, analysing and orienting ourselves with inherent thoughts, habitually and without knowing it.

Even "I do not think inherently" is usually held the same way, as a fact about a self standing behind the thought. It is Feynman's quip again. If you think you understand, you don't.

So I keep two frameworks apart.

The default framework is the conventional cutting itself: parts and whole, before and after, cause and effect, agent and action. It works. We need it to speak, to cook and to keep promises. Dependent arising lives here. "How does this come about?" Only this framework can ask that, and dependent arising is its answer.

The inherent framework is the habit of taking what the cutting singles out as standing by itself. That is the matrix. Not a world, not a field. A habit of seeing.

This does not mean we live in a matrix and must escape it. The framework of parts and conditions is not what binds us. What binds us is living in it with reified thoughts, taking the parts, the conditions and the sequence to be there from their own side. Dependent arising is not the matrix. It is the wisdom that shows the matrix up. The framework is not the prison.

Dependent arising lives in the first and unbinds the second. Run the two together, and "dependent arising lives in a framework" is heard as "dependent arising is only a story". That is the slide into annihilation.

And living in the framework with this wisdom, I begin to see that even spontaneous presence can be understood within it. Crudely and clumsily, because the framework still speaks in pieces and in sequence. But truly. Conditions are the appearing. Nothing produces it. It is complete as its conditions meet. It is not a place beyond concepts, waiting outside the framework. That would make it a new ground.

How is that different from reading a taste through a concept, which I warn against? The difference is not concepts. It is reification. When the concepts are taken as found, the taste is fixed into a self or a source. Held as designations, the same concepts point and do not fix. Understanding is still not the taste. It is the taste understood rightly.

Now, ignorance. Ignorance is not a power either. It is not a thing in us with a force to blind. It is a very deep habit of confused seeing, designated as ignorance, and not found when searched for. It blinds reliably because its conditions keep being renewed. And it can end, because nothing stands behind it.

Some readings say dependent arising originates with ignorance, since the twelve links begin there. That reading needs refining. Dependent arising as a view originates as the corrective antidote for ignorance.

If it simply came from ignorance, it would be odd to praise it as the supreme teaching. Yet that is what the texts do.

Nāgārjuna opens his Fundamental Verses by bowing to the Buddha who taught dependent arising: without arising or ceasing, the stilling of elaborations, peace. Candrakīrti opens his Clear Words by unpacking that homage. Tsongkhapa praises the Buddha for this one teaching, the best of all he taught, and says that seeing it reverses the root of the round of birth and death, which is ignorance.

And the Buddha himself: whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dhamma; whoever sees the Dhamma sees dependent arising. It stands whether or not buddhas arise. Not as a law kept somewhere behind things, but because this is how appearing is. So it is not something ignorance produced either.

The twelve links do begin with ignorance, and none of these masters denies it. They read the links two ways. Forward, from ignorance to old age and death, the links show how the round of suffering runs. In reverse, from ignorance ceasing to suffering ceasing, they show how it ends. No one praises ignorance. They praise the seeing that undoes it.

One last knot. I have read more than once that conventional truth is a veil, so dependent arising, being conventional, must be the veil.

The source is genuine. The Sanskrit word for conventional, saṃvṛti, can mean a covering. Candrakīrti gives it three meanings. Concealment, the ignorance that hides how things are. Mutual dependence. And worldly convention, the designations by which the world speaks.

Read carefully, this supports the refinement. What veils is ignorance, the concealer. Dependent arising is the second meaning of the same word, and designation the third. Concealment is the matrix. Mutual dependence is dependent arising. Worldly convention is the default framework.

The same appearance can be held two ways. Held by the concealer, it seems to stand by itself, and that seeming is the veil. Seen as dependent arising, it is the seeing that lifts the veil.

Candrakīrti says as much himself. For those who have abandoned afflictive ignorance, things appear like reflections. They are not truths for them, because they no longer fix on them as real. He calls this mere convention. Still appearing, still working, no longer veiling. The conventional, read with ignorance, veils. With wisdom, why would it veil?

Other readings deserve to be stated fairly. Gorampa and the Sakya school, as they are commonly read, and some Kagyu and Dzogchen teachers, define the conventional as what appears to mistaken cognition. Then convention veils by definition. That is a deliberate choice of meaning, taking the first meaning as the whole word. Where I differ is in giving dependence and designation their own place.

In Tsongkhapa's tradition, things can still appear as standing by themselves after ignorance is no longer believed, as a subtle obscuration that lasts until buddhahood. That does not contradict what I say. It is the habit of seeing inherently. The veil of belief goes first. The veil of habitual appearance thins in time.

And even held without ignorance, the framework is still an elaboration, compared with freedom from all elaborations. That is not a veil. It is the other mode of right knowing.

All views and understanding here are my own, from my practice and contemplation. I do not represent any tradition, school or teacher.

So what is it for? It explains, it dismantles, and it describes the taste. It is the antidote to ignorance, not its shape. And what veils is ignorance, never dependent arising.
