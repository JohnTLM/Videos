Look at a colour in front of you. Now ask: who is doing the seeing?

This is After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness.

When I look back over my books and articles, the same move keeps turning up. Something seems to stand behind an action, doing it. A seer behind seeing. A walker behind walking. A cause behind its effect, pushing it out. A ground behind appearances, giving them off. Each time it is looked for, it is not found. And each time, nothing is put in its place.

Anatta, dependent arising and spontaneous presence are three names for that one finding. Anatta makes it in seeing. Dependent arising makes it everywhere. Spontaneous presence is what is met when nothing is left that makes the appearing.

So they are not three teachings, and they are not three stages. They are three descriptions of one taste. Anatta says no one is behind it. Dependent arising says nothing in it stands on its own. Spontaneous presence says nothing is missing, and nothing is making it.

This talk has two parts. First the journey, in which the taste is met. Then the map, in which it is described.

The journey starts with anatta.

The Buddha told Bāhiya: in the seen, just the seen. No one behind the eyes, doing the seeing.

Think of the wind. We say the wind is blowing, as if the wind were one thing and the blowing another thing it does. But when the blowing stops, no wind is left over, resting somewhere. The blowing is the wind. In the same way, there is seeing, and no seer left over behind it.

When the witness is not found, experience does not go blank. The tree is more vivid, not less.

But anatta does not do everything. It removes the one who watches. It does not, by itself, remove the things watched. The observer has disappeared, but the world has not. Things, causes and time can still be held as solid, each sitting there by itself.

Right after anatta comes a first taste.

What are seeing and hearing, in real time? We call them events, processes. That is true, but it is still words. So look.

Seeing is just colours. No effort at all. Hearing is just sound. No effort at all.

Nothing is doing the seeing, and the seeing is not being done. Even what we called a process does not seem to be a process. Nothing runs underneath the colours, carrying them from one moment to the next.

And the colours are not dark. They are clear, known and vivid. The knowing is right there in the colour, and nothing stands behind it, knowing.

At the same time, the factors are intimate. The colour is there only with light, with the eye, with attention. The sound is there only with the air, the ear, and the car going by. None of these stands outside, feeding it in. The alignment of these factors is the appearing. Not two steps, factors first and then a lining up. They are how we speak of the appearing when we take it apart.

So here, right after anatta, is a first-hand taste of spontaneous presence. Remember it. I will come back to it.

How is this different from a refined non-dual state? A refined non-dual rests in oneness. It is still haunted by substance. It thinks of everything as made of one luminosity, one clarity that colours and sounds are made of.

Oneness merges the seer into everything. Anatta finds no seer there to merge. With nothing merged, no "one" is left for everything to be made of.

In the taste, there is no "made of". The colour is clear as colour. The sound is clear as sound. Each is complete with its own conditions. Six clarities, one for each of the senses and the mind, not one lamp lighting them all.

So the test is simple. Is there a "one" that everything is made of? If so, substance is still there.

This taste does not stay in seeing and hearing. After anatta it widens, often by itself, before I had any view for it.

Each event seems to spend itself completely. The sound of a car going by is wholly the sound. Nothing is behind it, holding back. Boundaries lose their solidity, yet distinctions never disappear. The car is still the car.

Doh-gen called this total exertion: each thing spending itself fully in its own place. Firewood in its place, ash in its place, each complete.

It is easily heard as "the whole cosmos is present in each moment". That is holism. It only makes the whole the ground instead of the parts. The event spends itself completely, with no cosmos packed inside it.

It is not a second taste. It is the same taste, widening beyond the senses. In seeing and hearing I spoke of it as spontaneous presence. Spreading through each event, it is total exertion. Already two descriptions of one taste.

But a taste is not yet freedom.

All of this is the taste: immediate, effortless, spontaneous. And still the mind has not released its habit of reifying. Ignorance has not been unbound.

How can both be there at once? Because they are not in the same place. The taste is in the immediacy of the senses. The habit is in how the world is understood around it.

While I see a flower, the seeing is just colours, with no one behind it. Yet the moment I turn away, the flower is still assumed to be sitting there by itself, its red belonging to it. A cause is still felt to push its effect. Death is still the end of a someone.

For years after anatta, the taste was there and the view still treated each thing as standing by itself. That gap is what I call the desynchronisation. The experience is free of substance. The framework is not.

The habit does not announce itself. It does not feel like a view. It feels like the world itself.

It is only when it is challenged by Nah-gar-joo-nah's verses that these habits are brought to the surface. That is what the logic is for. Not to give a better taste, but to bring out the habit, and then to unwind it, case by case.

The logic does not do this alone. The habit is deeper than thought. It is in perception, and even in the body. What the logic does is point to the place to look. Then I look, and check it against the taste. Is a whole found apart from its parts? Is a cause found pushing? Each time the answer is seen, not only agreed to, the habit loosens a little.

Insight without the logic drifts back into nouns. Logic without the taste stays a clever argument about words.

Nah-gar-joo-nah makes the same move as anatta, again and again. There is no seer, either bound up with seeing or apart from it. A goer does not go. Fire is neither the same as its fuel nor other than it. And in his first chapter he takes the doer out of causes. A self is the doer behind the doing. A causal power is the doer behind the causing. Neither is found.

Before freedom, the mind always hinges on something.

First it hinges on parts. A thing is made of its pieces. When the parts are seen through, it does not let go. It hinges on the whole. When the whole is seen through, it finds something finer. Qi, energy, a field, relations, a substrate. Each time, it is looking for a foundation.

The tools meet each hinge in turn. The chariot is not found among its parts, nor apart from them. Energy is singled out and designated like everything else. A field is the whole again, in a softer form. Relations are not found either. And a substrate is the foundation the mind was looking for all along. It is not found.

When all of these are exhausted, with nothing left to hinge on, the mind becomes free from all elaborations, and in a stable way. Stable does not mean the habit can never stir. It means the freedom no longer needs looking, or effort, to be there.

Then appearance is again tasted as vividly present, spontaneous and free. This is the first taste, revisited.

It is the same taste. What has changed is the understanding around it, and two things more. Reach: at first the taste lived in the senses. Now it covers thought, the body, things, causes, time and death. And stability: at first the habit took it back when I stopped looking. Now it no longer depends on looking.

Take away the doer from everything, and put nothing in its place. No seer, no producing cause, and not even dependence kept as a new thing. Then nothing is making the appearing. The sound of a car going by is not a product. It shows up whole and vivid, exactly as its conditions meet.

That is what spontaneous presence means to me. It is not uncaused. Every appearing has its conditions. And it is not a source. Nothing stands behind appearing, giving it off. Mind and matter lose their weight, and become light, magical and plastic.

Then I re-enter the marketplace, and dirty my hands.

The marketplace is ordinary life. Work, family, money, arguments, other people's troubles, choices that have to be made. The last of the Zen ox-herding pictures shows it: barefoot, smeared with mud and ashes, he comes into the market with helping hands, smiling.

Here dependent arising changes its role. Before, it was the tool that took each hinge apart. Now I deal in causes and conditions all day: what helps, what harms, what follows from what. Dependent arising is how help works.

Who decides, if no one is there? Nobody disappears. I still act, choose, keep my promises and answer for what I do, as a conventional person. What is gone is the doer behind the doing, pulling the levers. Responsibility is answered first, and nothing in the taste cancels it.

And why help, if others are not found? Their suffering still functions, and it arises in dependence. Because it is not fixed, it can be eased. A suffering that stood by itself could not be touched.

So, looked back on, the taste has been met three times. Anatta, in seeing. Freedom from all elaborations, when every hinge is exhausted. And dependent arising and emptiness, in the marketplace. In the taste there is no order. In a life there is one, and it goes round more than once.

Now the map.

Three descriptions, one taste. Anatta: no one is behind it. Not a blank; nobody disappears. Dependent arising: nothing in it stands on its own. Not a web, and not a ground. Spontaneous presence: nothing is missing, and nothing is making it. Not uncaused, and not a source.

None is higher. They are not a lower truth and a higher one, and not stages. Nor does one turn into another. I do not say dependent arising becomes spontaneous presence, any more than we say summer becomes winter. Nothing crosses.

And anatta, read as a seal and not only as the first insight, says the same of everything: all things are without self. Not only no seer behind seeing, but no self-nature in the seen, in the cause, in the condition.

The descriptions keep each other honest. Dependent arising keeps spontaneous presence from being heard as coming from a hidden ground. Spontaneous presence keeps dependent arising from being heard as a machine of causes working in the dark.

Dependent arising lives in the default framework: the everyday cutting of the appearing into parts and whole, before and after, cause and effect. That framework works. We need it to speak, to cook and to keep promises.

The framework is not the prison. What binds is living in it with reified thoughts, taking the parts and the conditions to be there from their own side. That habit is the matrix. Dependent arising is not the matrix. It is the wisdom that shows the matrix up.

Without the framework, there is one complete stroke. One here does not mean one stuff. It means not cut into before and after, parts and whole. The colour is still the colour, and the sound still the sound.

Freedom from all elaborations is known without the cutting. That does not mean somewhere else. It is the same appearing, known without concepts. That is why it can go back to work in the marketplace without becoming a refuge.

And from the taste, I recognise its many faces. A sound, a thought, a flash of anger, a cause and its effect, a stretch of time, washing the dishes.

A death, too. Conventionally someone has died, and that is mourned. But no one is found who goes, and nothing that was there by itself is cut off. The grief, vivid as it is, has nothing behind it.

It is much like a model that recognises faces. It knows a face across light, angle and age, not because it stored one photograph, but because it learned the face's structure. A sameness of structure. Once the taste is known, it is recognised by its shape.

The shape is the same each time. No one behind it, nothing standing on its own, nothing missing. Not the same stuff carried from one situation to the next. If the taste became an essence present in everything, it would be the ground again. And no one is doing the recognising. That too is an appearing, with no recogniser standing behind it.

Recognition can misfire. The I AM presence, a blank quiet, a refined oneness: each can look like the taste. The test is the shape. Is a witness, a source or a background still left? Is something being kept up?

In fact, every mistake on this path has the same shape: the doer, put back somewhere else, in a bigger coat.

After anatta, a vast Awareness in which everything happens. In total exertion, a cosmic whole, or a substrate flowing under the verbs. In dependent arising, a web or a mechanism behind things. In spontaneous presence, a source that gives off appearances, or appearances without causes. In the marketplace, the saviour who has arrived, or the bystander who says it is all empty, so nothing needs doing. And in practice, a state to keep up. Maintenance would be the split again. None of this is kept. It was always so. It is recognised.

Do I depart from the great masters? At the core, I do not believe so.

Nah-gar-joo-nah praises dependent arising as without arising and without ceasing. What arises in dependence does not arise from its own side. Zog-chen says spontaneous presence is not made by causes and conditions. I read that as Nah-gar-joo-nah's "unborn by nature": appearing through conditions, yet nothing produced.

Where I part, I say so. From Tsong-kah-pah, on the order of the path: mine was the taste first, and the view syncing with it later. On the bridge across time: I keep neither carried nor cut, with no link needed. And from the reading of Indra's net as the real: nothing stands alone, but the relations are not found either. These are my readings. Some lineages see it differently, and you should read them for yourself.

Try it. Listen to a sound nearby.

Look for the hearer behind the hearing. Then look at the hearing itself. Is a process running underneath, or just sound, with no effort at all?

Now look for what makes the sound. The engine, the air, the ear, the attention. Is any of them standing behind it, pushing it out?

Then stop looking. The sound is complete, exactly as its conditions meet. Do not try to keep this. When the next sound comes, it is complete too.

No one behind it, nothing standing on its own, nothing missing: one taste, met again and again.
