What is it that appears? This sound. This red. This thought.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Section 1 asked whether one finished world waits underneath, seen differently by different beings. It does not. What appears is formed. And nothing is lost in seeing so.

That clears the ground for a quieter question. Not the world. Not the mountain. But this sound, this red, this thought, right now. What is it?

Anatta asks who is behind experience. Here I ask what the experience itself is. Most of the big ideas we live by grow out of how we answer it. Private and public. Inside and outside. Mind and matter. Cause and effect.

The usual word for it is phenomenon. And the word needs care. In the Middle Way, a phenomenon is anything that can be known, and every one of them is empty. But the Ah-bee-dar-mah used the same word for something else. A basic element, carrying its own mark. What is finally there when the analysis stops. That second sense is what this section looks for. And it is never found. So for what is actually met, I will just say: what appears.

And I will test the question itself as I go. What is it? It sounds innocent. But it may already carry the given inside it.

As in Section 1, I will not argue first. I will set two ways of seeing side by side, three times. Then we can ask which one holds.

First, a bell.

A temple bell is struck. The sound is bright, full, and unmistakable. Nobody needs to be told it is there. It fills the courtyard.

Surely, then, the sound is something. It is vivid, so it must be real in itself, at least while it rings. Something has to be there for the brightness to belong to. And if it is real, it must be somewhere. Out there in the bell and the air. Or in here, in my ears and my mind.

Or, and it is a big or, look for it.

Is the sound in the bell? Touch the bell. There is metal, and a trembling. Not the sound.

Is it in the air? The air carries a wave of pressure. The wave is not the clear tone I hear.

Is it in the ear? The ear has membranes and small bones. None of them is the sound.

Is it in the mind, then? A little picture of a bell, kept in here? Look for that mind. It is not found either.

The sound is not in any of them. It is not something apart from them. And it is not their sum, as if bell plus air plus ear added up to a tone. Yet it has not gone anywhere. It is as clear as before.

This is the old test of Chun-drah-keer-tee's chariot. The chariot is not its parts. Not apart from its parts. Not their collection, and not their shape. Searched for in every way, it does not turn up. Yet it carries its passenger. The same search works on a sound.

And the sound came only because the bell, the striking, the air, the ear and the attention came together. Take one away, and there is no tone.

So the sound has three features, all at once. Vivid: it rings, and it cannot be denied. Not found: looked for, it has no core, no edge, no place where it sits. Dependently arisen: it sounds only when its conditions meet.

These are not three facts that happen to coincide. They are one fact, seen three ways.

And nothing is missing. The bell still calls the monks to the hall.

Second, a red flower.

A red flower by the path. The red is deep and glowing. If anything is simply there, surely a colour is. No thinking is needed to see it.

Surely the red belongs to the petal. The petal has it, the way a box has its contents. When no one is looking, the petal is still red. And seeing only reports the red that was there.

Or, and again it is a big or, look at how the red appears.

At noon it glows. At dusk it goes dark and grey. Under a sodium street lamp it turns brown. A bee, seeing in ultraviolet, meets a pattern on the petal that I never see, and may not meet red at all. For someone with red green colour blindness, it is a dull brownish olive.

The red forms with the light, the petal's surface, the eye, the kind of body, and the attention. All in one meeting. There is no red stored in the petal, waiting for an eye to fetch it.

Nor is it a private picture in here, painted by the mind from a colourless thing out there. That would only move the stored red from the petal into the head. And in the head, it is not found either.

Here is the point I most want to make. It is the easiest to miss.

The red is not first a raw red, and then, afterwards, dependent. It does not wait for my description to become dependent. It is dependent in the very moment it is red. There is no plain layer underneath that dependence is laid over.

In the same way, the red is singled out as this red in the seeing itself, before any word. Designating, meeting and appearing are one event, through and through.

And nothing is missing. The red still guides the bee to the nectar. And it still stops me on the path.

Third, a passing thought.

Surely a thought is different. A sound needs air and an ear. But a thought is mine. Inside. Made of mind. If anything is real in itself, it is this thinking, right here. Doubt everything else, and still there is this.

Or, and it is the biggest or, look at a thought as it passes.

I forgot to lock the door.

It arrives vividly. Words. A faint picture of the door. A small jolt in the chest.

Where is it? Not in the words. They are sounds remembered. Not in the picture. It fades. Not in the jolt. That is a feeling in the body. And not apart from them.

And where did it come from? From the door. From the habit of locking it. From the hour, the language I learned, a sound in the street that reminded me. Take those away, and that thought does not come.

So the thought is no more a little thing than the sound is. Vivid. Not found. Dependently arisen. Exactly like the bell and the red.

That matters. It closes the way out that the mind only schools offered. If the flower is not found out there, perhaps it is in here, as mind. Chun-drah-keer-tee refused that way out. The mind is looked for the same way, and it is not found either. Mind and its objects rise and fall together.

And nothing is missing. The thought still sends me back to lock the door.

Now, when the search finds nothing, the mind takes one more quick step.

So the sound is nowhere. Behind everything there is a great emptiness. A void. A silent space, in which things appear and vanish.

That feels like depth. It is the easiest mistake to make here.

The absence is not a place. It is not a background left over when things are gone. And it is not a container they appear in. Nah-gar-joo-nah warns that those who turn emptiness into a view are incurable. Even the word empty is only said to point.

The search removes the assumed thing, and puts nothing in its place. Not a void. Not a ground. Not some reality beyond words.

So not found does not mean nothing. The bell still rings. It means only this: there was never a thing there to be found. And the ringing never needed one.

So, which holds? I test them as in Section 1. By experience, by logic, and by how things work.

By experience. Listen to one sound, now. Where exactly is it? What is it made of? Could it ring without the ear, the air, the attention? The sound stays bright. And nothing is found. No sound thing behind the hearing. No hearer behind the sound. Only hearing, vivid and complete.

By logic. Suppose the sound were real in itself, with a nature of its own. Then it would depend on nothing. So it could not arise when the bell is struck, or stop when the ringing dies. A thing fixed from its own side could not change. And what cannot change cannot appear, or act. The very vividness the substance view rests on is the clearest mark of dependence.

By working. The bell calls the monks. The red guides the bee. The thought sends me back to the door. Each works as a meeting of conditions, reliably. And no essence is found doing it. Function never needed an essence.

So the substance reading is not the truer view. It is the default one, carried by habit and by language. The noun, the sound, quietly sets up one thing behind the hearing.

What about the question I began with? What is it?

In everyday life, it is a good question. What is that sound? The temple bell. The answer is true. It can be checked. And it sends me to the hall.

But asked as: what is it really, in itself? Then the question already assumes an it. A thing standing there, with a nature of its own, waiting for the right description. That is the given, slipping back in through grammar.

Asked that way, the question has no answer. Not because the answer is hidden, or beyond words. But because there was never an it, for an answer to be about. It is like asking where the fist goes when the hand opens.

The Buddha met questions like this again and again. And he did not answer them on their own terms.

Moh-lee-yah Pahg-goo-nah asked him: who feeds on consciousness? The Buddha said: not a valid question. The valid question is: for what is consciousness a condition? And that he answered, with dependent arising.

Kahs-sah-pah asked: is suffering made by oneself? By another? By both? By neither, arising by chance? To each, the answer was: not so. And then, dependent arising.

Vahch-chah-goht-tah asked where a fire goes when it goes out. North? South? East? West? The answer: that does not apply. The fire burned dependent on grass and sticks. When they are used up, there is no where, for it to go.

Asked, is there a self? The Buddha was silent. Asked, is there no self? Silent again. A yes would side with the eternalists. A no, with the annihilationists.

To Kahch-chah-nah he said: the world mostly leans on two notions. It exists, and it does not exist. Seeing arising and ceasing as they are, neither takes hold. The middle is dependent arising.

And he named four ways of meeting a question. Some are answered directly. Some by making distinctions. Some with a counter question. And some are set aside.

Each time, the move is the same. The frame of the question is the problem. So the answer changes the question. From who, or what is it in itself, to: dependent on what does it arise?

So vivid, not found, and dependently arisen is not an answer of the form: it is X. It names no nature. It shows how what appears appears. And why the question of what it is in itself was never needed.

The question is not answered. It falls away. And when it falls away, nothing needs to fill its place. Not even a void.

And something changes when this is seen. Each appearance is met as it is. Vivid, and passing. With nothing in it to hold, and no one behind it holding.

That is not a loss of the world. It is the world, without the weight.

This is Section 2, Part 1 of the Dependent Arising series.

In Part 2, I follow the habit when it retreats. Shown that the sound does not last, it says: surely each instant is real. Shown that the flower comes apart, it says: surely the smallest pieces are real. And if the pieces fail, surely space itself is.

I look for that floor, in time, in matter and in space. With Nah-gar-joo-nah, Vah-soo-bun-doo, and Shahn-tah-rahk-shee-tah.
