# Four Videos on the Rainbow Body: Why All Four

A body that shrinks to hair and nails. Rainbows over a small house in Tibet. It is a story that catches the imagination. And that is exactly the danger. The rainbow body is easy to dismiss, and just as easy to inflate.

This is After Anatta. For many years the rainbow body has been one of my interests. To read it in a balanced way, neither dismissing it nor inflating it, I looked into four things, and I made a video on each. No one of them is enough on its own. In this short video I want to say why they belong together, and in what order I would watch them.

The first is about background. Five Elements: Chinese versus Tibet.

When we hear the words five lights, we bring our own world with us. Light, for us, is photons and waves. A body of light sounds like physics, or like science fiction. But the five lights of the rainbow body come from India and Tibet, and from a very different way of speaking. There, the five elements are not five kinds of stuff. Earth is hardness. Water is cohesion. Fire is warmth. Wind is motion. Space is room. They are qualities of what appears.

And Tibet keeps two different fives side by side. One came from India, with Buddhism. The other came from China, as divination and the calendar. They meet in history. They never merge in view. Without this background, the five lights are read through our own culture, and they go wrong from the start.

The second video is about the practices of the body. Inner Winds and Subtle Body Practices.

Somatic practices are important. The habit of taking things as solid and real on their own side does not live only in ideas. It lives in the muscles, the diaphragm and the breath. You can feel it as a slight tightening behind the eyes, a held, high chest, a guarded belly, a leaning forward, as if you had to hold the world together. If emptiness stays a deduction in the head, the body goes on bracing underneath.

The street between insight and the body runs both ways. Insight frees bound energy. In my own experience, as the logic took apart what the mind was still leaning on, the mind and the body's energy opened by themselves. And a balanced, supple body lets the insight sink into the bones. When the breath is settled low in the belly, and the body is not locked in alarm or pulled up into the head, there is the quiet pliancy to notice clinging as it forms.

The traditions also agree on how. Balancing is settling, not forcing. The breath sinks below the navel. The posture is upright but not stiff. Movement brings attention back down into the hips and the feet. Forcing the breath, or pulling everything up to the crown, has left many sleepless and unsettled. These practices are a re-balancing, never an endurance test, and the forceful ones belong only with a qualified teacher.

The third video is the traditions in their own words. Rainbow Body, According to Reports: What the Traditions Report.

Here I only report. Dzogchen, with its two practices, trekchö, cutting through, and thögal, direct crossing. The four visions, and the three results the tradition names. Gelug, with its illusory body made of wind, and tukdam. The Kālacakra's empty form. Bön. Other traditions that tell of a body of light. And the reports themselves: what was seen, what was told, and what has never been checked.

The fourth video is about another kind of knowing. The Body's Own Knowing: Felt Sense, Inner Winds and the Rainbow Body.

Knowing is not only analysis and the taking apart of concepts. There is a more immediate knowing, before words: the felt sense of presence in the body. A dog barks behind you, and your shoulders have jumped before you know what it is. Fear and clinging are not only beliefs. They are bracing, held breath and contraction, and they are often understood, and loosened, more directly there. The inner-wind traditions are long recognitions of this side of knowing, and modern research supports it, within limits.

The limits matter. Direct is not the same as right. The body's knowing is shaped by old expectations, and in anxiety it gives false alarms. It needs refining, as analysis does. And it is not a deeper floor beneath experience. It is more immediate, not more fundamental, and as empty as what it knows.

For the rainbow body, this is the point. The body takes part in realization. That is why the traditions speak of realization reaching all the way into the body. That the body itself turns into light remains reported, not shown.

So this is the order I would watch them in. First the background, so the words are heard in their own culture. Then the practices of the body. Then the reports, so we know what the traditions hold, and how far the stories go. And then the body's own knowing, so the body is understood as part of knowing, not only something to be trained.

Each one guards against a different mistake. Without the background, the lights become modern light. Without the practices, the view stays an idea in the head. Without the reports, the stories are taken for facts, or dismissed out of hand. Without the body's own knowing, the body is left out of realization altogether.

Together they give a balanced reading. Neither dismissing the rainbow body, nor inflating it.
