# Four Videos on the Rainbow Body: Why All Four

A body that shrinks to hair and nails. Rainbows over a small house in Tibet. It is a story that catches the imagination. And that is exactly the danger. The rainbow body is easy to hear in a way that has nothing to do with what the traditions meant, or with what I mean.

This is After Anatta. I have made four videos that belong together. No one of them is enough on its own. In this short video I want to say why, and in what order I would watch them.

The first is about background. Five Elements: Chinese versus Tibet.

When we hear the words five lights, we bring our own world with us. Light, for us, is photons and waves. A body of light sounds like physics, or like science fiction. But the five lights of the rainbow body come from India and Tibet, and from a very different way of speaking. There, the five elements are not five kinds of stuff. Earth is hardness. Water is cohesion. Fire is warmth. Wind is motion. Space is room. They are qualities of what appears.

And Tibet keeps two different fives side by side. One came from India, with Buddhism. The other came from China, as divination and the calendar. They meet in history. They never merge in view. Without this background, the five lights are read through our own culture, and they go wrong from the start.

The second video is about the body. Inner Winds and Subtle Body Practices.

I want to give this one its full place. Somatic practices are important. The habit of taking things as solid and real on their own side does not live only in ideas. It lives in the muscles, the diaphragm and the breath. You can feel it as a slight tightening behind the eyes, a held, high chest, a guarded belly, a leaning forward, as if you had to hold the world together. If emptiness stays a deduction in the head, the body goes on bracing underneath.

The street between insight and the body runs both ways. Insight frees bound energy. In my own experience, as the logic took apart what the mind was still leaning on, the mind and the body's energy opened by themselves. And a balanced, supple body lets the insight sink into the bones. When the breath is settled low in the belly, and the body is not locked in alarm or pulled up into the head, there is the quiet pliancy to notice clinging as it forms, and to let the view soak through our emotional and bodily reflexes.

The traditions also agree on how. Balancing is settling, not forcing. The breath sinks below the navel. The posture is upright but not stiff. Movement brings attention back down into the hips and the feet. Forcing the breath, or pulling everything up to the crown, has left many sleepless and unsettled. These practices are a re-balancing, never an endurance test, and the forceful ones belong only with a qualified teacher.

So the rainbow body is not only a matter of thinking clearly. The body is where the old habit is held, and where the release is felt.

The third video is the traditions in their own words. Rainbow Body, According to Reports: What the Traditions Report.

Here I keep my own view out. Dzogchen, with its two practices, trekchö, cutting through, and thögal, direct crossing. The four visions, and the three results the tradition names. Gelug, with its illusory body made of wind, and tukdam. The Kālacakra's empty form. Bön. And other traditions that tell of a body of light. Then the reports themselves: what was seen, what was told, and what has never been checked.

This matters for balance too. Without it, my view can be mistaken for what the traditions say. And what the traditions say can be mistaken for proof.

The fourth video is my own understanding. Rainbow Body, My Personal View: Nothing Turns Into Light.

There I say how I see it, from a non-substantialist standpoint. Luminosity as luminous appearances: six lamps, not one. What we call things are only the six lamps, unrecognised, and that holds for the body too. The rainbow body, in essence, as an actualized realization. And where I part from the traditions, and why.

So this is the order I would watch them in. First the background, so the words are heard in their own culture. Then the body, so the practice is not left in the head. Then the reports, so we know what the traditions hold, and how far the stories go. And last, my view, read against all three.

Each one guards against a different mistake. Without the background, the lights become modern light. Without the body, the view stays an idea. Without the reports, my view is taken for the tradition, and the stories for facts. Without the view, the reports are just wonders.

Together they give a balanced picture. Not a goal to chase. A way to understand what is being pointed at, and what is not.
