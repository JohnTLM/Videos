# The Limits of the Tools

Listen to rain on a roof. Every drop is different. The sound swells, thins, and changes as the wind changes. It is vivid, and it never holds still.

Now try to say exactly what you heard. The words come out flat. Rain. A sound. Loud, then soft. What you heard was far richer than that.

This is After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness.

The difficulty in presenting this view is not mainly in the view. It is in the tools. Words, concepts and drawings were made for daily life. Lifting a cup. Catching a bus. Keeping a promise. For that they work well. A cup must stay a cup long enough to be filled. A friend must be the same friend tomorrow.

But turned on what actually appears, the tools are clumsy. And there is a worse problem. A tool that cannot show something does not stay silent. It shows something else instead, shaped like the tool. Not knowing the limits of our tools shapes what we present.

Three things make the trouble plain. What appears is vivid and moving. It is not found when looked for. And it follows its conditions. It is not haphazard.

Start with the vividness. A concept works by picking out: this, not that. It gives a thing edges and keeps it the same. But no sound is ever the same sound twice. A wall is one colour at noon and another at dusk. A tool made for picking out steady things cannot show what never stands still.

Here is the first trap. It is tempting to say: underneath the concepts there is raw, vivid experience, and the concepts are a thin layer on top. That brings the floor back. There is no bare layer waiting to be named. Whatever appears is already picked out as this, before any word. So the poverty of the tools is not a gap between words and a truer world behind them. The tools fit their job and hold much less than what is there. And the actual mistake starts only when a word is taken to have caught the thing itself.

Now the conditions. The bell rings when it is struck, not otherwise. Rice seed gives rice. This order is what makes the world workable.

But as soon as we say it, cause and effect turns into production. First the conditions, then something made out of them, like goods from a factory.

Nagarjuna refuses exactly that. Cause has three senses, and only one is refused. A cause that exists on its own and hands over an effect is refused. Conditions that come together, so that a result appears, are kept. That works. And the cause is the part attention picks out when it lays a process out in order. That is all a cause is.

So show the conditions as the shape of the appearing, not a machine behind it. A bell is struck. Bronze, the strike, the air, the ear. The ringing is not made by these and then sent out. The ringing is their meeting, vivid and complete. Crack the bell, add wind, change the ear, and it is not the same sound altered. It is a different vividness.

Verbs help. The wind is blowing: there is no wind apart from the blowing. It is raining: there is no it that rains.

Dzogchen says appearing is spontaneous presence, beyond cause and effect. Heard with the old habits, this swings to the other mistake. If appearing is not produced, perhaps it comes from nowhere, or flows out of a ground.

Keep two questions apart. How does this appearance come about? Through conditions. What is the nature of what appears? Emptiness, which is not produced, so why does not apply to it. Beyond cause and effect answers the second question. It never cancels the first.

Spontaneous means already complete. Complete because nothing is hidden behind it, not because nothing conditions it. It is dependent arising without a producer. And the last question, why does anything appear at all, is not answered by a hidden source. It simply loses its object.

Now the hardest part. The framework assumes, without saying so, that to be present is to be something, somewhere. So how can something so clearly present not be found at all?

Do not argue from outside. Search exactly as the framework says a present thing should be found. Is the sound in the bell? In the air? In the ear? In the mind? In all of them together? Apart from them? Each search fails, by the framework's own standard. The contradiction belongs to the premise, not to the view.

Each not closes a door. Not here, not there: here and there are named in relation to each other. Not in my mind: the mind is searched the same way, and is not found either. Not in another's mind. And not even nowhere. Having failed to find the thing, the mind quietly makes its absence into a place, a void, a background. But the search removes the assumed thing and puts nothing in its place. Where is it, really? That question, like the last why, has nothing left to hold.

And this is not despite the vividness. If the sound had a fixed nature of its own, it could not ring differently in wind, or from a cracked bell. What is vivid and answers to its conditions is exactly what cannot be found as a fixed thing. Vivid and not found are one appearing, seen from two sides.

Careful here. Emptiness does not do anything. It is not a power that makes vividness possible. Appearing simply never needed an essence.

A rainbow is vivid. Everyone sees it in the same place, and a camera records it. Walk to where it is, and nothing is there. But the rainbow only opens the door. The cup, the hand and the mind meet the same search, and work just as fully.

Now the third trap, and it catches people who have passed the first two. Appearing follows conditions, so surely what is there is relations. A web. A network. The things are not found, but surely the connections are.

The tools push us here. Try to draw a vivid, unfindable appearing in changing conditions. A diagram has only dots and lines. Dots for things, lines for relations. So it can be read only two ways. The dots are what is there, and the lines join them. Or the lines are what is there, and the dots are only where lines cross. Things as the floor, or the web as the floor.

The drawing does more on its own. It sets the conditions around the appearance, so they look like outside causes. It holds still, so the movement is lost. And someone looks at the whole web from outside. The onlooker that anatta took away is quietly put back.

Nagarjuna searched the connections as closely as the things. Contact, coming into contact, and the one who brings them into contact are not found. The line is as unfindable as the dot. Relation and the things related are named together, and neither is the ground. Nor is the order of conditions a hidden set of laws behind things. The order is fully valid. It works, and it can be relied on. It is not a floor.

So there is no presentation free of tools. Every presentation picks out this rather than that, so whatever it shows comes already cut into pieces and links. That is not a fault to fix. The fix is to know it, and to say so. A mapmaker cannot draw a round earth on a flat page without distortion. A good one says which distortion was chosen, and why.

Here is how to use the tools anyway. Say what the tool cannot do, while using it. Show dependence by change, not by maps. Strike the bell harder, crack it, add wind: each time a different vividness. Start inside the one appearing, and treat its conditions as its own detail. If you draw, then undo the drawing. Search a dot: not found. Search a line: not found. Search for the one looking at the picture: not found. What remains is not a blank, and not a web. It is the bell, ringing.

One word carries the whole difficulty. Real. It says that something works, and it denies that anything stands on its own. One word for two opposite jobs. So say validly functioning where it affirms, and not found where it denies.

Listen to the rain again. Vivid. Changing with the wind. Following its conditions. Not found anywhere, not even nowhere. The words were never meant to hold it. They only point, and then step aside.
