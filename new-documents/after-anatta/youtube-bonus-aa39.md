## Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 1: Vivid, and Not Found (AA39, 13:33)

### Title

Dependent Arising Section 2 Part 1: Vivid, and Not Found | Thusness

### Description

What is it that appears? This sound, this red, this thought. Section 1 showed that no finished world waits underneath; Section 2 asks what any single appearance is. The usual word is "phenomenon", but the Abhidharma used it for an element carrying its own mark, and that is exactly what the search does not find. So I simply say: what appears.

Two ways of seeing, three times. A temple bell: look for the sound in the bell, the air, the ear and the mind, and it is not found, yet it is as clear as before. A red flower: at noon, at dusk, under a street lamp, for a bee; the red is dependent in the very moment it is red, and designating, meeting and appearing are one event, through and through. A passing thought: "I forgot to lock the door." The mind is searched the same way and is not found either. Vivid, not found, dependently arisen: one fact, seen three ways. And "not found" is not a void.

Then the question itself. "What is it?" is a good everyday question. Asked as "what is it in itself?", it assumes an "it", the given slipping back in through grammar. The Buddha met such questions again and again and changed the question rather than answering it:
- SN 12.12, Moḷiya Phagguna: "Who feeds on consciousness?" Not a valid question. https://suttacentral.net/sn12.12
- SN 12.17, Kassapa: suffering made by oneself, by another, both, neither? Not so. https://suttacentral.net/sn12.17
- MN 72, Vacchagotta: where does the fire go when it goes out? It does not apply. https://suttacentral.net/mn72
- SN 44.10: "Is there a self?" "Is there no self?" Silence. https://suttacentral.net/sn44.10
- SN 12.15, Kaccāna: neither "it exists" nor "it does not exist"; the middle. https://suttacentral.net/sn12.15
- AN 4.42: four ways of meeting a question; some are set aside. https://suttacentral.net/an4.42

The question is not answered. It falls away, and nothing needs to fill its place.
