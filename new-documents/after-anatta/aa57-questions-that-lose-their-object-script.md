# Dependent Arising Section 4 Part 2: Questions That Lose Their Object

When did it start? Where did it go? Is it the same one? Each question asks where a thing is.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Part 1 set two logics side by side. The logic of substance starts with things, and asks what happens to them. The logic of dependency asks how anything is identified as a thing at all. And the famous refusals, neither existent nor non existent, turned out to be a failed presupposition. No bearer, so neither answer.

Here I turn the logic of dependency on the questions we ask about anything.

Nāgārjuna opens his Middle Way by paying homage to dependent arising, as free of eight extremes, in four pairs. Arising and ceasing. Permanence and annihilation. Coming and going. Identity and difference.

Under the logic of substance, these are not extremes at all. They are simply the questions one asks about anything. When did it start? When will it end? Does it last, or is it destroyed? Where did it come from, and where did it go? Is it the same as that, or different?

Every one of these assumes the same thing. That there is something with whereabouts. A position in time, in space, or in identity, belonging to it from its own side.

Arising and ceasing place it at the two ends of a line. Permanence keeps it on the line, and annihilation removes it. Coming and going move it from one place to another. Identity and difference set it beside something else, to be compared.

As I see it, the eight extremes are not eight separate errors. They are eight ways of asking where a thing is.

The logic of dependency does not answer these questions in a subtler way. It does not say the thing arises invisibly, lasts in a hidden form, or goes somewhere we cannot see. It says that what was supposed to have whereabouts is not found. And so the questions lose their object.

When did it come into being? No thing is found crossing an edge, from absent to present. It began at noon still holds, as convention.

What stays the same underneath? No carrier is found beneath the change.

Where did it go? No whereabouts are found after. The question does not apply.

Is it the same as that, or something else entirely? The sprout is not the seed. And yet rice yields rice. Not identical. Not wholly other.

Switch off a lamp. The room is dark, and the light went nowhere. Switch it on. The room fills, and the light came from nowhere. Both are true of every appearance, and both were true all along. Nothing has been taken from the lamp. Only the question, where did the light go, has nothing left to be about.

Now the decisive point. It is easy to miss, and for me it is the heart of this part.

Dependent arising is not one thing, and the eight negations another. As if what arises in dependence were then also, mysteriously, free of arising. The negations describe dependent arising itself.

If a sprout could be found arising from its seed, it would have an arising of its own. And then it would not need to depend. That it depends is exactly why no arising is found, when it is sought. Dependence and unfindability are one fact. Seen from the side of convention, and from the side of analysis.

So I do not hold emptiness in one hand, and dependent arising in the other. It is one hand.

Once the things are seen through, a new temptation comes. Keep the relations. There are no things, but everything is connected. The web is what is real.

But the relations are named together with what they relate. A connection between two things that have no standing of their own is not a third thing with standing. Looked for, the web is not found on its own side either. The relating is as empty as what it relates. And it works exactly as well.

Now, identity.

Under the logic of substance, what is it, and is it the same, have answers hidden in the thing itself. A thing is what its essence makes it, and it stays the same thing as long as the essence stays.

The logic of dependency moves the question. Identity is not what a thing is in itself. It is how the thing is reliably picked out. Is it the same? becomes: the same by which criteria, for which purpose, within which framework?

A cracked phone is the same phone, for the repair shop and for the insurer. A ship whose every plank has been replaced is the same ship on the register, and perhaps not for the museum that kept the old planks. The child in an old photograph is you, for your family, your birth certificate, and the story that runs from that afternoon to this one.

Each answer is correct within its framework. None is found in the thing, apart from the framework.

This does not make identity arbitrary. Criteria can be applied rightly or wrongly. A forged passport fails. A mistaken face in a crowd is corrected when it turns. What has gone is only the further claim, that beneath the criteria there is a sameness from the thing's own side.

Identity seemed the most undeniable thing there was. I am I. This is this. Seen this way, it is not less real. Its reality is of a different kind. The reality of a designation that works.

The same shift changes the word conventional.

The logic of substance hears it as a demotion. Agreed on. Perhaps arbitrary. Certainly less than real. And so the path must lead from the conventional to something truer behind it.

For the logic of dependency, conventional truth is the only kind of validity there is, and it is fully valid. It has standards. Tsongkhapa gives three. What is known to ordinary cognition. Not overturned by further ordinary cognition. And not contradicted by analysis of the ultimate. The snake in the rope fails the second test. The inherently existing rope fails the third. The rope, as named, passes all three.

Convention is not the opposite of truth. It is where truth, in every usable sense, lives.

And there is no second, deeper truth for convention to fall short of. The ultimate is not a higher world. It is the emptiness of this one. There is one appearance, seen two ways.

Next time one of these questions comes, notice its shape, before trying to answer it.

Where did that feeling go? Look for the place it went.

Is this the same person I married? Ask, the same by which criteria, for which purpose?

Is this only conventional? Ask what you hoped would stand behind it. And look for that.

This is Section 4, Part 2 of the Dependent Arising series.

The question does not need a cleverer answer. It needs to be seen, for what it assumes.
