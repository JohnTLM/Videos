# Dependent Arising Part 2: The Illusion of the Given

One bowl of liquid. A human sees water. A hungry ghost sees burning pus. A fish meets its home. A god meets nectar.

Which one is really in the bowl?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

In Part 1, a human, a bat, a bee and an ant stood before the same mountain. Their worlds did not match.

Even so, one belief usually survives. Surely something is there underneath. One finished world. And each creature only sees it through its own glasses. One world, many pictures of it.

That belief is what I call the given.

It is not a theory I hold. It is how things seem, before any thought. The world is already there, complete. And seeing only reports it.

So again, I will not argue first. I will set two ways of seeing side by side, three more times. Then we can ask which one holds.

First, a bowl.

Indian and Tibetan philosophers used this one bowl to bring the question into focus. A hungry ghost, in their telling, is a being consumed by craving and lack. Where I see water, it sees pus and blood.

Surely, then, something sits in the bowl before any of them looks. Call it H two O. Bare matter. Neutral stuff. It is undeniably there, and each kind of being puts its own glasses on it.

The water is real. The pus and the nectar are just ways of seeing it.

Or, and it is a big or, look at how each of these is formed.

The water forms with a human body, a human thirst, a human tongue. The pus forms with a ghost's craving. The home forms with gills and fins.

The thing seen, the one who sees, and the channel of meeting are designated together. None of them is a finished layer underneath the others.

Then what is in the bowl before anyone meets it? Ask what it is like.

If it has features of its own, say it is water, then why does the ghost meet pus? Its features should show through.

If it has no features at all, then a featureless something cannot be what any of them meet. Nothing with no qualities can set off the taste of water, or the burn of pus.

A careful mind falls back one step. Not water, and not pus. Just wetness. The moisture common to both.

But I have never met wetness on its own. It only ever shows up as this drinkable water. This burning pus. This home for a fish.

Wetness by itself is an idea drawn from those meetings. It is not a stuff found under them. As a working word for comparing notes, wetness is fine. As a hidden floor, it is the old belief in an essence, wearing new clothes.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. I still drink the water. It still quenches my thirst.

Second, a tick at a pool.

The biologist Jakob von Uexküll showed that a tick lives in a world of three signals. The smell of mammal sweat. The warmth of a body. And hair.

A clear pool of spring water sends none of them.

Surely the pool is still there for the tick, whether it knows it or not. It is undeniably objective. The tick is simply blind to it.

Or, and again it is a big or, look at how the meeting is formed.

The pool has no place in the tick's world of smell, warmth and hair. It is not hidden from the tick. It never forms there.

Yet if the tick falls in, the water holds it up and carries it away. So the pool is not nothing to the tick either.

The tick is cut off from the channels of sight and recognition. And it is fully joined to the water's surface, and its current.

That is the point. Contact runs along channels. And it comes in degrees.

Physics shows the same. Dark matter, as far as anyone can tell, passes through the Earth without a bump, because it does not meet light. Yet the Earth's mass still bends its path.

A neutrino passes through a wall of lead as if through mist. For one system, the lead is a wall. For another, it is almost open space.

Solidity is not stored in the rock. It is a resistance that forms when two systems that share the right coupling meet.

So the choice is not between a hidden object out there, and nothing at all. What appears, a wall, a drink, a burn, or open space, depends on which channels meet.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. The current still carries the tick, whether or not the tick ever meets a pool.

Third, the box.

Why does the given feel so certain? Because underneath the bowl and the pool sits a picture I rarely notice.

Space is an empty box, ready-made. I put a solid object in the middle of it. I put observers around it. Then each one either sees the real object, or is fooled.

Surely the box, at least, is given. It was there before anything was put in it. It would still be there if everything were taken out.

Or, and it is the biggest or, look at how space itself is formed.

In Einstein's general relativity, spacetime is not an empty stage. It is a field that bends with mass, and moves with energy.

And long before Einstein, Nāgārjuna asked whether space could exist before its own mark: the absence of obstruction. It cannot. Space and what is in it are designated together.

Without the box, the puzzle of the bowl loses its grip. There is no fixed place where the one real liquid sits, waiting to be seen rightly or wrongly.

Space, time, the one who looks, and what is looked at all come together. None of them contains the rest.

And even seen this way, nothing is missing. I still walk across the room. The cup is still where I left it.

But we agree.

That is the strongest reason for the given. You and I both see water in the bowl. We name it, pour it, drink it, and our reports match. Does that not prove one real liquid behind us both?

The Indian philosopher Candrakīrti answered no.

Shared, reliable agreement is not proof of a hidden floor. It is what conventional truth is.

Within one kind of being, healthy senses agree, and correct each other. A human with jaundice sees a white conch shell as yellow. Other humans see it white. So by human standards, the jaundiced eye is mistaken.

But the hungry ghost is not a jaundiced human. By the standards of its own kind, its senses work normally. For it, the liquid really does work as pus.

Is that circular? Healthy senses are the ones that agree with the group. And the group is those who share the senses.

Yes. It is a circle. A circle is fatal only if something must rest on a judge standing outside every view. No such judge is found.

Inside the circle, mistakes are still caught. A second look corrects a misreading. A healthy eye corrects a jaundiced one. What the circle rules out is only a correction from nowhere.

The bowl story did not begin with Candrakīrti. Vasubandhu used the ghosts to argue that there are no outer things at all. Shared karma, ripening in the mind, is enough to explain why the ghosts all see one river of pus.

That makes the mind the floor instead.

Candrakīrti turned the story around. If the pus is not found standing on its own, neither is the mind that sees it. They rise and fall together.

So the bowl does not prove that the world is inside the mind. It shows that neither the world nor the mind is the floor.

What is left in the bowl, then? A working reference point. This bowl is a handy label, made from the human side. It lets different beings' appearances be lined up and compared. Its job is to coordinate the views, not to sit underneath them.

Two ways of seeing, three times over, and agreement tested as well. Which carries more valid truth?

I test them as I did in Part 1. By experience. By logic. And by how things work.

By experience. Look into a glass of water, honestly. What is met? Coolness. Clearness. Wetness on the lip. The name.

At no point do I meet a neutral liquid behind them. One that is neither my water, nor the fish's home, nor the ghost's pus. That liquid is never found. It is supplied.

Then turn around, and look for a mind holding the water inside it. That is not found either. Mind and object come together.

By logic. A given world would have to be like something. If it has features, they should show through to every being. They do not. If it has none, it cannot set off any meeting at all.

A self-standing wetness, or matter, undoes itself the moment it is picked out. Picking it out needs contrast, a channel and a name. The very dependencies it was meant to escape.

By working. The given view says the world is orderly because something solid underneath holds it steady. But what is fixed cannot change. And what cannot change cannot act.

The formed view explains the working fully. Barley seeds grow barley. Fire burns. The current carries the tick. Humans agree that this is water. These are dependently arisen patterns, meeting other patterns reliably, along shared channels.

Order never needed a floor.

So the given is not the truer view. It is the default one. It carries the weight of habit, and of language. The noun, the bowl, quietly sets up one object behind every view.

The formed view carries experience and logic both.

None of this makes my everyday claims false. The water is water. I still drink it, and I still say so. What fails is only the extra claim: a finished world underneath, waiting to be seen.

And one thing matters more than the argument.

If the ghost's pus is truly pus for the ghost, its suffering is not an optical mistake, to be waved away from the human side. No single view owns the real world. So no being's lived world can be brushed aside.

Seeing through the given takes nothing away from another's pain. Or from the wish to ease it.
