# The Two Truths

This moment ceases as it arises. Does it arise, or does it cease?

This is the fourth step of The Logic, from After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness. The koan has two answers, and they do not conflict.

Conventionally, the moment arises and ceases. The bell is struck, the ringing stops, and we can say when. Looked for, nothing that arises and nothing that ceases is found. The tradition calls these the two truths. They are not two worlds, and not two levels. There is one moment, met two ways.

Nagarjuna introduces them at the turning point of his work. The Buddhas teach by relying on two truths: the conventional truth of the world, and the truth in the ultimate sense. Miss the difference, he says, and the deep teaching is missed.

The old word for "conventional" carries three meanings. Concealment: ordinary seeing hides how things are, by presenting them as existing from their own side. Dependence: what is conventional exists only in dependence on other things. And worldly convention: the names and agreements we live by. Conventional truth is valid as dependence and as convention. It is mistaken only in the concealment. That habit is the real target.

Listen to a bell. We can say when it was struck, and when it stopped. Now look for the ringing apart from its sounding, its conditions and its name. Nothing is found. Nagarjuna opens his whole work this way: without ceasing, without arising, without coming and without going. "Without arising" is not a denial that the bell rang. Not found never means did not happen.

Seen with the logic of substance, the ringing is a thing, and arising and ceasing happen to it. So the two answers seem to compete. If analysis finds nothing, the ringing must be unreal. Seen with the logic of dependency, the ringing is merely designated as arising or ceasing, upon conditions. "Arising" is picked out against the silence before, "ceasing" against the silence after. Both are correct, like the stairs of Step 1, up for someone climbing and down for someone coming down. Nothing competes.

Walk into a garage at dusk. In the corner something lies coiled, and for a moment it is a snake. Someone switches on the lamp. It is a rope. The fright goes. You pick the rope up and tie down the load on the car. Later, you look for the rope apart from its strands, its twist, its use and its name. You do not find one. The load stays tied.

Two corrections happened there, and they were not the same kind. The lamp corrected the snake into a rope. The search corrected nothing about the rope. It only removed the idea of a rope standing there from its own side. The koan's "looked for, not found" is the second kind.

Most of us carry a picture of reality in two storeys. In one version, the sounds we meet are a surface, and particles do the real work backstage. In the spiritual version, the passing world is a veil, and behind it is the Absolute: a pure Being, a Source, a cosmic Awareness.

The author lived in the second picture for years. The realisation of I AM was real. And it gave him exactly this shape: a pure, unchanging Witness on one side, and a world that came and went before it. The Witness was the deeper floor. The world was the show.

The two truths leave no room for either version. No hidden substrate. No deeper dimension underneath, and no higher one floating above. In the author's words: "Appearance is not a cover over something more real. There is nothing behind it for it to cover." The two truths are not a map of two places. They are two ways of meeting one place.

"Conventional" sounds like a demotion. But that is the logic of substance speaking. Conventional truth is fully valid. A table, a rainbow, a thought, a moment: all thoroughly real, conventionally.

And convention has standards. Tsongkhapa gives three checks. Known to ordinary cognition. Not overturned by another sound ordinary cognition. Not overturned by analysis of how things ultimately are. The snake fails the second, because the lamp shows a rope. A rope existing from its own side fails the third. The rope as named, in dependence on its strands, passes all three. The everyday world can tell a rope from a snake, and it must.

Ultimate truth is not a hidden particle, not a pure Awareness behind the world, and not a timeless Now. When any conventional thing is looked for apart from what it depends on, no own-nature is found. In the author's words: "Ultimate truth is not a higher world. It is the emptiness of this one." And it gets no exemption. Single it out as "the ultimate", and it too is designated, and empty. There is no level that stays.

The two truths need each other. Without relying on convention, the ultimate cannot be taught. Convention is the ladder, and it is not thrown away at the top, because there is no top. Taken apart, each truth turns into a fixed object. Joined, they dissolve each other's solidity.

Mipham draws the line another way. Meet the cold of ice as a cold thing over there and a feeler over here, and the experience is discordant. Meet the same coldness without that fixing, and it is concordant. Nothing different appears. What differs is whether the appearance is misread. After anatta, presence did not fade into some deeper truth. It became more vivid, because it was no longer pushed behind experience. What changed was how it was held.

Raise one hand and lower the other. For the raised hand, time passes a little faster. Carlo Rovelli once showed this with two watches, one held high and one low. The best clocks can measure it over a height of about thirty centimetres. GPS satellites sit high and move fast, so their clocks run about thirty-eight millionths of a second a day ahead. Left uncorrected, your map would drift by about ten kilometres a day. Your watch is right, and so is the correction. Looked for, no single time is found apart from where the clock is and how it moves. Two truths, and the map still gets you home.

Every thing has the koan's two answers. The self: "I wrote this" is correct, and it can be checked. Looked for, no I is found apart from the body, the mind and the history it is named on. Not a false self with a true one hiding behind. One self, met two ways.

A ship whose every plank has been replaced still sails. Looked for, no ship is found apart from its planks, its use and its name. Fire burns, and a hand put in it is hurt. Yet no power to burn is found in the fire on its own. And the friend you love keeps promises, and grieves. Looked for, they are not found as a self standing on its own. Seeing that takes nothing from the love. Love, sorrow and care stay where they were.

Try it. Stand on a scale and read the number. That answer is correct. Then look for the weight in your body alone, apart from the earth that pulls it. Notice that the second look takes nothing from the first.

Recall a coat on a chair, taken for a person. Notice how cleanly the second look corrected the first. Convention has standards.

One weight, met two ways. And nothing extra behind it.
