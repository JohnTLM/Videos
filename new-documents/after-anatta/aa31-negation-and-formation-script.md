# Negation and Formation: The Two Halves

Over the past months my articles have taken up one question after another. Cause without causal power. Continuity without a carrier. How identities form in the meeting. What a phenomenon is.

Looking back over them, I see one shape running through all of them.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

There are two halves to the work. One half is negation: seeing that nothing is found from its own side. The other half is formation: understanding how a world without essence still appears. Vivid. Stable. Working.

Start with negation, and with what it has to face.

One of the most important turns in leaving a substantialist view is this. The things, the substrates, and everything I took as given, undeniable and there from its own side, turn out to be frameworks. I built them. And I grew so used to them that I never questioned them.

Substantialism is not mainly a theory anyone chooses to believe. It is an innate, default way of perceiving. It assumes that separate nouns exist first, from their own side, and enter into relations only afterwards.

Ordinary grammar supports this every time I speak. The wind blows. The person walks. Each sentence slips in a fixed bearer, standing behind the action. Because the habit is so reliable, the act of naming disappears into what is named. A convention feels like a fact standing outside any framework.

Direct experience is never like that. The flashing is the lightning. The blowing is the wind. No seer stands behind the seeing.

Nor is there a raw, unnamed given lying underneath my concepts, waiting to be labelled. Vividness and naming are mixed through and through. There is no seam between them, and no unnamed floor at the bottom.

Modern physics points in a similar direction. Again and again, what was supposed to be the bottom layer turns out to be defined by interaction and relation, rather than by fixed properties of its own.

But this view does not rest on physics, and does not need it as proof. Physical theories are themselves valid frameworks. Reliable within their domains. Open to revision.

More basically, a fixed nature does not hold together. Anything proposed as standing on its own undermines itself at once. To pick it out at all needs contrast, context and naming: the very dependencies it claims to escape. And a thing with a fixed, independent nature could never depend on conditions. It could never change. It could never interact.

Leaving this habit is not a one-time change of opinion. The old picture comes back quietly the moment attention is off duty. In my own experience, three strands have to work together, again and again, each checking the others.

The first strand: confirm it in direct experience.

Agreeing with dependency in theory changes very little. It has to be seen directly, many times, until it is no longer just an idea. For me this began with anatta, no-self: looking for a seer behind the seeing, a hearer behind the hearing, and finding none. Then it went on with ordinary objects. A phone. A cup. A tree.

But experience alone has a limit. Experience can become deeply non-dual. A taste of clarity, of presence with no centre. And yet the framework that reads it can stay substantialist. Then the habit quietly turns a genuine taste back into a hidden ground. Solid things are simply replaced by a subtler substrate. A universal Awareness. A Presence with a capital P.

The second strand: equip the view with tools.

Analytical tools bring thinking in line with what experience has already shown. Some are simple and easy to carry, like the chariot of the monk Nāgasena. Look for the chariot in the wheels, the axle, the frame, and it is not found there. Not in the parts, not apart from them. Some tools are thorough and exacting, like Candrakīrti's sevenfold analysis, and Nāgārjuna's Middle Way verses.

The analysis has to work as a pure negation. It removes a nature of its own, and puts nothing in its place. Otherwise the habit simply moves to a subtler level. A void. A dynamic process. A web of relations. Or a store of possibilities, quietly standing underneath.

The third strand: taste the gradual loosening.

Whatever is taken to exist from its own side is gripped with the tightness that solidity invites. As view and experience keep confirming each other, that grip loosens. Gradually. The emotional grasping, and the subtler grasping of the mind.

What is left is not a special state to be guarded. Trying to keep it would bring the fixing back. Instead, mind and matter lose their felt weight. They become light, vivid, and workable.

And here is the turn. Relentless negation alone is only half the story.

Negation targets a fixed nature, inside the old framework. If I stop there, I am left wondering. If these vivid appearances are not things, then what are they?

The other half is formation. How a world empty of essence still appears vivid, stable and workable.

In this view, a phenomenon is a vivid appearing that is not found when looked for, even while it appears so vividly. Every appearance has three features, at the same time.

It is vivid and clear. Nothing is dimmed or taken away. Its clarity belongs to the appearing itself. A sound is clear as sound. A colour is vivid as colour. No witness behind it is needed to light it up.

It is not found. Searched for, it has no core, no edge, no location. Not out there in the world. Not in here, in a private mind. And not even nowhere, since nowhere is just one more place.

And it is dependently arisen, and validly functioning. It shows up only when its conditions meet. And it works completely.

This third point is the one I have had to come back to most. At first it sounds so strange. Habit equates being there with being independent. It hears naming as a thin sticker on a thing already there. It pictures causes as one substance pushing another. So empty sounds flimsy. Ghostly.

But empty does not mean weak. A car with no essence can still injure you. Medicine still heals. A cup still holds hot tea.

Take an apple, tossed from hand to hand. At once, habit supplies the impression of a hard, heavy thing standing on its own.

Yet when I look closely, the apple is not found apart from the streams of consciousness. And it is not made by them either.

Hardness, coolness and weight arise only as touch meets skin and fruit. The red shifts to orange under candlelight, and to grey at dusk. It arises with light, eye and attention.

When the habitual knot is untied, nothing is missing. The apple can still be tossed. Still eaten. It still nourishes me. Its solidity and its effects arise because this vivid pattern meets hands, gravity and bodies, reliably.

One guardrail stays in place. Consciousness is not a new floor in here, producing the apple. Mind and matter are two cuts in the same vivid appearing. Neither is the substrate underneath the other. Both are named together.

The same two halves run through the rest of my recent work. Briefly, then.

First, cause. Strike a match, and slow the film down as far as you like. At every frame, something is happening. At no frame does a finished flame cross over from nowhere. And still, she lit the fire at eight is perfectly valid.

Causes work. Rice grows from rice seed. Bread without yeast stays flat. What is never found is a causal power: a force sitting inside the cause from its own side, handed across a gap to the effect.

So I keep three senses of cause apart. A producer with its own power: refused. Conditions coming together: kept. A link that attention singles out and names: kept too. Conditions are not a basement under the appearing. Conditions are the appearing, cut into parts for explaining, and for getting things done.

When no gap and no producer are found, the question of why anything appears at all is retired. What remains is called spontaneous presence. Not uncaused. Not a hidden source. Just dependent arising itself, met without the habit of fixing.

Second, continuity. If nothing persists from its own side, what carries me from this moment to the next?

The question rests on a gap that thought drew first. In the living moment it never comes up. Each moment is complete. And a carrier would never solve it anyway. Whatever links one moment to the carrier, and the carrier to the next, needs bridges of its own. Without end.

As Nāgārjuna says, whatever arises in dependence on something is neither that very thing, nor wholly other than it. Like a lamp burning through the night, continuity is named across moments that condition one another reliably. Nothing travels from one to the next.

Nothing is lost. Memory, learning, responsibility and karma hold. And nothing is fixed. Habits can loosen. Character can change.

Third, the words themselves. Both views use the same words, so the words have to be sorted. Heard by the logic of substance, dependent arising means things that already exist, linked in a vast web. Meant by the logic of dependency, it means things identified only in dependence. Relation and what is related are named together. Neither comes first.

Emptiness, heard the first way, is a blank void, or a hidden reservoir behind things. Meant the second way, it is simply this: no nature of its own is found, in appearances that are vivid and working. And the word real is retired. Working, conventionally. Not found from its own side, ultimately.

If things existed with fixed natures, nothing could ever meet. Two things with sealed edges could never touch. And if they merged, they would collapse into one.

In dependent arising, meeting means something else. The two sides define each other in one stroke. A hand touches ice. They meet, with no gap crossed, and no fusion. And the meeting itself is not a third thing.

So, with both halves in place, what has changed?

Nothing has been taken away from the world. And no new ground has been added.

The apple is still red. The car is still dangerous. The cup still holds hot tea. A friend's voice is still warm.

What has gone is only the floor that was never there.

What remains is not a final picture to defend, but the freedom of not needing one. Each appearance is met as it is. Vivid. Exact. Workable. Releasing itself as it arises.

Light and complete, with nothing to hold, and no one holding.
