# Nothing Turns Into Light: Luminous Clarity and the Rainbow Body

Listen for a moment. A car passes. A bird. The hum of a fan. Each sound is clear. Nothing had to switch on for it to be clear. It was clear as it came.

Now the old stories. A master dies in a small room in Tibet. Over a week the body grows smaller. Rainbows are seen over the roof. When the door is opened, only hair and nails are left.

What could the clearness of a passing sound have to do with a body turning into light? More than it seems. In this video I want to show you that link.

This is After Anatta. Here I bring together what I have written, across my books and articles, about two things. Non-dual luminous clarity. And the rainbow body. What I see is simple to say and hard to see. The rainbow body is luminous clarity, recognised all the way through form, the body included. And nothing turns into light.

Let me start with clarity.

When I say luminosity, I mean one thing. It is the knowing clarity of appearing itself. A sound is heard as sound. Its clearness is not found apart from the sound, the ear and the hearing. It is not a light shining on things from behind. It is not a knower standing apart to see them. And it is as empty as everything else.

Luminous does not mean bright. A dull ache is clear as dull. Darkness is seen, vividly, as dark. A grey wall is as vivid as a sunset, in the sense that matters here. Vivid means nothing in between. Not extra intensity. Just appearing, met directly.

And clarity is never missing. You cannot speak of anything appearing without clarity. The only question is whether the clarity is lifted out and made into a thing. Nāgārjuna has a verse about a lamp. A lamp lights itself and others, his opponent says. Nāgārjuna answers that in a lamp there is no darkness to remove. I read that verse simply. No appearing ever waited in the dark for a knower to light it up.

There is not even one clarity. There are six. Seeing is clear in its own way. Hearing in its own way. Touching, tasting, smelling, thinking. Six lamps, not one. And no lamp that lights itself.

So what goes wrong? The mind notices that every appearing is clear. It notices the sameness. Then it lifts that sameness out and calls it awareness. A background. A witness. A mirror. A ground. The clearness was real. Making it a thing is the error.

That error has many disguises. A lamp that is always on, behind everything. A lamp left burning through deep sleep. A lamp behind glass, watching. Or a lamp put out, so that emptiness becomes a kind of darkness. Each one keeps a light somewhere, standing on its own.

And there is a deeper version still. A subtle clear light, hidden under the coarse mind, like a lamp under a basket, waiting to be uncovered. I read it differently. Subtle means fewer conditions. It does not mean a deeper stuff. The coarse minds were never darkness covering a truer light. What they add is the knot of grasping, not a cover.

There is a name for this clearness that many practitioners know. I AM. For me, I AM names luminous clarity itself, realised directly. It is real. What turns it into a witness, an observer, a background awareness, is the sense of I added to it. The clarity is not the error. The I is.

That is how it went for me. Presence was vivid from early on. But it belonged to someone. It was my awareness. Then I made it a Witness. Then a Source. Then a Mirror. Then a seamless field of luminous presence, non-dual, but still something real on its own side. For many years I believed this was the destination. The question that turned it was simple. Does Presence itself exist from its own side?

Around nineteen ninety-seven, anatta came. No seer behind seeing. Just the seen. The hearer was never more than the hearing. It was a genuine realization. But each deconstruction frees only what it has relinquished. Anatta relinquished the self, and the Self behind it, and that much was freed. The world was not yet. The self had dissolved, but the universe had not. Space still sat like a container. Time still ran like a conveyor belt. And awareness could still quietly become a subtle ground. That habit has to be seen through in each place it works, in space, time, cause, death and the world itself.

What sees it through is emptiness. Not only the emptiness of the self, but of everything that appears, and of the clarity itself. Even the luminosity, even the awareness, is seen as dependently designated. That is twofold emptiness.

I should be honest about where I am. The deconstruction of self and Self in anatta, and then total exertion, where all conditions are fully this one moment, gave a taste that keeps bringing me forward. But it is not a finished state. Even now it is still in oscillation.

And here the two halves meet. The vividness of the apple and its unfindability are not two facts. They are one fact, seen twice. The red is vivid exactly because it depends on light, eye and skin. It is never found apart from them. The clarity is not found from its own side, and the emptiness is never a blank. Each is the other's way of being said.

Dzogchen has a word for this side of it. Spontaneous presence. For me, spontaneous presence is dependent arising with no producer. It is dependent arising described from the side of experience instead of analysis. Neither description is higher. Each guards the other. Dependent arising stops spontaneous presence being heard as a hidden source. Spontaneous presence stops dependent arising being heard as a machine running in the dark, waiting for a knower.

There is one guard I keep close. If only the vividness is affirmed, presence turns into a new ground. If only the unfindability is affirmed, the world turns thin and void. Both halves, always.

Now the body.

Dzogchen speaks of the basis in three aspects. Its essence is empty, pure from the beginning. Its nature is clear. And its display never stops. I follow Longchenpa here. The basis is empty of being a ground. Nothing that appearing comes from, or rests on.

The display first shows as sound, light and rays, and the light as five lights. When the display is not recognised as the basis's own radiance, it is taken as something other, standing over there. Then, the tantras say, the five lights harden into the five elements. From the elements come the body and the world. So the ordinary body is light not recognised.

The rainbow body is said to be the same process run the other way. When the display is recognised through and through, nothing holds the elements as solid any more.

But listen to that carefully. It is easy to hear the lights as a shining stuff underneath, and the body as frozen light that melts back. Read that way, it is a substrate again, made of light instead of matter.

I read it another way. The five lights are not a stuff. They name the clarity of appearing, in five qualities, as empty as what appears. Condensing into the elements names how appearing is taken. It does not name a stuff turning solid. The trouble was never the clarity. It was the grasping.

Hold an apple. The hardness. The coolness. The red. The sweetness. Each arises only as its conditions meet. None is found stored in the apple. Then a habit ties them together. One solid apple, out there, as if hardness and red and sweetness all belonged to a hidden core.

That habit is the knot. Dzogchen describes the same moment from the side of light: the display taken as other, condensing into a solid world. I describe it from the side of the view: a habit tying separate appearings into a thing. One movement, described twice.

Untie the knot, and nothing is lost. There is just this hardness, this red, this coolness, this sweetness. Vivid. Appearing as their conditions come together. Dzogchen would say, the five lights. I say, just this, vivid and light.

And the body is held the way the apple is held. Hardness, warmth, weight and the felt sense of mine, tied into one solid body, in here. The rainbow body, in essence, is that knot untied all the way through, the body included.

So, in essence, it is a realization. In two thousand thirteen I wrote that the rainbow body in Dzogchen does not mean that your body disappears. And when someone summed it up as, in essence a realization, I added: maybe actualization of realization would be better. In essence it is an actualized state.

I also said what it rests on. Twofold emptiness, and luminosity carried into all three states. Waking. Dreaming. Deep sleep. And that this is supported by the strength of view and realization. Not by the intensity of a non-conceptual experience. So it is not a special experience, however bright. It is the view, realised, and carried through day and night.

Dzogchen calls the last of its four visions the exhaustion of phenomena. What is exhausted? Not appearing. What is used up is appearing held as things, each with its own mark. Even the lights of the visions are not kept as something seen. In twenty twenty I put it this way. Exhaustion of phenomena is like the sense of observer being dissolved, and the sense of object also disappearing. No seer. No seeing. Nothing seen. And appearing goes on.

Then how does one get there? Here I part from the traditions. Dzogchen gives the appearing side a practice of its own. The Gelug school works through the winds and the subtle body. Taoist inner alchemy refines energy into a body of spirit. The method is shared widely. The view is not.

For me, what frees is seeing through the inherent framework. Not where the winds go. No practice of winds, channels or drops makes the lights. Nothing such a practice does turns a body into light. The way is a de-reification. Seeing that what seems so solid, so direct, so undeniable, is vivid appearing, found nowhere. That is not easier than any energy practice. It is just as hard to break through.

The body is not left out. In my own experience, when the view let go, the mind and the body's energy opened by themselves, without effort. I was not forcing the breath to break the knots. The knot in the body uncoiled when the knot in the view did. Energy practices can help loosen what the body holds. But luminous clarity was never a product of the winds.

And the shrinking corpse? The logic shows how the body is known and lived. It does not show what others will see at a funeral. That a body physically vanishes is reported, not proved. Many great masters left ordinary bodies. Insight is measured by the freedom of the mind, not by what happens to the body.

And it is not a goal. To practise for a body of light is still to practise for something. Tibetans speak of a leather butter bag. Worked with butter while in use, it stays soft. Left full and never worked, it goes hard, and cannot be softened again. Knowledge of the lights that is never tested hardens the same way. While I still think inherently, the elements stay solid, however much I hear about light.

So I come back to the sound. The car. The bird. The fan. Each one clear as it comes. No lamp behind it. No one holding it. Found nowhere, and never missing.

If nothing is found, what turns into light? Nothing found turns into light. The body appeared solid because it was held. When the holding ends, it no longer appears so. Luminous clarity, recognised all the way through, the body included. That is the rainbow body.
