## 2. Does It Arise or Cease? (the koan, 11:54)

### Title

Does It Arise or Cease? A Koan Read With Two Logics | After Anatta · Nāgārjuna's Middle Way

### Description

This moment ceases as it arises. Does it arise, or does it cease? A koan is not a riddle with a clever answer. It is a question built to catch a habit in the act: before any answer comes, the question has already handed us an "it".

The koan is worked through twice. With the logic of substance, all four answers fail (it arises, it ceases, both, neither), and each way out keeps the "it": a timeless Now, an awareness behind, a pure flow, point instants, or nothing at all. With the logic of dependency, the moment is picked out together with its arising and its ceasing, so "ceases as it arises" is a plain description, and the question falls away. Then the koan is run through Nāgārjuna's five core logics: the four corners, same or different, the three times, infinite regress and mutual dependence. Each searches a different hiding place of the "it", and each finds nothing, while the moment is still here. Dōgen's firewood and ash, Dzogchen's self-release, and why it matters: nothing to keep, nothing to lose.

## 9. Isn't Energy the Substrate? (bonus article, 3:00)

### Title

Isn't Energy the Substrate? Physics, Conservation and the Middle Way | Thusness · John Tan

### Description

Strike a match. A small flame flares, gives off heat and light, and is gone. Where did that energy come from, and where did it go? If anything deserves to be called the substrate of the world, surely it is energy, the one thing that stays the same through every change. Readers trained in physics often raise exactly this objection.

But physics itself does not treat energy that way. As Richard Feynman put it, physics has no picture of energy as little blobs: it has a number, worked out in many ways, that comes out the same before and after. Three facts show that this number is not a hidden stuff: it depends on the frame; its conservation comes from a symmetry (Emmy Noether); and it is not conserved everywhere. Conservation is a designated regularity of the strongest kind, as reliable as conventions get, and not a stuff underneath.

## 13. Recognition, Not Achievement (Book 4, Chapter 23 on the website, 3:16)

### Title

Recognition, Not Achievement: What Practice Cannot Produce | Book 4 · Thusness

### Description

There is one thing effort cannot do: it cannot produce what is already complete. The natural state is not produced by stopping thought or pushing appearances away. Thought is recognised as already empty, already not fixed, and in that recognition it stops fixing.

Lhun grub, spontaneous presence, means already complete: not a state one gets more of by trying harder. The same caution applies to negation: a way out that negates appearances until nothing is left is still a fixer. Recognition denies and puts nothing in its place. Then the three recognitions practice turns around: conditions are what work; openness is simply what dependence means; and there is nothing to hold, and no one apart from the holding. Openness, too, is empty. The book ends where it began.
