# Mind and Consciousness: Both Are Also Singled Out

Notice a thought. Now notice that "a thought" has been picked out. This, rather than the sound in the room or the feeling in the chest.

This is After Anatta: notes by John Tan, known as Thusness.

People often expect me to say it. Anatta is seen, the witness is gone, and what remains is vivid appearing, clear and knowing. So why not simply call that mind? Why not say that mind is the empty vivid appearing?

Because mind too is singled out. It is picked out as "this", and designated. To say "mind is the appearing" takes one thing that was singled out and makes it the name of everything that appears. That move looks harmless. It is the same move that once made the Witness, the Source and the Mirror. The cut is quietly promoted to the whole.

So I do not give mind that place. Not because mind is less than appearances. Because nothing singled out gets that place. Not mind, not matter, not clarity, and not emptiness.

Start with what singling out is.

Designation does not need words. It needs only singling out. This sound, this colour, this ache, picked out as "this". A "this" stands out only against what it is not, and only with this seeing, this light, this attention, these conditions. In a world of only blue, no one would see blue.

So singling out is not something done to an appearance that was there first. It is the appearing. And because it is already dependence, it is already empty. By appearing, a thing is already empty. That is about what appearing is. It is not how it seems to us. To us, things seem to stand by themselves. That seeming is the habit, and we will come to it.

Most people agree with this, as long as we talk about apples, sounds and tables. The trouble starts when we turn the same seeing on the one who seems to be doing the singling out.

Mind is not standing outside the act, doing it. Mind is one of the things singled out. If you ask who, then, does the singling out: no one stands behind it. The act, what is singled out and the mind arise together, the way the doer and the deed arise together in Nāgārjuna's eighth chapter. A singler standing behind would be the witness again.

Mind is picked out as "this knowing", "this thought", "my mind". And like every "this", it stands out only with its conditions. Its characteristics and its behaviour are not its own. They depend on all the other factors in that act of singling out, and they fit the framework in which mind is being singled out.

Look at how differently mind is cut. In everyday talk, mind is my thoughts and my feelings, something inside the head that I own, calm or upset. In the Abhidharma, mind is a run of mind-moments with their mental factors, counted and classified. In Yogācāra, mind is eight consciousnesses: six for the senses, one that takes them as "I", and a storehouse. In neuroscience, mind is brain activity: networks, signals and measurable states.

Each of these works. A friend can comfort with the first. A meditator can train with the second and the third. A doctor can treat with the fourth. But none of them is mind as it is from its own side, because no such mind is found. In each framework, mind behaves exactly as that framework's cut requires.

That is what I mean when I say mind is designated. It does not mean mind is a mere word. It means that what we call mind, its edges, its unity and its properties, arises together with the act that singles it out, and is not found apart from it.

The same holds the other way. Matter is not stuff standing by itself, with mind floating on top. A table is singled out as "this table" by eye, hand and use. A physicist singles it out as molecules, then fields, then probabilities. The cook, the chemist and the physicist cut the same loaf differently, and each cut works. None of them is the loaf from its own side.

Once both are seen this way, an old puzzle loosens. The Hard Problem asks how matter, complete without experience, could give rise to mind. But the question begins by taking two cuts within one vivid appearing, and treating each as standing on its own. Then it asks for a bridge between them. No bridge is found, because the gap was made by the starting point.

One caution about the word "cut". The appearing is never found uncut, waiting for a framework to slice it. "One appearing" means "not two". It does not mean a whole lying underneath its parts.

So I do not say all is mind, and I do not say all is matter. They are not two things to be joined, and not one thing in two disguises. They are two ways the appearing is singled out, each within its framework.

So mind, consciousness and luminous clarity are the same in this respect. They are phenomena. A phenomenon is whatever can be known. Mind can be known. Consciousness can be known. Clarity can be known. So they are dependently arisen, and empty like everything else.

This needs care, because I also say that clarity is never absent. You cannot talk about phenomena without clarity. Every appearing is clear, known as it appears. Dependent arising is never a mechanism in the dark that a knower then lights up.

But "never absent" means never absent from what appears. It does not mean an awareness that stays switched on. Under anaesthesia, when the conditions for the six streams are not met, nothing appears, and no clarity is left over, waiting in the dark.

And clarity being never absent is not the same as clarity being the floor. Its sameness across sights, sounds and thoughts is easily lifted out, and taken to stand on its own, as a background, or a Mind that gives off appearances. Not lifted out, the clarity stays the appearing's own. Lifted out and held, it becomes the last refuge of the self.

So I put it this way. Clarity is never missing from what appears. And the moment clarity is picked out and named, it is one more phenomenon. Not the thing all phenomena are made of.

There was a very key phase in my own insight. It was the recognition that appearances are one's radiance clarity. It came between phase four, Presence as a bright mirror, and phase five, no mirror reflecting, when anatta became clear.

It is a direct taste. Appearances are not out there, reflected in a mirror. They are luminous, vivid and intimate, and the distance between knower and known collapses. I do not deny any of it.

But it misses something, and at the time I did not know that it was being expressed vaguely, inside the old framework. Listen to the words. "One's" keeps an owner. The appearances belong to someone, even if that someone has become very large and very subtle. "Radiance" pictures a source and what it gives off. And "clarity" quietly becomes the stuff that appearances are made of.

So the taste was non-dual, but the framework reading it was still inherent. This was the first desynchronisation I met: the taste ahead of the view. One moment the taste is vivid and complete. The next, the mind tries to say what it is, and what it says is contradictory and untenable, exactly as Nāgārjuna's analysis shows. A mind that lights its own appearances is the lamp of his seventh chapter. A clarity that is the source of everything is the self-standing cause of his first. The taste was not wrong. Its reading was.

Many practices work by quieting the symbolic layer. Dropping labels, suspending thought, resting without elaboration. Bare attention, bare awareness, naked awareness. I do not dismiss this. Letting go of concepts does allow a direct taste of luminous clarity, and of its non-duality with what we call appearances.

But I see it for what it is. Bare attention is one part of the training: learning to taste luminous clarity directly. Tasting appearances as one's radiance clarity is another insight again. And for those who break through to a direct taste of anatta, in my view that whole course is completed. Once there is no seer behind seeing, there is nothing left to strip away to reach the seeing. In seeing, only the seen.

What quieting the symbolic layer does not do is resolve the distorted view that led to the confusion. When thought returns, and it always does, the same framework reads the taste again. The clarity becomes a ground. The presence becomes a universal substance. The openness becomes a field from which appearances emerge. The habit was never only in the words. Singling out happens before language, and so does the habit of taking what is singled out as standing on its own. Quieting words does not reach it. Seeing it does.

So concepts are not the enemy, and silence is not the cure. The cure is understanding the habit.

So what is needed after anatta is not more bare attention. It is work on the view. In my own case it went in stages, and it goes round more than once.

First, anatta itself. In seeing, only the seen. There is thinking, and no thinker. But anatta removes the one who watches. It does not by itself remove the things watched, the moment, the space they seem to sit in, or the causes that seem to drive them.

So the next stage is a strange one. The same pattern as the radiance phase, one level deeper. The experience has become non-substantialist, but the framework that reads it is still substantialist. The self has dissolved, but the universe has not. Space sits as a container. Time runs like a conveyor belt. And awareness, or presence, or energy, quietly becomes a subtle ground. The danger is that it feels complete. Then "this is beyond concepts" becomes a reason not to look.

Then refining and syncing the view. Get familiar with the logic and the tools of dismantling: the search for what is designated, the four corners, the steps of the Logic After Anatta. Not as beliefs to hold, but as tools to use, wherever the mind assumes that something stands by itself.

Catch the framework while it is working. Take sadness. "My mind is heavy" arrives as one solid fact: a mind, and its heaviness. Look at what is actually there. A weight in the chest. A sinking. A thought replaying. A face that comes to mind. Each is vivid. None is the mind those words placed underneath them. "My mind" was the cut that tied them together and took itself as found.

Then taste relaxing into openness. This is not bare attention coming back. Bare attention strips things away to reach a seeing. Relaxing lets go of a holding that the tools have already shown to be unfounded. Nothing is reached, and nothing is kept. Maintenance would be the split again.

This takes time, because we are so used to the inherent framework. It does not give way to one argument or one good sitting.

And in time, the veil dissipates with clear seeing. It is not torn away, and nothing hidden is found behind it. The veil was the habit of seeing inherently, and as the seeing becomes clear, it loosens by itself. Appearing presents itself: no maker, no knower, no background. That is spontaneous perfection. Mind and matter lose their weight, and become light, magical and plastic.

None of this is a state that is kept. It is recognised, and recognised again wherever the habit still returns. Without the tools, the taste is read back into a ground. Without the taste, the tools stay dry.

So two things work together. The habit: by default we see inherently, and whatever is singled out is taken as there by itself. We designate that habit as ignorance. And the framework, the matrix: the way the appearing is broken into parts, mind and matter, subject and object, cause and effect, for explanation and for use, within a set of agreements.

The matrix is not a field beneath appearances. It is the framework itself, and the habit of taking its cuts as found. The trouble is never that we single out. Singling out is the appearing, vivid and exact. The trouble is that we take the framework's cuts as the joints of the world.

How does this stand with Nāgārjuna? That mind is designated like everything else is his twenty-fourth chapter: whatever arises dependently is empty, with no exception for mind. And Candrakīrti told the Mind-Only school that you cannot keep the mind and throw out the objects. Searched for, neither is found.

And with Dzogchen, read without a ground? Longchenpa says the ground is called a ground only from a relative point of view. Refusing to make clarity the floor is Dzogchen's own warning.

On one question my reading is closer to Mahāmudrā. I keep the distinction between ordinary mind and rigpa, but I hold it this way. Ordinary mind is appearing held by the habit of fixing. Rigpa is the same appearing known without that fixing. One mind, held two ways. Gampopa put it this way: co-emergent mind is the truth body, and co-emergent appearances are its light.

Dzogchen separates the two more sharply, and rightly, because a calm, clear ordinary mind can pass for rigpa. But separating them is discerning, not finding two things. And once rigpa is singled out and named, it too is designated.

This links to what I have long said: spontaneous presence is dependent arising. In the appearing itself, they are not even two. Dependent arising is the appearing described while the dividing is at work: this condition, that condition, this result. Spontaneous presence is the same appearing when the fixing stops: already complete, with nothing found that produces it. One appearing, described two ways, at the same time. Neither is higher.

In practice, they can come in an order. Often the tools come first, and the taste after. Here Dōgen's firewood and ash fit. Firewood is complete in its own position. Ash is complete in its own position. Neither is a lesser stage of the other. So I do not say that dependent arising becomes spontaneous presence, any more than we say summer becomes winter. Nothing crosses from one to the other.

And each refines our reading of the other. Without dependent arising, spontaneous presence is heard as a source, a ground giving off appearances from nowhere. Without spontaneous presence, dependent arising is heard as a mechanism, a web of causes running in the dark. Together, they keep each other honest.

Two guards, to end. "Vivid empty appearing" is singled out too. If I do not mark that, it becomes the next floor, a quieter name for the Mind I just set down. And nothing is lost. Thinking, remembering, feeling and choosing work as they did. The brain still matters. Medicine still helps. What is denied is only a mind standing on its own side, and a matter that does the same.

Try it. Notice a thought, and see that it has been singled out. Now notice the mind that knows the thought. That too has been singled out. Look for it apart from that. Then do the same with this hand, and this table.

And if a sense of luminous presence comes, do not push it away. Ask only: is this clarity standing on its own, behind what appears? Or is it the appearing's own, and empty too?

Then let go of the holding, and rest, without trying to keep anything.
