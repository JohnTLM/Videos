# The "It", and Formation

Then what is it that carries?

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is Part 4 of 7. In Part 3, the chariot carried you to town, and no chariot was found behind the carrying. And still the mind asks: then what is it that carries? This part is about that question.

For years I have said it in many ways. The chariot has no essence, yet it still carries. There is no wind, yet the blowing is felt. The breeze is still cold on the skin.

I have come to see that these sentences disturb more than they open. The mind cannot wrap around them. It hears a paradox. Either it files the paradox away as something deep and unclear, or it rejects it as nonsense.

Look closely at the sentence. The chariot has no essence, yet it still carries.

It does two things at once. It keeps the chariot as the subject of the sentence. And it empties that subject: no essence. What is left is a chariot minus its core, which still somehow does the carrying. A hollow thing, pushing a load. A ghost that acts.

So the mind asks, quite reasonably: then what does the carrying? And whatever I answer, conditions, the parts, the process, is heard as a new thing put in the empty place. The gap is not closed. It is only filled with something new.

The word that does the damage is despite. Or yet. Or still. It sets the working against the emptiness, as if things worked in spite of lacking something. As long as the sentence has that shape, more explanation only makes it worse.

The problem is not the insight. It is the grammar.

Ordinary grammar supports the habit every time I speak. The wind blows. The person walks. Each sentence slips in a fixed bearer, standing behind the action. Because the habit is so reliable, it goes unseen.

So look at how the question, then what carries, is built. It asks for a carrier behind the carrying. It has put an it there, before any answer is given. Whatever I answer will be heard as the carrier.

So I do not answer it. I show what it is. The carrying is wheels, axle, road, load, the horse's pull and the driver's purpose, meeting. Nothing extra stands behind them doing the work. Nor do they add up to a new hidden carrier. They are the carrying, described in parts.

The same trap waits in another question. Having negated, the mind asks: if these vivid appearances are not things, then what are they?

That question is the old view, still running. It has to be seen through, not answered. In five steps.

Step one. There is no substrate. No thing, no substance, no bearer is found behind or beneath any appearance. Not out there. Not in here. Not as a ground of mind, or energy, or awareness.

Step two. So the question, what is it, is invalid. It assumes something is there with a nature of its own, waiting to be identified correctly. Take that something away, and the question has nothing to land on. The answer is not hidden. It is not nothing. It is not beyond words. The question was never valid.

Step three. So the eight extremes do not apply. All of them, all at once. Nāgārjuna opens his verses by praising dependent arising as free of them. Does it arise, or cease? Is it permanent, or annihilated? Does it come, or go? Is it the same, or different? Each asks what becomes of a thing with a nature of its own. They belong to the inherent view, and make sense only inside it. They need not be refuted one by one. And the middle way is not a middle answer between them.

Step four. Watch the it slip in. Even the question, how does it form, can carry the old view. The it is assumed before any question is asked. It is never seen in the appearing. It is supplied. A grammatical ghost, that habit puts first: a subject waiting for its verbs. It arises. It forms. It lasts. It ends.

This is the same habit as the self. A knower, put behind the knowing, now running through everything. Changing the question does not remove it. Only seeing the habit does.

Step five. The it is what is formed, not what forms. Formation does not happen to a thing. The it is named in the forming, together with its conditions, and is not found apart from them. The cup formed on the potter's wheel is valid, and it works. Heard as a bearer that was there first and then went through the forming, it is as invalid as, what is it.

Others have seen the grammar sharply. Nietzsche wrote that the popular mind separates the lightning from its flash, as if a doer stood behind the deed, when the deed is everything. I share his reading of the grammar. Where I part from him: the doing that remains is not a new ground either. It too is named, and not found.

Looking back over my articles, they say one thing, again and again. The it is never needed.

Not in experience. The flashing is the lightning.

Not in formation. The it is formed, named together with its conditions.

Not in causes. Conditions work without causal power, and no it carries the power across.

Not in functioning. The cup holds tea, and the car can injure. Neither needs a core to do it.

Not in continuity. The lamp burns through the night, and nothing is carried.

Not in the meeting. No glue holds the hand and the ice together.

And so there is no it to undergo the eight extremes. They are not false answers. They are invalid questions.

Arguments can only point. This has to be met directly.

Next time there is a breeze, feel it. The coolness on your face. The moving. The slight pressure. Then ask: is anything missing? What would a wind add to this, if there were one behind it?

Look honestly. Nothing would be added. The coolness and the moving are complete. Wind is the name for them.

Now hold a cup of tea. The warmth. The weight. The tilt to your lips. Notice the pull to ask, what is this, really. See that the pull already assumes a thing in there. Drop the it, not the cup. The cup is still fully here, vivid and working. And I still call it the cup.

If you have tasted no-self, this will feel familiar. It is the same seeing, now turned to the seen. Nothing is missing, on either side.

I want to say plainly how serious I have found this to be. As a practitioner, during my own journey and in my own journals, I have come to see this way of speaking as a major disservice.

Many teachers and writers on emptiness fall into it without knowing it. And I am one of them. My own books and articles said, not found, yet it carries you. A car with no essence still injures. How a world without essence still appears. Again and again.

Each time, the sentence kept the it, while trying to remove it. It left the reader with a ghost that acts. It makes the view sound like a paradox to be accepted, or a mystery to be revered. It is neither.

Someone who is serious about a non-substantialist view deserves sentences that do not quietly rebuild the substance they are meant to dissolve. So this must be corrected. I have begun with my own writing.

So I now say it in the order of experience. Instead of, the chariot has no essence, yet it still carries:

The chariot carries. Chariot is the name for this carrying: wheels, axle, road and load, meeting. Look for a chariot behind the carrying, and none is found. None is needed. One would only get in the way.

And instead of, there is no wind, yet the blowing is felt:

The breeze is cold on my skin. That coldness, that moving, is what wind names. There is no wind behind it that the blowing needs.

With the it seen through, nothing has been taken away. The cup is in my hand. That was one half of the work. Now the other.

There are two halves to the work. One half is negation: seeing that nothing is found from its own side. The other half is formation: understanding how appearances form, vivid, stable and working, with no essence anywhere.

Relentless negation alone is only half the story. If I stop there, the world starts to feel thin. Appearance is treated as a leftover. Everything becomes only concepts, only a story. Actions and promises lose their weight. Not found becomes a reflex. That is what I call the disease of relentless negation. And it is relentless for one reason: formation is not understood. As long as I do not see how appearances form, the solidity keeps coming back, and I keep negating it.

So, how does an appearance form?

Every appearance has three features, at the same time.

It is vivid and clear. Nothing is dimmed or taken away. Its clarity belongs to the appearing itself. A sound is clear as sound. A colour is vivid as colour. No witness behind it is needed to light it up.

It is not found. Searched for, it has no core, no edge, no location. Not out there in the world. Not in here, in a private mind. And not even nowhere, since nowhere is just one more place.

And it arises with its conditions, and validly functions. It shows up only when its conditions meet. And it works completely.

This third point is the one I have had to come back to most. At first it sounds strange. Habit equates being there with being independent. It hears naming as a thin sticker on a thing already there. It pictures causes as one substance pushing another. So empty sounds flimsy. Ghostly.

But empty does not mean weak. A car can injure you, and no essence is found in it doing the injuring. Medicine heals. A cup holds hot tea. None of them needs a core to do it, and none would be helped by one.

Take the apple of Part 1 again, tossed from hand to hand. At once, habit supplies the impression of a hard, heavy thing standing on its own.

Look closely. Hardness, coolness and weight arise only as touch meets skin and fruit. The red shifts to orange under candlelight, and to grey at dusk. It arises with light, eye and attention.

And do not swap the solid apple for a subtler picture either: a hardness, a red and an arc that are given first, with the name apple stuck on them later. The hardness, the red and the arc are no more given than the apple. Nothing bare waits underneath for a label. The naming is part of the appearing, arising in dependence like the rest.

When the habitual knot is untied, nothing is missing. The apple can still be tossed. It is eaten. It nourishes me. Its solidity and its effects arise because this vivid pattern meets hands, gravity and bodies, reliably.

One guardrail stays in place. The apple is not found apart from the streams of consciousness. And it is not made by them either. Consciousness is not a new floor in here, producing the apple. Mind and matter are two cuts in the same vivid appearing. Neither is the substrate underneath the other. Both are named together.

So formation is not a new foundation. Conditions, frameworks and agreements are designated too. It is how a world appears and holds, with nothing underneath holding it.

So, with both halves in place, what has changed?

Nothing has been taken away from the world. And no new ground has been added.

The apple is still red. The car is still dangerous. The cup still holds hot tea. A friend's voice is still warm.

What has gone is only the floor that was never there.

What remains is not a final picture to defend, but the freedom of not needing one. Each appearance is met as it is. Vivid. Exact. Workable. Releasing itself as it arises.

And that raises the next question. If seeing this changes nothing in the world, what is it for? And does seeing it mean falling silent at last?

That is Part 5.
