## 1. Annihilation Is an Inherent View (AA16, about 7:41)

### Title

Annihilation Is an Inherent View: Nothing to Lose | After Anatta

### Description

A sound has just stopped. A day is over. Someone you loved has died. And with it comes a picture: something was fully there, and now there is a blank where it stood. If nothing lasts for ever, is everything wiped out in the end?

Annihilation sounds like letting go. It holds on to the same thing eternalism holds on to: something can be annihilated only if it was there from its own side first. Nāgārjuna sets "it is" and "it is not" side by side (MMK 15.10–11), shows that "it is not" leans on "it is" (15.5), and that the fork between lasting for ever and being cut off opens only for a thing with its own nature (21.14). Seen where it can be felt, a sound that stops goes nowhere: the source, the air, the ear and the attention part, as they came together. Then where annihilation hides: the fear of death, "empty means nothing", and "after awakening, causes no longer apply". One point, seen from three sides, with Neither Carried Nor Cut and One Flame Lights Another.
