After twenty five chapters of refutation, Nah-gar-joo-nah stops. And simply explains how suffering works.

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. These are my own notes, from my own practice and contemplation.

Part 1 set out how Nah-gar-joo-nah reads when this is, that is. Not a handover between real things. A pattern holding, with conditions named from the effect's side.

Section 5 asked, in the tradition's own terms, who goes round the wheel. The discourses answered, not a valid question. The Ah-bee-dar-mah answered, look, and you find only momentary factors, and no self among them.

Here I follow the twelve links through Nah-gar-joo-nah's own chapter on them. He keeps the answer of the discourses. And he takes it one step further. Not only is there no owner among the pieces. The pieces themselves stand on nothing of their own.

For twenty five chapters, he shows that nothing is found standing on its own. Not motion. Not the senses. Not the self. Not time. Not cause. Then he stops refuting. He simply sets out the twelve links, one after another.

A reader may be surprised. After all that unfindability, how can he speak so plainly of one link arising from another?

As I see it, that is exactly the point. Emptiness does not stop the world working. A world of things with their own natures could not work at all. Here, dependent arising is shown doing its everyday job. Producing suffering while ignorance is present. And ending it when ignorance ends.

And the working chain is the very chain that was found empty. A chain whose links had natures of their own could never stop. Because nothing in the chain has a nature of its own, it works. And because it works that way, it can end.

The chapter opens with rebirth. Covered over by ignorance, one makes formations, and through these actions goes to a new destiny. With formations as condition, consciousness enters a new destiny.

Enters sounds like a traveller moving house, carrying a suitcase from one life to the next. It is not meant that way. Formations shape a direction, the way a river cuts its bed. And what follows flows along that bed. Nothing passes over. The old flame lights a new one.

The chain ends with a phrase the Buddha used often. In this way, this whole mass of suffering comes to be.

The word for whole means only. Unmixed. Nothing else in it. Chun-drah-keer-tee reads it as, without a self, or anything belonging to a self. There is no one who owns the suffering. There is only the suffering, arising from its conditions.

Then comes the line I take as the heart of the chapter. The one who does not know makes the formations that are the root of the round. The one who does not know is the maker. The one who knows is not, because they see how things are.

The maker of the round is not a person. It is not knowing. This is not a fate imposed from outside, and not a punishment. Take away the not knowing, and nothing is left to make the formations.

If not knowing is the maker, what becomes of the one who acts?

Nah-gar-joo-nah answered that earlier. The one who acts depends on the action. The action depends on the one who acts. We see no other way they could be established.

The one who is bound, the one who acts, the one who reaps. Each is named together with the binding, the action and the fruit. Not found before them. Not found apart from them.

He gives an image. Suppose a magician makes a magical person, and that person in turn makes another. The doer is like the first. The action, like the second. Afflictions, actions, bodies, doers and fruits are like a city in the clouds, like a mirage, like a dream.

Like a dream does not mean nothing happens. In a dream, a fall still frightens. The acting is real as acting. What is not found is someone standing behind it, turning the wheel.

Section 5 set out what the tradition keeps. Karma. Rebirth. Responsibility. The path. Nah-gar-joo-nah keeps all of it. What he adds is the reason it can work.

If an action stayed until the time it ripens, it would be permanent. If it has ceased, being ceased, what fruit could it produce? An action with its own nature would be stuck on one of those two sides.

Because the action, the doer and the fruit are found only together, and none on its own, the link between what is done and what is met can hold. Karma works because it is empty.

If not knowing makes the formations, where does it get its grip?

Nah-gar-joo-nah traces it back. Freedom comes from the ending of action and affliction. Action and affliction come from conceptual construction. And these come from elaboration. The mind's endless spinning of it is, it is not, mine, not mine.

So the cure is not a better belief about the twelve links. It is seeing. The next part takes that up.

Take a simple act. Typing a message. There is the reaching for the keys, the words forming, the pressing, the sentence appearing on the screen. Look for the one who types, apart from the typing. What is found is the typing. Fingers, words, intention, screen, all arising together.

Not that no one types. The conventional I types, and is responsible for what is sent. But the typist is named with the typing, the way fire is named with fuel.

And the same is true of the one who is hurt, the one who wants, the one who goes round the wheel. Each is named with its action.

This is Section 6, Part 2 of the Dependent Arising series.

None stands behind it, turning the wheel.
