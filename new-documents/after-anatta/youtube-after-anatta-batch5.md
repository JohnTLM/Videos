## 1. Decoding Anatta and MMK Chapter 3 (AA09, 18:40)

### Title

Decoding Anatta and MMK Chapter 3: Why Dependent Arising Is Spontaneous Presence | After Anatta

### Description

The Buddha told Bāhiya: in the seen, there will be just the seen. Nāgārjuna, in chapter 3 of his Fundamental Verses, says: without a seer there is no seen, and without the seen there is no seer. Many people hear a contradiction. There is none: one is the direct insight, the other is how the logic of dependency expresses that same insight and taste.

In John Tan's own words: the doer behind the doing, and why "the wind blows" is a fact about sentences. What falls away in anatta. Why Nāgārjuna refutes the agent instead of affirming it, and how "no seer without the seen" becomes "just the seen". The same move in chapters 2, 10 and 1: the walker, the fire, the cause. Why "found as neither the parts nor the whole" is emptiness. Not mind either: weight, Ernst Mach, and clarity that belongs to no one. A negation that puts nothing in its place. And how it ends in spontaneous presence: complete, effortless, not uncaused and not a source. Clarity is never missing; the only question is whether it is lifted out and made into a thing.

## 2. What the Logic Is Looking At (AA12, 11:22)

### Title

What the Logic Is Looking At: Dharma, Designation, and Blue and Red in One Stroke | After Anatta

### Description

Listen to a sound nearby: a car, a bird, the hum of a fridge. It is vivid and clear, and it needs no help to be known. Every step of the logic looks at something: a self, an object, a cause, matter, a person. They are not many topics. Each time, the logic is looking at a phenomenon, what Buddhism calls a dharma.

What a phenomenon is, and why there is no bare appearance under the names. Designation needs no words, only singling out, and singling out is already dependence. Picture a world of only blue: no one there would ever see blue. Then let a spot of red appear, and at once both appear. Five points from that one example: neither comes first; dependence is not a sequence; each colour is empty and vivid at the same moment; nothing produced it, which is spontaneous presence; and it is not one thing either. Then hold an apple, see the knot the habit ties, and the clarity that is the appearing's own.

## 3. The Logic: After Anatta (overview, ep96, 13:45)

### Title

The Logic After Anatta: Two Logics, Four Insights and One Koan | Nāgārjuna's Middle Way

### Description

Listen to a car going by. The sound is bright and clear, and it is already passing as it sounds. This moment ceases as it arises. Does it arise, or does it cease?

The overview of The Logic, from After Anatta: notes by John Tan (Thusness) on what turned out to matter after the insight of anatta in 1997. Why the logic of dependency needs a direct insight into what a phenomenon is. The four insights: every appearance is vivid and cannot be found; it arises whenever its conditions come together; we habitually think inherently, in the shape of our language; and there is no given world beneath it all. Anatta as the first door, and how the four lead to spontaneous presence. Why removing concepts is not enough, the three inventions it leaves, the koan read with both logics, the eight steps in brief, and the same logic carried to a self, an object, a cause and a person.
