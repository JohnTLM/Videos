# One Taste, Many Faces

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is the last of seven parts.

In Part 6, the taste was met three times, and found to be one taste. Now the taste itself: how it is described, how it is recognised, and how it is misread.

I say dependent arising is spontaneous presence. They are not two things.

Three descriptions, one taste.

Anatta says no one is behind it. No seer, no doer. Its guard: it is not a blank. Nobody disappears.

Dependent arising says nothing in it stands on its own. Conditions meet, and none is found from its own side. Its guard: it is not a web, and not a ground.

Spontaneous presence says nothing is missing, and nothing is making it. Its guard is double. It is not uncaused: what is gone is the maker, not the order. And it is not a source.

They are three angles on one face. None is higher. Not stages, first analysis and then recognition. Nor does one turn into another, any more than summer becomes winter. That way of speaking brings back a traveller, crossing from one to the other. Nothing crosses.

Anatta needs one note. As the first insight, it covers only the subject. But anatta is a seal, not a stage. As the seal, all dharmas are without self: no self-nature in the seen, in the cause, in the condition. Read as the seal, anatta describes the whole taste, from the side of no one.

Freedom from all elaborations is not a fourth description. It says nothing about the taste. It says only that nothing reified is added to it. And spontaneous presence adds nothing to the negation. It does not slip a positive reality in behind the refutation. It names only what the refutation never denied: the appearing itself, once nothing is added to it.

The descriptions keep each other honest. Dependent arising keeps spontaneous presence from being heard as coming from nowhere, or from a hidden ground. Spontaneous presence keeps dependent arising from being heard as a machine of causes, working in the dark.

Keep two questions apart. How does this appearing come about? Through causes and conditions, always. What is its nature? Empty. Not produced. So, why, has nothing to point to. Beyond cause and effect answers the second question. It never cancels the first.

Now the many faces.

From the taste, I recognise its many faces, in very different situations. A sound, a thought, a flash of anger, a cause and its effect, a stretch of time, a death, washing the dishes.

It is much like an AI model that recognises faces. It knows a face across light, angle and age, not because it has stored one photograph, but because it has learned the face's structure. Once the taste is known, it is recognised by its shape, wherever it shows up.

A death, for instance. Conventionally someone has died, and that is mourned. But no one is found who goes. Nothing that was there from its own side is cut off. And the grief, vivid as it is, has nothing behind it. The shape is the same. No one behind it, nothing standing on its own, nothing missing.

Four things keep this recognition honest. First, name the shape. Second, no one is doing the recognising; the recognising is itself an appearing. Third, the shape does not carry across; same form, not same stuff. Fourth, recognition can misfire. The I AM presence, a blank quiet, a refined oneness: each can look like the taste. The test is the shape. Is a witness, a source or a background still left? Is something being kept up?

So what does the view taste like? Held properly, it is tasted in every appearing in six ways.

Vividly appearing and unfindable. The colour is as bright as ever, the sound as clear. And when you look for what the name stands for, nothing is found, and nothing is needed. Vividness never needed anything findable.

Centerless. No one sits at the middle of what appears. The sound is not heard from a point.

Mutual. Each appears only with the others. The sound with the ear and the air. The thrower with the catcher. And the relations are not found either.

Seamless. No gap opens between one moment and the next that would need a carrier. Seamless does not mean merged into One. Sight stays sight, and sound stays sound.

Effortless. Nothing produces the appearing, and nothing has to keep it going. There is nothing to hold, so nothing to maintain.

Intimate. No distance stands between a knower and what is known. Nothing lights the appearing, not another, and not itself.

These six describe the appearing, conventionally. They are not six states to reach or to keep. If one of them is taken up as a goal, the doer comes back to chase it.

In fact, every mistake on this path has the same shape: the doer, put back somewhere else.

After anatta, a vast Awareness in which everything happens. The seer in a larger coat. In total exertion, a cosmic whole present in each moment. In dependent arising, a web, or a mechanism behind things. In spontaneous presence, a source that gives off appearances. In the marketplace, the helper who has arrived and now saves others, or the bystander who says it is all empty, so nothing needs doing. And in practice, a state to keep up.

None of this is kept. It was always so. It is recognised.

Do I depart from the great masters? At the core, I do not believe I do. The Buddha: when this is, that is. Nāgārjuna: dependent arising is emptiness, a designation in dependence, the middle way. Candrakīrti: the chariot, found neither among its parts nor apart from them, and conventions left as the world uses them. Tsongkhapa: dependence on causes, on parts, and most subtly on designation. Mipham: appearance and emptiness in union.

Where I part, I say so. From Tsongkhapa, on the order of the path: his is the view first; mine was the taste first, and the view syncing with it later. From Huayan and modern interbeing: nothing stands alone, and the relations are not found either. There is no net behind things. And from any reading that makes spontaneous presence an unconditioned ground. Dzogchen says spontaneous presence is not made by causes and conditions. Read with Nāgārjuna, that means unborn by nature: appearing through conditions, with nothing produced. I read Longchenpa's ground as empty, as Mipham does.

These are my readings, not the schools'. All views and understanding here are my own, from my practice and contemplation. I do not represent any tradition, school or teacher.

Try it. Listen to a sound nearby. A car, a fan, a voice.

Look for the hearer behind the hearing. Is anyone there, receiving it?

Find its conditions. The source, the air, the ear, attention. Is any of them standing behind the sound, pushing it out?

Change one condition. Cover one ear, or turn your head. It is not the same sound, altered. It is a different appearing.

Look for the between that joins the conditions. Then stop looking. The sound is complete, exactly as its conditions meet. When the next sound comes, it is complete too.

Later in the day, when a thought or a flash of irritation comes, look for the same shape. Anyone behind it? Anything in it standing on its own? Anything missing?

Seven parts ago, I began with a quip. If you think you understand, you don't. Now hold up your hand, and make a fist. Then open it.

Nothing is lost. No fist was ever hiding in the hand. And the hand is free to make a fist again.
