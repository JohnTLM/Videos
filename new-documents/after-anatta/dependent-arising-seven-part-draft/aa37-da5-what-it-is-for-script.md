# What It Is For: Freedom in Conventions

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is Part 5 of 7.

In Part 4, both halves were in place, and nothing was taken from the world. So what is dependent arising for? And does seeing it mean falling silent?

First, there are two ways of knowing rightly here. It helps not to collapse them.

The first works through concepts, within the framework. It is how a doctor treats, how a cook cooks, how a promise is kept, how karma is understood. This is not a lesser knowing. In this mode, dependent arising also shows emptiness. It corrects the inherent reading from inside the framework.

The second is freedom from all elaborations. Nothing reified is added. Nothing is cut into before and after, part and whole, cause and effect. Without the framework, there is one complete stroke. One here does not mean one stuff. It means not cut up. The colour is still the colour, and the sound is still the sound.

Not within the framework does not mean somewhere else. It is the same appearing, known without the cutting.

Neither cancels the other. The first is not a ladder to throw away. The second is not a place outside the world to retreat to.

So the framework can be held three ways. Without it: one complete stroke. Within it, with the wisdom of dependent arising: the same parts and conditions, now light. And in between, where we mostly live: within it, reified. That middle one is the matrix.

Within the framework, dependent arising does three kinds of work.

It explains. How does this come about? These conditions, met together.

It dismantles. What arises in dependence is not there from its own side. So dependence is the reason that takes apart the self, the object, the cause, the whole, the field, energy and the web. Tsongkhapa's tradition calls it the king of reasons. And it puts nothing in place of what it removes. It dismantles itself too, since dependence is not found from its own side either.

And it describes the taste: nothing standing on its own, nothing missing.

Dependent arising is an enlightened view. By that I do not mean a view only the enlightened can hold. I mean the right view of how things are, which we do not yet live in.

Have a second thought. If you think you do not think inherently, have a second thought. Toss the apple. Is it simply there, before any thought at all? For almost all of us, it is. That is the habit at work. It does not feel like a view. It feels like the world itself. So agreeing with dependent arising is not living in it.

So I keep two frameworks apart.

The default framework is the conventional cutting itself: parts and whole, before and after, cause and effect, agent and action. It works. We need it to speak, to cook and to keep promises. Dependent arising lives here.

The inherent framework is the habit of taking what the cutting singles out as standing by itself. That is the matrix. Not a world, not a field. A habit of seeing.

The framework is not the prison. What binds is living in it with reified thoughts. Dependent arising lives in the first, and unbinds the second. Run the two together, and dependent arising lives in a framework is heard as, dependent arising is only a story. That is the slide into annihilation.

And living in the framework with this wisdom, even spontaneous presence can be understood within it. Crudely, because the framework still speaks in pieces and in sequence. But truly. Conditions are the appearing. Nothing produces it. It is complete as its conditions meet. It is not a place beyond concepts, waiting outside the framework. That would make it a new ground.

How is that different from reading a taste through a concept? The difference is not concepts. It is reification. When the concepts are taken as found, the taste is fixed into a self or a source. Held as designations, the same concepts point, and do not fix.

Here I speak only for myself, about one thread. Take the apple once more: hardness, coolness, red, weight and motion, appearing together. Those same qualities are the elements, read the Indian way. Earth is hardness, water is cohesion, fire is heat, wind is motion, and space is room. What makes them seem solid is the habit of tying them into one thing out there, and one body in here. Untie that habit and nothing is lost. Hardness, warmth and motion remain, vivid and unowned. That vivid, unfound appearing is what Dzogchen calls the five lights: the elements recognised as display. The lights are not a luminous stuff. Nothing condenses, and nothing returns to a source.

A disclaimer for this thread in particular. I have not read these links put this way anywhere else, and I do not claim that this is how orthodox Dzogchen states them. Take it as my contemplation, to be tested, not as a teaching of any tradition.

Now, ignorance. Some readings say dependent arising originates with ignorance, since the twelve links begin there. That reading needs refining. As a view, dependent arising is the antidote to ignorance.

If it simply came from ignorance, it would be odd to praise it as the supreme teaching. Yet that is what the texts do. Nāgārjuna opens his verses by bowing to the Buddha who taught it. Tsongkhapa praises it as the best of all the Buddha taught. And the Buddha himself: whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dhamma.

The twelve links do begin with ignorance, and none of these masters denies it. Forward, they show how suffering runs. In reverse, how it ends. No one praises ignorance. They praise the seeing that undoes it.

I have also read that conventional truth is a veil, so dependent arising, being conventional, must be the veil. Candrakīrti gives the word three meanings: concealment, mutual dependence, and worldly convention. What veils is the first, ignorance. Dependent arising is the second. The same appearance, held by ignorance, seems to stand by itself, and that seeming is the veil. Seen as dependent arising, it is the seeing that lifts the veil.

Then, does freedom mean falling silent?

Many teachers, rightly, say the eight extremes do not apply. It is Nāgārjuna's own opening verse. But it is often said with a large gap. It is not shown why they do not apply: that the it they need was never there. Nor is it shown how the world works, with no it anywhere.

Then the gap is crossed by a leap. After freedom from all elaborations comes spontaneous presence, presented as the end of all speech. Silence, as the final answer.

I do not take that route. Silence keeps the it, only out of reach. The habit only rests, and slips back into the next sentence. And conventions are left unexplained, so presence is easily heard as a ground: the final it, behind appearances.

Dzogchen at its best does not stop at silence. Read with Mipham, spontaneous presence is dependent arising met without fixing, and the two truths stay one. Where I part is from a presentation of the leap as the end of speech.

Speech was never the problem. The it heard in it was. Once the it is seen through, words and names do not have to stop. They are used freely, and they work. The conventional is not a lower floor to leave behind. The ultimate is not a silence to leap into. They are one appearing. Named, and not found.

The Buddha said it plainly. Milk becomes curds, curds become butter, butter becomes ghee. While it is milk, it is not called curds. Nothing is carried, yet each name is right in its time. These are the world's common usages, he says, which the Realized One uses to communicate, without getting stuck on them.

Even the first it, the I, is used this way. One who is fully freed still says, I speak. Skillful, understanding the world's labels, they use these terms as mere expressions.

Tsongkhapa makes the same point as method. As long as appearance and emptiness seem separate, the Buddha's meaning is not yet realized. The view is complete when merely seeing dependent arising undoes the grasping. No leap beyond it.

And Dōgen sees it most clearly, for me. The old saying runs: a painted rice cake does not satisfy hunger. Dōgen turns it over. There is no remedy for satisfying hunger other than painted rice cakes. Without painted hunger, you never become a true person.

There is no unpainted cake behind the painting. No it behind the convention. The painting is not a poor copy, waiting to be dropped. It feeds.

So what is it for? It explains, it dismantles, and it describes the taste. It is the antidote to ignorance, not its shape. And it frees conventions rather than silencing them.

But all this is still view. How is it actually met, as a taste, in a life? That is Part 6.
