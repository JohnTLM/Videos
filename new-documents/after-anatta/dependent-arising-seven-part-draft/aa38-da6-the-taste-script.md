# The Taste

I am John Tan, known as Thusness. This is Part 6 of 7.

So far, dependent arising has been view: what it says, what a name does, the it, formation, and what it is for. But it did not come to me first as a view. It came as a taste, and the view came to sync with it later. This part is about that taste.

Looked back on, the taste was met three times. Right after anatta. Again when every hinge was exhausted. And again in the marketplace.

Start with anatta. Part 3 described it as the seeing that finds no seer behind the seeing. Here is how it tasted.

What are seeing and hearing? We say they are events, processes. That is true, but it is still words. So look, in real time.

Seeing is just colours. No effort at all. Hearing is just sound. No effort at all.

Nothing is doing the seeing, and the seeing is not being done. Even what we called a process does not seem to be a process at all. Nothing runs underneath the colours, carrying them from one moment to the next.

And the colours are not dark. They are clear, known and vivid. The knowing is right there in the colour, and nothing stands behind it, knowing.

So here, right after anatta, is a first taste of spontaneous presence. Immediate, effortless and spontaneous.

How is this different from a refined non-dual state? A refined non-dual rests in oneness. It thinks of everything as made of one luminosity, one clarity that colours, sounds and thoughts are made of. The seer has merged, but the one it merged into is still a something.

Anatta finds no seer there to merge. With nothing merged, no one is left for everything to be made of. In this taste there is no made of. The colour is clear as colour. The sound is clear as sound. Each is complete with its own conditions: light and eye for the colour, air and ear for the sound. Six clarities, one for each stream of the senses and of thinking. Not one lamp lighting them all.

So the test is simple. Is there a one that everything is made of? If so, substance is still there.

This taste does not stay in seeing and hearing. It widens. Each event seems to spend itself completely. The sound of a car going by is wholly the sound. Nothing is behind it, holding back. The car is still the car, more vivid than before.

Dōgen called this total exertion: each thing spending itself fully in its own place. Firewood abides in its own place, and ash in its own. It is easily heard as, the whole cosmos is present in each moment. That only makes the whole the ground, instead of the parts. The event spends itself completely, with no cosmos packed inside it.

But a taste is not yet freedom.

The taste is effortless, and still the mind has not released its habit of reifying. How can both be there at once? Because they are not in the same place. The taste is in the immediacy of the senses. The habit is in how the world is understood around that moment.

While I see a flower, the seeing is just colours, with no one behind it. Yet the moment I turn away, the flower is still assumed to be sitting there by itself, its red belonging to it. A cause is still felt to push its effect. Death is still the end of a someone. The self had dissolved, but the universe had not.

For years after anatta, the taste was there, and the view still treated each thing as standing by itself. That gap is what I call the desynchronisation.

Experience alone has a limit. Experience can become deeply non-dual, and the framework that reads it can stay substantialist. Then the habit quietly turns a genuine taste back into a hidden ground. A universal Awareness. A Presence with a capital P.

So, in my experience, three strands have to work together, again and again, each checking the others.

The first: confirm it in direct experience. Not once, but many times, until it is no longer an idea.

The second: equip the view with tools. Some are simple, like Nāgasena's chariot. Some are thorough and exacting, like Candrakīrti's sevenfold search and Nāgārjuna's verses. The tools have to work as a pure negation. They remove a nature of its own, and put nothing in its place. Otherwise the habit just moves to a subtler level.

The third: taste the gradual loosening. Whatever is taken to exist from its own side is gripped with the tightness that solidity invites. As view and experience keep confirming each other, that grip loosens. Gradually.

The habit does not announce itself. It is only when it is challenged by Nāgārjuna's verses that it comes to the surface. The logic points to the place to look. Then I look, and check it against the taste. Insight without the logic drifts back into nouns. Logic without the taste stays a clever argument about words.

Before freedom, the mind will always hinge on something.

First it hinges on parts. A thing is made of its pieces. When the parts are seen through, it hinges on the whole. When the whole is seen through, it finds something finer to stand on. Qi. Energy. A field. Relations. A substrate. Each time, it is looking for a foundation.

The tools meet each hinge in turn. The chariot is not found among its parts, nor apart from them. Qi and energy are not a stuff under appearances. A field is the whole again, in a softer form. Relations are not found either. And a substrate is the foundation the mind was looking for all along. It is not found.

When all of these are exhausted, neither parts nor whole, with nothing left to hinge on, the mind becomes free from all elaborations, and in a stable way. Stable does not mean the habit can never stir again. When it stirs, it is met by the same seeing, and it does not take root as it did.

Then appearance is again tasted as vividly present, spontaneous and free. This is the first taste, revisited.

It is the same taste. What has changed is the understanding around it, and two things more. Reach: at first the taste lived in seeing and hearing. Now it covers thought, the body, things, causes, space, time and death. And stability: it no longer depends on looking.

Take away the doer from everything, and put nothing in its place. Then nothing is making the appearing. The sound of a car going by is not a product. It shows up whole and vivid, exactly as its conditions meet.

That is what spontaneous presence means to me. It is not uncaused. Every appearing has its conditions. And it is not a source. Nothing stands behind appearing, giving it off.

Mind and matter lose their weight. They become light, and workable.

After freedom from all elaborations, I re-enter the marketplace, and dirty my hands.

The marketplace is ordinary life. Work, family, money, arguments, other people's troubles, choices. The last of the Zen ox-herding pictures shows it: barefoot, smeared with mud and ashes, he comes into the market with helping hands, smiling broadly.

Here dependent arising changes its role. Before, it was the tool that brought each hinge up and took it apart. Now I deal in causes and conditions all day: what helps, what harms, what follows from what. Dependent arising is how help works.

Who decides, if no one is there? Nobody disappears. I still act, choose, keep my promises and answer for what I do, as a conventional person. What is gone is the doer behind the doing, pulling the levers. No one is there, so nothing matters, is not freedom. It is the annihilation view.

And why help, if others are not found? Others suffer, validly, in dependence. Their suffering is eased when its conditions change. And looked for, no one is found suffering from their own side, so nothing in it is fixed for good.

So, looked back on, the taste has been met three times. Anatta, no seer in seeing. Freedom from all elaborations, when every hinge is exhausted: the same taste, stable and wide. And the marketplace.

These are not stages of the taste. The logic does not produce the taste. It unbinds the habit. And it is not a ladder climbed once. It goes round more than once, wherever the habit of fixing returns.

One taste. How many faces does it have? That is Part 7.
