# Bonus. Insubstantial Appearances

We carry two mistakes about appearances, and they mirror each other.

The first is familiar. We treat what we see and touch as if each thing had a fixed nature of its own, simply there, waiting to be found. This is the habit of reification, and it rarely shows up as a view. It shows up as the plain sense that things are real.

The second is less often noticed. Having heard that things lack a fixed nature, we imagine they must be faint and ghostly, like shadows we could brush aside. Both mistakes share one assumption: that solidity and emptiness exclude each other. Four points loosen that assumption.

First, mutual dependence. Nothing is long or short in itself. A stick is long beside a pebble, and short beside a tree. A person is a parent only because there is a child. "My car" is its wheels, its engine, the road, the driver's purpose, and the name we give it. Gather those relations, and no car is found lying underneath.

Modern physics reaches a similar place. In Carlo Rovelli's relational reading of quantum theory, and in Karen Barad's account, relation comes before the things related. Yet both still treat the relating as a structure to be finally described: a ground. The Middle Way asks whether that ground can be found at all. Relations are as empty as the things they relate. So mutual dependence is not a hidden structure tying everything together. It is simply what is found when we look for an essence and find only relations.

Second, empty appearances. A coin pressed into the palm feels hard and weighty. It has no essence of its own, and it is not flimsy for that. Emptiness is not harmlessness either. A car is empty in exactly this sense, and when it strikes someone, the injury is entirely real. Emptiness does not dim appearance. It is the way appearance is: vivid, usable, and empty, all at once.

Third, functioning without inherent existence. A bridge carries traffic, though no single part of it is the bridge. Steel, deck, supports and the name work together, and it holds. A truck crosses it. A truck too heavy brings it down. Both outcomes are real, and neither needs the bridge to exist in its own right. Nor are the conditions a hidden foundation. They are patterns too, named in the same way. There is no bottom layer, only the web and its vivid patterns.

Fourth, karma and habit. What appears to each mind is shaped by the tendencies it carries. People see water as something to drink. Fish see it as a home. In the old image, hungry ghosts see it as pus and blood. Each version acts on the one who meets it. A rope in dim light is seen as a snake, and the fear is real, though no snake was ever there. Habit is not a hidden cause behind appearances. It is how they come to look solid and separate.

So two errors loosen together. Nothing stands behind appearances holding them in place. And without such a ground, they are not unreal.

They are vivid, workable and empty, with real consequences, and they can be met without the grasping that made them seem like things.
