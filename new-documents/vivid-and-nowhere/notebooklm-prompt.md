## Steering prompt

> Cover all 12 sections of the source in order, with every argument and example. Treat every "precision note" as
> binding. Key points: (1) the habit of innate grasping retreats to real moments and atoms; section 3 searches for
> them. Vasubandhu argued against the partless atom, he did not propose it, and add no mathematics of your own.
> (2) Solid, dreamlike and scientific appearances are equally unfindable; neither matter nor mind is the floor.
> (3) A phenomenon is vivid and not found anywhere: not in the mind, and not even "nowhere". The negation installs
> nothing. (4) The conditions are as empty as what they condition; dependence is not a mechanism, an infinite chain
> or a new foundation. (5) Appearing already is dependent arising. (6) Illusions work: never say anything works or
> appears "because it is empty". (7) Clarity is the appearing's own, with no knower behind it. (8) Give all six
> Dzogchen terms and the three cautions; rigpa is the knowing of the basis, not the basis. (9) Recognition is
> neither an achievement nor passivity. End on wonder and the ordinary world, not on what remains. Pronounce names
> as in the pronunciation list. Main visuals: the cup, the magic show and the dream, the moment with three parts,
> the atom and its six neighbours, the ball, the thought and the field, the chariot, the bell, long and short, the
> magician's elephant, the mirage, a drawing on water.

If the box has a character limit, use this shorter version:

> Cover all 12 sections in order and follow every precision note. Vasubandhu argued against the partless atom.
> Matter and mind are equally unfindable. Not even "nowhere"; the negation installs nothing. Conditions are as empty
> as what they condition; dependence is no mechanism. Never "because it is empty". Clarity has no knower behind it.
> All six Dzogchen terms and the three cautions; rigpa is not the basis. End on wonder, not on what remains.
