# Bonus — Beyond Cause and Effect

Beyond cause and effect. In Dzogchen, few phrases are so easily misheard. It sounds like an exception. A realm where causes stop working. Or a ground that makes everything, while nothing makes it.

A careful reader of Madhyamaka will ask a fair question. Is this a hidden substance, slipped back in through a side door?

The answer offered here is no. The phrase is not an exception to dependent arising. It is what dependent arising looks like once the habit of fixing things has been seen through.

Most thinking about causes rests on three quiet assumptions. That a thing needs an essence of its own to appear. That a cause needs a power of its own, carried into its effect. And that the meeting of conditions needs a deeper foundation underneath.

Nāgārjuna looks for each one. The chariot is not found in its wheels, its axle or its pole. The power of a cause, when sought, turns out to be named only in relation to the effect it is said to produce. And the foundation, when sought, becomes one more thing with parts, and the parts are named too.

None of this says that chariots are unreal. It says that the essence the search assumed is not there.

Notice what analysis is for. It is not a mind that must keep dissecting forever. It answers one habit, the habit of fixing. When that habit has been seen through, there is nothing left to search for. The dissection ends itself.

But one assumption survives. It no longer asks what produces things. It asks why they appear at all. As if the coming together of conditions still owed a final reason.

This is the last spell. It is looking for permission to stop looking.

The question why has force only while something seems to stand behind appearing. When nothing is found there, the question is not answered. It stops applying. In this book's reading, that is the sense of the old words, no why and because.

Two ways of knowing are at work. One divides. A cup becomes a handle, a rim and a body. The other does not divide. It meets what appears whole, before the parts and names are added.

These are not two stages. The dividing mind is the habit that analysis answers. The undivided seeing is what remains when the habit stops.

Then the eight extremes fall away together. Arising and ceasing. Lasting and ending. Coming and going. Same and different. All of them needed a thing with an edge of its own. Without it, nothing is lost. Nothing ever had the whereabouts those questions required.

Dzogchen calls this appearing spontaneous presence. It is not a new ingredient laid over emptiness. Dependent arising describes it from the outside. Spontaneous presence describes the same appearing from within. Emptiness itself is empty, so it cannot become a ground.

The old teaching to Bāhiya names the taste. In the seen, only the seen. In the heard, only the heard. No seer stands behind the seeing. Colours are vivid. Sounds are immediate. Thoughts lose their heavy ownership.

This is not an attainment to keep. The moment it is held, the fixing has returned.

Seen is just seen. Heard is just heard. And nothing needs to be put in its place.
