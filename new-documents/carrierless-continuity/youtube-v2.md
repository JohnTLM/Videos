## Description

Your cells turn over, your memories rewrite themselves, your moods never hold still. Yet life feels continuous. For millennia, philosophy and theology have explained this with an unchanging bearer: a soul or an ego that carries you from birth to death.

This video shows why the bearer cannot do that job, and why the momentary stream offered in its place fails too. Drawing on Nāgārjuna's Middle Way, the lamp and the fire, Dōgen's firewood and ash, and Candrakīrti's and Tsongkhapa's "mere I", it builds an account of continuity with no carrier at all: nothing carried, and nothing cut off. It then asks the question beneath it all: what is a phenomenon? The answer is vivid, unfindable appearing, read through Madhyamaka and a non-foundational Dzogchen.
