# The Illusion of the Given

Picture a single bowl of liquid. A human sees clear water. A hungry ghost, driven by craving, sees pus and blood. A fish finds the air it breathes. A god tastes nectar. An awakened being sees the pure form of water, the Buddha Māmakī.

Common sense says something must sit in that bowl first. Some neutral stuff, and each creature looks at it through its own lens.

But look at that stuff. If it has qualities, like quenching thirst, it cannot explain the ghost's burning pus. If it has no qualities at all, a featureless blank can cause no experience whatsoever. The neutral substance is an imagined nature in a modest disguise.

Then why do humans agree so reliably that it is water? Candrakīrti turns the question around. Agreement does not prove a hidden object. Shared, reliable agreement is simply what everyday truth is.

And within each kind of being, mistakes are still caught. A person with jaundice sees a white shell as yellow, and other people correct them. But the hungry ghost is not a mistaken human. By the standards of its own kind, its senses work normally. For it, the liquid really does function as pus.

Does that make the world a dream inside the mind? The Mind-Only school said so. Shared karma, it argued, explains why beings agree. Candrakīrti answered that the seeing and the seen stand or fall together. If the pus cannot be found on its own, neither can the mind that sees it. Neither the world nor the mind is the floor.

Language carries human agreement further. A child learns the word water because it works. Say it, and a glass arrives. Yet the noun tempts us to believe in one solid bowl behind every view of it.

The Great Perfection, Dzogchen, describes the same thing from experience. Appearances are display, with nothing standing behind them. Knowing awareness is not a projector throwing out a world. The clarity belongs to the appearing itself, and like the appearing, it is empty.

Contact comes in channels. A tick senses sweat, warmth and hair. A pool of spring water has none of these, so it never appears in the tick's world. Yet the tick can still drown in it. Dark matter passes through the Earth as if it were not there, yet gravity still bends its path. Nothing here is either a solid wall or simply nothing.

So why is the world orderly at all? Barley grows from barley seed, not rice. Fire burns and does not freeze. Things never needed a fixed essence in order to work. Emptiness does not make them work either. What falls away is only the demand for a floor.

This matters beyond philosophy. The ghost's suffering is real for the ghost. No point of view owns the real world, so no being's world can be dismissed. Compassion needs no self behind it.

Nāgārjuna said it in one line. For whom emptiness fits, everything fits.

The water is still cool in a human hand. Everyday life does not carry on in spite of having no foundation. It never needed one.
